BS&W and Water Cut

BS&W (Basic Sediment and Water) and WC (Water Cut) both express the water share of the total liquid production. The calculator works in two modes: from water rate and total liquid rate, or from water rate and oil rate.

BS&W (%) = (Qwater / Qliquid) × 100 = Qwater / (Qwater + Qoil) × 100
WC (fraction) = Qwater / Qliquid
Qliquid = Qwater + Qoil

Inputs and units

  • Water rate, and either total liquid rate or oil rate
  • Units: STB/d (or bbl/d) or m³/d
  • Results: water cut in %, water cut as a fraction, and the derived oil rate or oil share

Input checks in the calculator

  • Water rate must be zero or greater.
  • Total liquid rate must be greater than zero.
  • Water and liquid (or oil) rates must use matching units.
  • Water rate cannot exceed total liquid rate.

Worked example

Water rate = 150 STB/d and total liquid rate = 1,000 STB/d.

BS&W = (150 / 1,000) × 100 = 15.00%
WC = 150 / 1,000 = 0.1500
Qoil = 1,000 − 150 = 850.00 STB/d
Keep both rates in the same unit, either both in STB/d or both in m³/d.

Water Rate from Gross Rate and Water Cut

When you know the gross liquid rate and the water cut percentage, the calculator gives the water rate, the net oil rate and the oil cut.

QW = Gross × (WC / 100)
Qoil = Gross − QW
Oil Cut (%) = 100 − WC

Inputs and units

  • Gross liquid rate in STB/d (or bbl/d) or m³/d
  • Water cut WC in percent, from 0% to 100%

Input checks in the calculator

  • Gross liquid rate must be zero or greater.
  • WC must be between 0% and 100%.

Worked example

Gross rate = 1,000 STB/d and WC = 25%.

QW = 1,000 × (25 / 100) = 250.00 STB/d
Qoil = 1,000 − 250 = 750.00 STB/d
Oil Cut = 100 − 25 = 75.00%
Divide the percentage water cut by 100 before multiplying by the gross liquid rate.

Net Oil Rate from Gross and Water Rate

This calculator subtracts the water rate from the gross liquid rate to give the net oil rate. It also shows the water cut and the oil cut.

Net Oil Rate = Gross − Water Rate
WC (%) = (Water Rate / Gross) × 100
Oil Cut (%) = 100 − WC (%)

Inputs and units

  • Gross liquid rate in STB/d (or bbl/d) or m³/d
  • Water rate in the same unit

Input checks in the calculator

  • Gross rate and water rate must be zero or greater.
  • Water rate cannot exceed the gross liquid rate.

Worked example

Gross liquid rate = 1,500 STB/d and water rate = 300 STB/d.

Net Oil Rate = 1,500 − 300 = 1,200.00 STB/d
WC = (300 / 1,500) × 100 = 20.00%
Oil Cut = 100 − 20 = 80.00%
Keep the gross liquid rate and the water rate in the same unit.

Pen Reading to LWC Converter

This calculator converts a chart pen reading into the Liquid Weight Chart (LWC) value, using the chart range and the DPU calibration range.

LWC = (Pen Reading / Chart Range) × DPU Range

Inputs and units

  • Pen Reading
  • Chart Range
  • DPU Range

Input checks in the calculator

  • Chart Range and DPU Range must be greater than zero.

Worked example

Pen reading = 40, chart range = 100 and DPU range = 200.

Pen / Chart Range = 40 / 100 = 0.400000
LWC = 0.4 × 200 = 80.000
The calculator also shows the step Pen / Chart Range, so you can check the arithmetic.

Refractometer Analysis

The refractometer reading gives the salt content of a water sample. The calculator converts the percent reading into sodium chloride and into chloride ions.

[NaCl] mg/L = Reading (%) × 10,000
Cl− ppm = [NaCl] mg/L / 1.84

Inputs and units

  • Refractometer reading in %
  • Results: mg NaCl and ppm chlorides

Worked example

Refractometer reading = 3.5%.

[NaCl] = 3.5 × 10,000 = 35,000 mg/L
Cl− = 35,000 / 1.84 = 19,021.7 ppm
Make sure the refractometer has been zeroed before use.

Chloride Determination (Argentometric)

This calculator finds the chloride concentration from a silver nitrate titration, then estimates sodium chloride and total dissolved solids.

mg/L Cl− = ((A − B) × N × 35,450) / D
NaCl = Cl− × 1.65
TDS ≈ NaCl × 1.15

Inputs and units

  • A: silver nitrate used, in ml
  • B: titration for the blank, in ml (use 0 for distilled water)
  • N: AgNO3 strength in normality (usually 0.1 N or 0.0282 N)
  • D: sample volume in ml (a standard field determination uses 1 ml)

Titrate the sample with silver nitrate until the colour changes to reddish-brown, and record the ml used. The number 35,450 is the milliequivalent weight of chlorine multiplied by 1,000, to give mg/L. The page also shows the formation water type, the pressure gradient and the equivalent mud weight for the result.

Worked example

A = 10 ml, B = 0.2 ml, N = 0.0282 and D = 1 ml.

Cl− = (10 − 0.2) × 0.0282 × 35,450 / 1 = 9,796.96 mg/L
NaCl = 9,796.96 × 1.65 = 16,164.99 mg/L
TDS ≈ 16,164.99 × 1.15 = 18,589.74 mg/L
Check the normality on the bottle label before you enter N.
