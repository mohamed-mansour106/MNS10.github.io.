1. Measurement Systems & Comparisons

A measurement must have a standard[span_0](start_span)[span_0](end_span). A standard is an exact quantity people agree to use for comparison, ensuring that two people using the same object get close to the same results[span_1](start_span)[span_1](end_span).

American vs. SI (Metric) Systems

The United States is currently the only western country not using the metric system as its primary system of measurement (alongside Yemen, Brunei, and a few small islands)[span_2](start_span)[span_2](end_span).

English System Memorization Rule: In the English system, remember: 12 inches in a foot, 3 feet in a yard, 5,280 feet in a mile, 16 ounces in a pound, and 4 quarts to a gallon[span_3](start_span)[span_3](end_span).

  • Deca: Ten (10)[span_6](start_span)[span_6](end_span)

  • Centi: One-hundredth (1/100)[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span)
  • Milli: One-thousandth (1/1000)[span_9](start_span)[span_9](end_span)

2. Estimation, Accuracy & Precision

Estimation is using your knowledge of something similar in size or amount to determine the size of a new object[span_15](start_span)[span_15](end_span). It helps make rough measurements and is crucial in business, economics, and project planning due to multiple variables[span_16](start_span)[span_16](end_span).

Precision vs. Accuracy:
  • Precision: A description of how close measurements are to each other[span_17](start_span)[span_17](end_span).
  • Accuracy: Comparing your measurement to the actual or accepted target value[span_18](start_span)[span_18](end_span).

3. Volume Calculations (Solids & Liquids)

Volume is the amount of space inside a solid[span_19](start_span)[span_19](end_span) or the fluid capacity a container can hold.

Liquid Volume Conversions

  • 1 Gallon = 4 Quarts[span_20](start_span)[span_20](end_span)
  • 1 Quart = 2 Pints[span_21](start_span)[span_21](end_span)
  • 1 Pint = 16 Fluid Ounces (or 2 Cups)[span_22](start_span)[span_22](end_span)
  • 1 Barrel (bbl) = 5.6146 Cubic Feet[span_23](start_span)[span_23](end_span)[span_24](start_span)[span_24](end_span)

Geometric Formulas

1. Cylinder Volume

$V = \pi r^2 h$
$\pi = 3.14$, $r = \text{radius}$, $h = \text{height}$[span_25](start_span)[span_25](end_span)

2. Sphere Volume

$V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$[span_26](start_span)[span_26](end_span)

3. Cone Volume

$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$[span_27](start_span)[span_27](end_span)

4. Cuboid Volume

$V = \text{Length} \times \text{Width} \times \text{Height} = a^3$[span_28](start_span)[span_28](end_span)

Oilfield Tubular Volume Formulas

Standard oilfield formulas for calculating capacities in wellbores:

  • Casing / Tubing Capacity:
    $V_{\text{tubing}} = \frac{ID^2}{1029.4} \times \text{Depth (ft)}$[span_29](start_span)[span_29](end_span) (in bbls)
  • Annular Volume:
    $V_{\text{annulus}} = \frac{ID_{\text{casing}}^2 - OD_{\text{tubing}}^2}{1029.4} \times \text{Depth (ft)}$[span_30](start_span)[span_30](end_span) (in bbls)

4. Temperature, Density, API & pH

Temperature Conversions

°C = (°F - 32) / 1.8[span_31](start_span)[span_31](end_span)
°F = (°C × 1.8) + 32[span_32](start_span)[span_32](end_span)
Kelvin (K) = °C + 273[span_33](start_span)[span_33](end_span)
Rankine (°R) = °F + 460[span_34](start_span)[span_34](end_span)
Critical Points: Pure water freezes at 32°F (0°C) and boils at 212°F (100°C)[span_35](start_span)[span_35](end_span).

API Gravity & Specific Gravity

The American Petroleum Institute (API) scale classifies crude oil weight at a baseline temperature of 60°F[span_36](start_span)[span_36](end_span).

$\text{API} = \frac{141.5}{\text{S.G.}} - 131.5$[span_37](start_span)[span_37](end_span)

$\text{S.G.} = \frac{141.5}{\text{API} + 131.5}$[span_38](start_span)[span_38](end_span)

pH Scale

Measures acidity or basicity of aqueous solutions. Values below 7 are acidic, above 7 are basic/alkaline, and 7 is neutral (pure water)[span_39](start_span)[span_39](end_span).

5. Pressure & Hydrostatic Head

Pressure is the force exerted per unit area: $\text{Pressure} = \frac{\text{Force}}{\text{Area}}$[span_40](start_span)[span_40](end_span).

Hydrostatic Pressure (HP) Formula

$\text{HP (psi)} = 0.052 \times \text{Density (ppg)} \times \text{TVD (ft)}$[span_41](start_span)[span_41](end_span)

Where constant 0.052 is derived from unit conversions ($12\text{ in/ft} \div 231\text{ in}^3/\text{gal}$)[span_42](start_span)[span_42](end_span).

Pressure Gradient (PG)

$\text{Gradient (psi/ft)} = \text{Mud Weight (ppg)} \times 0.052$[span_43](start_span)[span_43](end_span)

Bottom Hole Pressure (BHP)

  • Static Condition: $\text{BHP} = \text{HP} + \text{Surface Pressure (SP)}$[span_44](start_span)[span_44](end_span)
  • Dynamic Condition: $\text{BHP} = \text{HP} + \text{SP} + \text{Frictional Pressure (FrP)}$[span_45](start_span)[span_45](end_span)

6. Comprehensive Case Study Exercise

Problem Statement: Calculate hydrostatic pressures and fluid volumes based on the following well profile data:

  • Tubing ID: 3.5 inches[span_46](start_span)[span_46](end_span)
  • Pipe OD: 4 inches[span_47](start_span)[span_47](end_span)
  • Casing ID: 9 inches[span_48](start_span)[span_48](end_span)
  • Brine Gradient: 0.550 psi/ft[span_49](start_span)[span_49](end_span) (Density = 10.2 ppg)
  • Annulus Fluid: 9.2 ppg brine down to 8,200 ft TVD[span_50](start_span)[span_50](end_span)
  • Tubing Fluid: 7.50 ppg oil from surface to 10,500 ft[span_51](start_span)[span_51](end_span)

Step-by-Step Solutions:

  1. Annulus HP (at 8,200 ft TVD):
    $\text{HP} = 8200 \times 9.20 \times 0.052 = \mathbf{3922.88\text{ psi}}$[span_52](start_span)[span_52](end_span)
  2. Tubing Oil Column HP (at 10,500 ft):
    $\text{HP} = 10500 \times 7.50 \times 0.052 = \mathbf{4095\text{ psi}}$[span_53](start_span)[span_53](end_span)
  3. Tubing Capacity Volume (10,500 ft of 3.5" tubing):
    $V = \frac{3.5^2}{1029.4} \times 10500 = \mathbf{123.4\text{ bbls}}$ (or $559.2\text{ bsc}$ / $693.19\text{ ft}^3$)[span_54](start_span)[span_54](end_span)[span_55](start_span)[span_55](end_span)
  4. Annular Volume (8,200 ft between 9" casing & 3.5" tubing):
    $V = \frac{9^2 - 3.5^2}{1029.4} \times 8200 = \mathbf{599.86\text{ bbls}}$ (or $637.39\text{ bbls}$ depending on casing selection)[span_56](start_span)[span_56](end_span)[span_57](start_span)[span_57](end_span)
