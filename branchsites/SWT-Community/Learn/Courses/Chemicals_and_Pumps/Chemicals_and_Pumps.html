Chemicals and Pumps

Video lesson: Chemicals and Pumps

Well Test Chemicals Dosage

This calculator gives the chemical injection rate in cc/min from the oil flowrate and the dosage in ppm. It has three products: antifoam, demulsifier and scale inhibitor.

Rate (cc/min) = Qbpd × Dosageppm × 42 × 3785.41 / (1,000,000 × 1440)
Simplified: Rate ≈ Qbpd × Dosageppm × 0.0001104

Inputs and units

  • Oil flowrate in bpd
  • Required dosage in ppm for each product
  • Result: injection rate in cc/min
ChemicalPurposeInjection point
AntifoamReduces foam in the separator and prevents carry-overUpstream manifold
DemulsifierBreaks the oil-water emulsion for clean separationUpstream manifold / heater
Scale inhibitorPrevents mineral scale in flowlines and surface equipmentUpstream manifold

Worked example

Oil flowrate 1,000 bpd. Dosages 5 ppm antifoam, 20 ppm demulsifier and 10 ppm scale inhibitor (example dosages chosen for this course).

Antifoam = 0.6 cc/min
Demulsifier = 2.2 cc/min (simplified formula gives 2.2)
Scale inhibitor = 1.1 cc/min
The steps are: ppm to a fraction, times barrels per day, times 42 gallons per barrel, divided by 1440 minutes, times 3785.41 cc per gallon.

Mud Pump Performance

This calculator gives the output of a mud pump per stroke and the flow rate at a given stroke speed.

Pump output (bbl/stk) = 0.000243 × D² × L × Eff
Gallons per stroke = bbl/stk × 42
Strokes per bbl = 1 / (bbl/stk)
BPM = bbl/stk × SPM, and GPM = gal/stk × SPM

Inputs and units

  • Liner diameter D in inches and stroke length L in inches
  • Efficiency in % (the page divides it by 100)
  • Strokes per minute (SPM)

Worked example

6 in liner, 12 in stroke, 95% efficiency and 100 SPM (example inputs chosen for this course).

Output = 0.000243 × 36 × 12 × 0.95 = 0.09973 bbl/stk
Gallons per stroke = 4.1885 and strokes per bbl = 10.03
BPM = 9.9727 and flow = 418.85 GPM
Efficiency is entered as a percentage. The results only show when the liner diameter and stroke length are above zero.

Use the Mud Pump Performance to check your own numbers.

Bottoms Up Time Estimation

Bottoms up (lag) time is how long it takes fluid to travel through the tubing volume at the current flow rate.

qbpm = qbpd / 1440
Tmin = Vtubing / qbpm

Inputs and units

  • Flowrate in bpd (the page also shows it in bpm)
  • Tubing volume in bbls
  • Results: bottoms up in minutes and as hh:mm

Worked example

4,320 bpd and a tubing volume of 180 bbls (example inputs chosen for this course).

qbpm = 4,320 / 1440 = 3.00
T = 180 / 3.00 = 60 min
That is 01 : 00 (hh : mm)
Typing the flowrate in bpd also fills the bpm box. Use the same units for the volume and the flowrate.

Use the Bottoms Up Time Estimation to check your own numbers.
