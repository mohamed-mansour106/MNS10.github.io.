How to Operate Diverter Manifold

1- Diverter Manifold

  • What is a Diverter Manifold?

    A Diverter Manifold is a flow-control assembly designed to direct fluid from a single inlet to one of two different outlet lines.

    It allows operators to safely divert flow between process equipment, storage tanks, flare systems, or other operational destinations without disconnecting pipework.

    Basic Configuration

    A standard Diverter Manifold consists of:

    • One Inlet Connection.
    • Two Outlet Connections.
    • Minimum of Two Ball Valves.

    The manifold is typically configured in a "T" shape, allowing flow from one inlet to be diverted to either outlet line.

    Valve Arrangement

    The ball valves are used to control and direct the flow path.

    By opening one outlet valve and closing the other, the operator can safely route fluid to the required destination while maintaining system integrity.

    Connection Types

    Diverter Manifolds may be supplied with various connection types depending on operational requirements:

    • Threaded inlet connections.
    • Hammer Union Figure 602 outlet connections.
    • Greylock hub connections.
    • Raised Face Flange connections.
    Hammer Union Connections

    Outlet connections are commonly equipped with Figure 602 Hammer Unions.

    Typical sizes include:

    • 3-inch Figure 602.
    • 4-inch Figure 602.

    These connections are generally manufactured using Schedule 80 (SCH 80) or Schedule 160 (SCH 160) pipe materials.

    Greylock Connections

    Some Diverter Manifolds are fitted with Greylock connections on the inlet and outlet sides.

    Greylock systems provide:

    • Quick installation and removal.
    • High-integrity sealing.
    • Reliable performance in high-pressure applications.
    Flange Ratings

    Diverter Manifolds may also be supplied with raised-face flanges suitable for various pressure classes:

    • CL 600
    • CL 900
    • CL 2500

    The selected flange class depends on the system operating pressure and service requirements.

    Working Pressure

    Diverter Manifolds are commonly designed with a Cold Working Pressure (CWP) ranging from:

    • 2,000 psig
    • 3,000 psig

    The maximum allowable pressure depends on the manifold design, materials, connection type, and pressure rating.

    Typical Applications

    Diverter Manifolds are widely used in Surface Well Testing (SWT) and production operations to direct flow between different process systems.

    • Storage Tanks.
    • Surge Tanks.
    • Flare Systems.
    • Loading Trucks.
    • Calibration Tanks.
    • Production Facilities.

2- Utilization of Diverter Manifold

  • Flow Diversion to Flare Booms

    The 3", 4", and 6" Gas and Oil Diverter Manifolds are designed to safely divert process flow to one of two flare booms when dual burner systems are installed.

    The selection of the flare boom is normally based on the prevailing wind direction to ensure safe flaring operations and minimize heat, smoke, and gas exposure to personnel and equipment.

    Production Test Separator Applications

    Diverter Manifolds can be installed on the outlet lines of a Production Test Separator to direct fluids to different destinations as required.

    • Water Outlet Line.
    • Oil Outlet Line.
    • Gas Outlet Line.

    This provides operational flexibility and allows safe routing of process fluids during well testing operations.

    Meter Calibration Operations

    Diverter Manifolds may be used to direct fluid flow to a Surge Tank or Calibration Tank during meter testing and calibration activities.

    This enables accurate verification, testing, and calibration of flow measurement equipment.

    Storage Tank Transfer Operations

    Diverter Manifolds can be installed between oil storage tanks and water storage tanks to facilitate fluid transfer and operational flexibility.

    Typical applications include:

    • Tank-to-tank transfer.
    • Tank isolation.
    • Tank changeover operations.
    • Temporary routing of process fluids.
    High-Pressure Pump Suction Service

    A Diverter Manifold may be installed on the suction side of a High-Pressure (HP) Pump.

    This arrangement allows operators to select and divert flow from different fluid sources before entering the pump suction.

    Important Limitation

    A Diverter Manifold must never be installed on the discharge side of a High-Pressure Pump.

    High discharge pressure may damage the manifold or its connections, potentially causing equipment failure, process disruption, environmental impact, and serious safety hazards.

    Engineering and Design Assurance

    All equipment is engineered by qualified engineering teams and manufactured in accordance with approved industry standards.

    Engineering documentation typically includes:

    • P&ID (Piping and Instrumentation Diagram)
    • Equipment Layout Drawings
    • Cause and Effect Diagrams
    • HAZOP (Hazard and Operability Studies)

    Equipment fabrication is performed by approved ASME Section VIII fabricators to ensure quality, safety, and compliance with design requirements.

3- Operating Guidelines for Diverter Manifold

  • 1. Verify Ball Valve Operation Before Opening the Well

    Before allowing well flow into the system, both ball valves on the diverter manifold must be checked for smooth and unrestricted movement.

    Cycle each valve from the fully closed position to the fully open position and verify that the valve indicator correctly reflects the flow path.

    • Indicator in line with flow direction = OPEN
    • Indicator crossing flow direction = CLOSED

    This check ensures proper valve functionality and prevents operational problems during flow diversion.

    2. Maintain Interlocking Handles in Position

    The interlocking handle system supplied with the diverter manifold must remain installed at all times during operation.

    The interlock mechanism ensures that:

    • One ball valve remains open.
    • The opposite ball valve remains closed.
    • Both valves cannot be closed simultaneously.

    This design prevents back pressure from building up toward the separator and eliminates the risk of human error during operation.

    Important Operational Note

    Before directing flow to a flare boom, operators must verify that the propane or diesel pilot system is activated and that a stable flame is present at the selected burner.

    Once the pilot flame is confirmed, the appropriate diverter manifold valve can be selected to route the process flow to the desired flare boom safely.

    3. Emergency Well Shut-In Procedure

    In an emergency requiring immediate well shut-in, the well must be isolated using the designated well control equipment.

    Acceptable shut-in devices include:

    • Choke Manifold.
    • Sand Management System.
    • Emergency Shutdown Valves (ESD).
    • X-Mas Tree Valves.

    These devices are specifically designed to safely stop well flow under pressure.

    WARNING – Never Use the Diverter Manifold to Shut In a Well

    Operators must never attempt to stop well flow by closing the diverter manifold valves.

    The diverter manifold is designed for flow diversion only and is not intended to function as a primary well control barrier.

    Closing both valves against high-pressure well flow may result in:

    • Equipment damage.
    • Excessive pressure build-up.
    • Process failure.
    • Serious safety incidents.
    WARNING – Proper Valve Operation

    Never use a Pipe Wrench or Cheater Bar (Chita Bar) to operate diverter manifold ball valve hand wheels.

    Excessive force may damage the valve stem, gearbox, hand wheel, or sealing components.

    All hand wheels must be operated manually by hand only, in accordance with the manufacturer's operating requirements.

Diverter Manifold - Evaluation Questions & Answers

  • Q1: What is the main purpose of a Diverter Manifold?

    Answer: To divert fluid flow from one inlet to two different outlet directions safely and efficiently.

    Q2: What is the minimum number of ball valves installed in a Diverter Manifold?

    Answer: A minimum of two ball valves.

    Q3: What shape does a typical Diverter Manifold form?

    Answer: A "T" shape consisting of one inlet and two outlets.

    Q4: What hammer union type is commonly used on Diverter Manifolds?

    Answer: Figure 602 Hammer Union.

    Q5: What are the common pipe schedules used in Diverter Manifolds?

    Answer: Schedule 80 (SCH 80) and Schedule 160 (SCH 160).

    Q6: What alternative connection type can be used instead of Hammer Unions?

    Answer: Greylock connections.

    Q7: What flange pressure classes may be used on Diverter Manifolds?

    Answer: CL600, CL900, and CL2500.

    Q8: What is the typical cold working pressure (CWP) of a Diverter Manifold?

    Answer: Between 2,000 psig and 3,000 psig.

    Q9: Why are two flare booms sometimes installed?

    Answer: To allow flow diversion based on wind direction and improve safety during flaring operations.

    Q10: What determines the selection of the flare boom?

    Answer: The prevailing wind direction.

    Q11: Where can Diverter Manifolds be installed on a Test Separator?

    Answer: On the water outlet, oil outlet, and gas outlet lines.

    Q12: Why is a Diverter Manifold used during meter calibration?

    Answer: To divert flow to a surge tank or calibration tank for meter testing and verification.

    Q13: Can a Diverter Manifold be installed between storage tanks?

    Answer: Yes, it can be installed between oil storage tanks or water storage tanks for transfer operations.

    Q14: Can a Diverter Manifold be installed on the suction side of an HP Pump?

    Answer: Yes, it can be safely installed on the suction side of a High-Pressure Pump.

    Q15: Why should a Diverter Manifold never be installed on the discharge side of an HP Pump?

    Answer: Because high discharge pressure may damage the manifold, cause equipment failure, environmental impact, and safety hazards if the manifold is sheared off.

    Q16: What does "sheared off" mean?

    Answer: Broken, torn, or completely separated from its original position due to excessive force or pressure.

    Q17: What should be checked before opening the well?

    Answer: The free movement of both ball valves by cycling them from fully closed to fully open positions.

    Q18: How can you identify an open ball valve?

    Answer: The valve indicator is aligned with the direction of flow.

    Q19: How can you identify a closed ball valve?

    Answer: The valve indicator is perpendicular (crossing) the direction of flow.

    Q20: What is the purpose of the interlocking handle system?

    Answer: To ensure one valve remains open while the other remains closed, preventing back pressure and operator error.

    Q21: Why must the interlocking handles remain installed at all times?

    Answer: To prevent both valves from being closed simultaneously and causing back pressure to the separator.

    Q22: What must be confirmed before diverting flow to a flare boom?

    Answer: The propane or diesel pilot flame must be activated and burning properly.

    Q23: Can a Diverter Manifold be used to shut in a well during an emergency?

    Answer: No. A Diverter Manifold is only a flow diversion device and must never be used for well shut-in.

    Q24: What equipment should be used to shut in a well?

    Answer: Choke Manifold, Sand Management System, Emergency Shutdown Valves (ESD), or the X-Mas Tree.

    Q25: Why should a pipe wrench or cheater bar never be used on valve hand wheels?

    Answer: Because excessive force can damage the valve stem, hand wheel, seals, and other valve components.

    Q26: How should Diverter Manifold hand wheels be operated?

    Answer: By hand only, following the manufacturer's operating requirements.

Course Summary – Diverter Manifold

  • What is a Diverter Manifold?

    A Diverter Manifold is a flow-routing device used in Surface Well Testing and production operations to divert fluid flow from one inlet to two different outlet directions.

    It typically consists of a minimum of two ball valves, one inlet, and two outlets forming a T-shaped configuration.

    Design & Construction
    • Minimum of two Ball Valves.
    • T-Shape design with one inlet and two outlets.
    • Figure 602 Hammer Union connections.
    • SCH 80 and SCH 160 pipe schedules.
    • Optional Greylock connections.
    • Raised Flanges: CL600, CL900, and CL2500.
    • Cold Working Pressure (CWP): 2000–3000 psig.
    Main Applications
    • Diverting flow between two flare booms.
    • Production Test Separator oil outlet lines.
    • Production Test Separator water outlet lines.
    • Production Test Separator gas outlet lines.
    • Meter calibration systems and surge tanks.
    • Oil storage tank transfer operations.
    • Water storage tank transfer operations.
    • High Pressure Pump suction lines.
    Important Safety Rules
    • Always check free movement of both ball valves before opening the well.
    • Valve indicator aligned with flow = OPEN.
    • Valve indicator crossing flow = CLOSED.
    • Keep the interlocking handles installed at all times.
    • Ensure one valve remains open while the other remains closed.
    • Confirm propane/diesel pilot flame is active before directing flow to a flare boom.
    Critical Warnings
    • Never install a Diverter Manifold on the discharge side of an HP Pump.
    • Never use a Diverter Manifold as a well shut-in device.
    • Never close both valves against high-pressure well flow.
    • Never use a Pipe Wrench or Cheater Bar on valve hand wheels.
    • Operate hand wheels by hand only.
    Emergency Response

    In emergency situations, the well must be shut in using approved well-control equipment only:

    • Choke Manifold.
    • Sand Management System.
    • Emergency Shutdown Valves (ESD).
    • X-Mas Tree Valves.

    Never attempt to stop well flow by closing the Diverter Manifold.

    Key Learning Points
    1. Diverter Manifolds are used for flow diversion, not pressure control.
    2. One valve must always remain open during operation.
    3. Flare boom selection depends on wind direction.
    4. HP Pump suction installation is acceptable; discharge installation is prohibited.
    5. Interlocking handles are critical safety devices.
    6. Well shut-in must always be performed using dedicated well-control equipment.
