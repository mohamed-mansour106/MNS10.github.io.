How to Operate ESD valve

Emergency Surface Shutdown Valve (ESDV)

  • What is an ESD Valve?

    The Emergency Surface Shutdown Valve (ESDV) is a hydraulic actuated gate valve designed to provide an additional layer of safety protection on the well test spread. It works alongside the Master Valve (MV) and Wing Valve (WV) installed on the wellhead tree.

    How Does the ESDV Operate?

    The valve is controlled by a hydraulic control panel or by High/Low Pressure Pilots (Hi/Lo Pilots). It can automatically shut down when unsafe operating conditions occur or manually by the Well Test crew through the ESD Panel.

    Main Safety Function

    The ESDV provides emergency isolation of well flow whenever abnormal pressure conditions or emergency situations are detected, protecting personnel, equipment, and the environment.

    Typical Installation Location
    1. Normally installed upstream of the Choke Manifold.
    2. Connected to High/Low Pressure Pilots for automatic shutdown.
    3. May also be installed downstream of the Choke Manifold and upstream of the Heater to protect against pressure surges.
    Operating Methods
    1. Manual shutdown from the ESD Control Panel.
    2. Remote shutdown from remote ESD stations.
    3. Automatic shutdown by Hi/Lo Pressure Pilots.
    Hydraulic Pressure Precaution

    When opening the ESD Valve, consider the effect of hydraulic oil thermal expansion. Excessive hydraulic pressure may damage the actuator system.

    Do not exceed the normal operating hydraulic pressure range of 2000–3000 psig.

    Important Limitation

    The ESD Valve is intended purely as a safety device and should not be used for pressure testing of surface equipment.

    Emergency Shutdown Recommendation

    Following an emergency shutdown, it is recommended to equalize pressure on both the upstream and downstream sides of the ESD Valve before reopening whenever possible.

    This helps protect the gate and seat components from erosion and excessive wear.

    Key Learning Points
    • ESDV = Emergency Surface Shutdown Valve.
    • Hydraulically actuated gate valve.
    • Provides emergency isolation of well flow.
    • Can be operated manually, remotely, or automatically.
    • Usually installed before the Choke Manifold.
    • Not recommended for pressure testing operations.
    • Maximum hydraulic operating pressure should remain within 2000–3000 psig.
    • Pressure equalization is recommended before reopening after an emergency shutdown.

2- Sizes of Emergency Shutdown Valves (ESDV)

  • ESDV Design Standards

    Emergency Shutdown Valves (ESDVs) are hydraulic actuated gate valves manufactured in accordance with API Standards to ensure safe, reliable, and standardized operation in oil and gas applications.

    Connection Types

    Depending on the required Cold Working Pressure (CWP) and system design, ESD valves may be equipped with:

    1. Hammer Union Connections
    2. Greylock Connections

    These connection types provide secure, pressure-rated connections for high-pressure well testing operations.

    Available Valve Sizes & Pressure Ratings

    ESD valves are available in different sizes and pressure ratings to match various well conditions and operating requirements:

    Valve Size Pressure Rating
    3 inch 5,000 psig
    3 inch & 4 inch 10,000 psig
    3 inch & 4 inch 15,000 psig
    3 inch & 4 inch 20,000 psig
    Why Different Pressure Ratings?

    Different wells operate at different pressures. Higher pressure wells require ESD valves with higher pressure ratings to safely contain the well fluids and maintain operational integrity.

    Sour Service Certification

    All hydraulic operated ESD gate valves are certified for Sour Service applications.

    This means they are specially designed and manufactured to safely handle fluids containing:

    • H₂S (Hydrogen Sulfide)
    • CO₂ (Carbon Dioxide)

    The valve materials comply with sour service requirements to prevent sulfide stress cracking, corrosion, and material failure.

    Key Learning Points
    • ESDVs are API certified hydraulic gate valves.
    • Available with Hammer Union or Greylock connections.
    • Common sizes are 3" and 4".
    • Pressure ratings range from 5,000 psi to 20,000 psi.
    • Used for emergency isolation of well flow.
    • Certified for Sour Service containing H₂S and CO₂.

3- Opening the Emergency Shutdown Valve (ESDV)

  • Fail-Close Valve Design

    The ESDV is a spring-loaded hydraulic gate valve designed as a Fail-Close Safety Valve. Hydraulic pressure must be applied to the actuator in order to open the valve. If hydraulic pressure is lost, the valve automatically closes to protect personnel and equipment.

    Check Hydraulic Oil Level

    Before operating the valve, ensure the ESD Control Panel hydraulic oil reservoir is at least 80% full. Adequate hydraulic oil is required to generate pressure and move the valve actuator.

    Connect the Hydraulic Control Line

    Connect the ESD Panel output to the ESDV actuator using a ½" Ball Valve / Needle Valve Manifold and install a suitable pressure gauge for monitoring hydraulic pressure during operation.

    Quick Release System for Gas Wells

    For gas well operations, it is recommended to install a Hydraulic Quick Release System on the ½" manifold.

    • Provides rapid emergency shutdown.
    • Reduces valve closing time.
    • Typical shutdown response time: 5–10 seconds.
    • Should be connected to a proper dumping bucket.
    Verify Air Supply Pressure

    Ensure the control panel receives sufficient air or nitrogen supply pressure.

    Recommended supply pressure: 80 – 100 psig

    Perform a functional test before operating the valve.

    Adjust Hydraulic Pressure

    Set the ESDV hydraulic regulator output to approximately 2000 psig.

    Although the actuator may be rated up to 10,000 psig, the normal operating pressure range is:

    1500 – 2000 psig

    Open the ESD Valve
    1. Apply hydraulic pressure between 1500 – 2000 psig.
    2. Observe the actuator stem movement.
    3. Continue pressurizing until the stem reaches its fully open position.
    4. Confirm that the ESDV gate valve is completely open.
    Activate the Safety System

    After opening the valve, energize the control panel to ensure automatic shutdown protection is active.

    The ESDV should respond to:

    • Manual Emergency Shutdown
    • High Pressure Pilots
    • Low Pressure Pilots
    • High Level Pilots
    • Low Level Pilots
    Important Note A – Pressure Equalization

    Following an Emergency Shutdown, it is recommended to equalize pressure upstream and downstream of the ESDV before reopening whenever possible.

    This minimizes gate erosion and protects internal valve components from excessive wear.

    Important Note B – Choke Manifold Precaution

    If the ESDV has closed during operations due to an emergency shutdown, it is recommended to close the Choke Manifold before re-energizing the ESD system.

    This prevents pressure surges and reduces the risk of Hammer Effect (Water Hammer) damage to equipment and pipework.

    Key Learning Points
    • ESDV is a Fail-Close hydraulic safety valve.
    • Hydraulic oil reservoir should be at least 80% full.
    • Required air supply pressure is 80–100 psig.
    • Normal hydraulic operating pressure is 1500–2000 psig.
    • Quick Release Systems are recommended for gas wells.
    • Always monitor stem movement during opening.
    • Pressure equalization is recommended before reopening after ESD activation.
    • Close the Choke Manifold before restarting the system to avoid Hammer Effect.

4- Closing the Emergency Shutdown Valve (ESDV)

  • Fail-Close Operating Principle

    The ESDV is a spring-loaded Fail-Close gate valve. To close the valve, hydraulic pressure must be released from the actuator by allowing the hydraulic oil to return to the panel oil reservoir.

    Once hydraulic pressure is removed, the internal spring force automatically pushes the actuator back and closes the gate valve.

    Check Reservoir Capacity

    Before closing the ESDV, ensure that the control panel oil reservoir has enough free capacity to receive the returned hydraulic oil.

    Recommended reservoir level: Approximately 60% Full

    Monitor Hydraulic Pressure

    Observe the pressure gauge installed on the ½" Ball / Needle Valve Manifold.

    As the valve closes, hydraulic pressure should gradually decrease until it reaches:

    0 psig

    Zero pressure on both the manifold gauge and the ESD panel gauge confirms that the valve has fully closed.

    Quick Emergency Closure (Gas Wells)

    For gas well operations, a Hydraulic Quick Release System is highly recommended.

    • Installed on the ½" hydraulic manifold.
    • Connected to a proper dumping bucket.
    • Provides rapid hydraulic pressure release.
    • Typical shutdown response time: 5–10 seconds.
    Manual Valve Closure

    The ESDV may be closed manually by turning the control manifold ball valve indicator from:

    SERVICE → SHUT IN

    This action releases hydraulic pressure back to the reservoir and allows the spring mechanism to close the valve.

    Continue monitoring the pressure gauge until it reaches 0 psig.

    Emergency Shutdown Method

    In an emergency situation, the ESDV can be closed instantly by pressing the RED Emergency Shutdown Button located on the ESD Control Panel.

    This action initiates a complete system shutdown and rapidly dumps hydraulic pressure, causing all connected safety valves to move to their fail-safe position.

    Important Note A – Pressure Equalization

    After an Emergency Shutdown, it is recommended to equalize pressure on both sides of the ESD Valve whenever possible.

    • Reduces gate erosion.
    • Protects valve internals.
    • Improves valve service life.
    • Minimizes pressure shock during reopening.
    Important Note B – Choke Manifold Precaution

    Whenever the ESDV has shut in due to an emergency activation, it is recommended to close the Choke Manifold before attempting to re-energize the ESD system.

    This prevents:

    • Pressure Surges
    • Hammer Effect (Water Hammer)
    • Equipment Damage
    • Pipework Stress
    • Valve Impact Loads
    Key Learning Points
    • ESDV is a Spring-Loaded Fail-Close Safety Valve.
    • Closing is achieved by releasing hydraulic pressure back to the reservoir.
    • Reservoir should have sufficient free capacity (around 60% full).
    • Valve closure is confirmed when hydraulic pressure reaches 0 psig.
    • Quick Release Systems are recommended for gas wells.
    • Manual closure is performed by switching from SERVICE to SHUT IN.
    • Emergency closure is performed using the RED Emergency Shutdown Button.
    • Always equalize pressure before reopening when possible.
    • Close the Choke Manifold before restarting after an ESD event.

Emergency Shutdown Valve (ESDV) – Course Summary

  • What is an ESDV?

    The Emergency Shutdown Valve (ESDV) is a hydraulic-operated, spring-loaded Fail-Close Gate Valve used as a critical safety device during Well Testing operations.

    It provides an additional layer of protection to the Master Valve (MV), Wing Valve (WV), and other tree safety valves by automatically isolating well flow during emergency conditions.

    Main Functions
    • Provides emergency well isolation.
    • Protects personnel and equipment.
    • Responds to High/Low Pressure Pilots.
    • Responds to High/Low Level Pilots.
    • Can be activated manually or automatically.
    • Protects process equipment from abnormal operating conditions.
    Location of ESDV
    • Normally installed upstream of the Choke Manifold.
    • Connected to Hi/Lo Pressure Pilot systems.
    • Can also be installed upstream of the Heater to protect against pressure surges.
    Available Sizes & Ratings
    • 3" × 5,000 psig
    • 3" & 4" × 10,000 psig
    • 3" & 4" × 15,000 psig
    • 3" & 4" × 20,000 psig
    • API Certified Gate Valves
    • Hammer Union or Greylock Connections
    • Sour Service Certified (H₂S / CO₂)
    Opening the ESDV
    1. Verify hydraulic oil reservoir is approximately 80% full.
    2. Check air supply pressure (80–100 psig).
    3. Connect hydraulic control line and pressure gauge.
    4. Adjust hydraulic regulator to approximately 2000 psig.
    5. Apply hydraulic pressure between 1500–2000 psig.
    6. Observe stem movement until valve is fully open.
    7. Activate ESD panel and safety shutdown system.
    Closing the ESDV
    1. Release hydraulic pressure back to the oil reservoir.
    2. Ensure reservoir has enough free volume (approximately 60% full).
    3. Observe pressure decreasing to 0 psig.
    4. The internal spring automatically closes the valve.
    5. Valve can be closed manually by switching from SERVICE → SHUT IN.
    6. Emergency closure can be initiated using the RED ESD Button.
    Emergency Shutdown Sequence

    During an emergency, the ESD system rapidly dumps hydraulic pressure, causing the ESDV to automatically move to its fail-safe closed position.

    • Manual ESD Button Activation
    • Remote Shutdown Station Activation
    • High Pressure Pilot Trip
    • Low Pressure Pilot Trip
    • High Level Pilot Trip
    • Low Level Pilot Trip
    • Loss of Hydraulic Pressure
    Quick Release System

    Gas wells should utilize a Hydraulic Quick Release System connected to the ½" manifold.

    • Provides rapid pressure dumping.
    • Reduces shutdown time to approximately 5–10 seconds.
    • Improves emergency response performance.
    Important Operational Precautions
    • Never exceed recommended hydraulic pressure of 2000–3000 psig.
    • Consider hydraulic oil thermal expansion effects.
    • Equalize pressure upstream and downstream before reopening after shutdown.
    • Close the Choke Manifold before re-energizing the ESD system.
    • Avoid Hammer Effect (pressure surge) damage.
    • Do not use the ESDV for pressure testing operations.
    Key Learning Points
    • ESDV = Emergency Shutdown Safety Valve.
    • Hydraulic Operated + Spring Loaded = Fail Close.
    • Opens using hydraulic pressure.
    • Closes when hydraulic pressure is released.
    • Primary purpose is emergency well isolation.
    • Controlled by ESD Panel and Safety Pilots.
    • Common operating pressure: 1500–2000 psig.
    • Air supply required: 80–100 psig.
    • Quick Release Systems improve shutdown response.
    • Always prioritize safety and pressure equalization procedures.

ESDV Course – Questions & Answers

  • Q1: What does ESDV stand for?

    A: Emergency Shutdown Valve.

    Q2: What is the primary purpose of an ESDV?

    A: To safely isolate well flow during emergency conditions.

    Q3: What type of valve is an ESDV?

    A: A hydraulic-operated, spring-loaded gate valve.

    Q4: What is meant by "Fail Close"?

    A: The valve automatically closes when hydraulic pressure is lost.

    Q5: Where is the ESDV normally installed?

    A: Upstream of the Choke Manifold.

    Q6: What devices can automatically trigger an ESD shutdown?

    A: High/Low Pressure Pilots and High/Low Level Pilots.

    Q7: Can the ESDV be operated manually?

    A: Yes, from the control panel or by remote shutdown systems.

    Q8: What hydraulic pressure is normally required to open the ESDV?

    A: Approximately 1500–2000 psig.

    Q9: What should be the minimum hydraulic oil level before opening the valve?

    A: Around 80% full.

    Q10: What air supply pressure is required for the control panel?

    A: 80–100 psig.

    Q11: What indicates that the ESDV is fully open?

    A: Full stem movement and stable hydraulic pressure.

    Q12: What should be observed while opening the valve?

    A: The actuator stem movement and hydraulic pressure gauge.

    Q13: Why is a hydraulic quick release system recommended for gas wells?

    A: To achieve rapid emergency shutdown within 5–10 seconds.

    Q14: How is the ESDV manually closed?

    A: By switching the ball valve indicator from SERVICE to SHUT IN.

    Q15: What pressure should the hydraulic gauge show when the valve is fully closed?

    A: 0 psig.

    Q16: What happens to the hydraulic oil when the ESDV closes?

    A: It returns to the control panel oil reservoir.

    Q17: Why should upstream and downstream pressure be equalized after an emergency shutdown?

    A: To reduce gate erosion and protect valve internals.

    Q18: Why should the Choke Manifold be closed before re-energizing the ESD system?

    A: To prevent Hammer Effect and pressure surges.

    Q19: Is the ESDV recommended for pressure testing operations?

    A: No, it is intended only as a safety shutdown device.

    Q20: What service are hydraulic-operated ESDVs certified for?

    A: Sour Service (H₂S and CO₂ environments).
