3 Phase Electrical Calculations

This calculator gives the full load current and the start up current of a three phase motor, and converts kW to hp and hp to kW.

IFLC = PkW × 1000 / (√3 × V × PF)
Istartup ≈ IFLC × 7
1 kW = 1.34 hp and 1 hp = 0.7457 kW (the page rounds the answers)

Inputs and units

  • Supply voltage in volts (line to line)
  • Power rating in kW
  • Power factor from 0 to 1 (the page uses 0.875 when it is left empty)

Worked example

415 V, 75 kW and power factor 0.875 (example inputs chosen for this course). Conversions: 100 kW and 1,380 hp.

IFLC = 75,000 / (1.732 × 415 × 0.875) = 119.2 A
Start up current about 835 A (7 × FLC)
100 kW = 134 hp and 1,380 hp = 1,029 kW
The printed start up formula says 7 times the full load current, while the page code uses 6.998. The difference is tiny (835 A vs 834 A here).

Cable Sizing and Voltage Drop

This calculator picks the smallest cable area that carries the load, then gives the voltage drop and a health status.

Vdrop = K × L × I × R / 1000, with K = 1.732 for 3-phase and 2 for 1-phase
%Vdrop = Vdrop / Vsys × 100
R is the resistance per km in Ω/km, L is the length in meters and I is the load in amps

Inputs and units

  • Required load in amps and cable length in meters
  • System voltage: 220 V (1-phase) or 440 V (3-phase)
  • Number of conductors: 2, 3 or 4
StatusVoltage drop
Excellent3% or less
Acceptable3% to 5%
Criticalover 5%

Worked example

100 A, 150 m, 440 V (3-phase) and 3 conductors (example inputs chosen for this course). The page table gives 50 mm² for 100 A with 3 conductors (123 A rating, 0.387 Ω/km).

Vdrop = 1.732 × 150 × 100 × 0.387 / 1000 = 10.05 V
% loss = 10.05 / 440 × 100 = 2.3%
Status: Excellent (3% or less)
The page picks the first cable in its own table whose amp rating is at or above the load. Check the real cable rating before use.

Wire Cable Length for Tie Back

This calculator finds the length of wire cable needed between the king post and the boom tie point, using the law of cosines.

c = √(a² + b² − 2ab × cos C)

Inputs and units

  • King post height a in feet
  • Boom tie point b in feet
  • Angle C from the post in degrees
  • Result: wire cable length c in feet

Worked example

a = 40 ft, b = 30 ft and C = 60° (example inputs chosen for this course).

c² = 1,600 + 900 − 2 × 40 × 30 × cos 60° = 1,300
c = 36.06 ft
Both a and b must be above zero, or the page does not update the result. Add extra length for the end fittings yourself, the page does not.

Use the Wire Cable Length for Tie Back to check your own numbers.

Transducer Signal Scaling

This calculator converts the raw voltage of a transducer to a reading in engineering units, for each channel.

Reading = (Vraw − Vzero) × Range / (Vmax − Vzero)

Inputs and units

  • Raw voltage, zero value and max value of the signal (the page starts with 1 V to 5 V)
  • Transducer range for the channel
  • Result: calculated reading
CodeDescriptionTypical range
WHPWellhead pressure0 - 10,000 psi
WHTWellhead temperature0 - 400 °F
CSGCasing pressure0 - 5,000 psi
STATICStatic pressure (orifice)0 - 2,000 psi
DIFFDifferential pressure0 - 400 inH₂O

Worked example

A WHP channel with range 10,000 psi, raw 3 V, zero 1 V and max 5 V.

Reading = (3 − 1) × 10,000 / (5 − 1) = 5,000 psi
At 1 V the reading is 0 and at 5 V it is the full range
A raw voltage below the zero value gives a negative reading, and above the max value gives a reading above the range.
