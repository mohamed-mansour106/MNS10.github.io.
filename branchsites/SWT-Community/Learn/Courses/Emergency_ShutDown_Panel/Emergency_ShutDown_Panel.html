How to Operate Emergency ShutDown Panel

1- Emergency Shut Down (ESD) Control Panel

  • What is an ESD Control Panel?

    The Emergency Shut Down (ESD) Control Panel is a safety system designed to protect personnel, equipment, and the environment by automatically or manually shutting in the well during emergency situations.

    It controls several hydraulic safety valves installed on the Wellhead and Christmas Tree (X-Mas Tree).

    Main Components of the ESD Panel
    1. Hydraulic Oil Reservoir – Stores hydraulic oil used to operate safety valves.
    2. Pneumatic High-Pressure Pump – Converts air pressure into hydraulic pressure.
    3. Instrument Air Regulator – Controls and regulates instrument air pressure.
    4. Hydraulic Interface Valves – Direct hydraulic pressure to the required valves.
    5. Hydraulic Regulators – Maintain stable hydraulic operating pressure.
    Valves Controlled by the ESD Panel

    The ESD Panel supplies hydraulic pressure to keep safety valves open during normal operation.

    • SCSSSV – Surface Controlled Sub-Surface Safety Valve
    • MV – Master Valve
    • WV – Wing Valve
    • SSV – Surface Safety Valve
    • ESDV – Emergency Shut Down Valve
    How the ESD System Works

    During normal operation, hydraulic pressure is supplied to the safety valves, keeping them in the open position and allowing production flow.

    If an emergency occurs, the ESD system trips and removes hydraulic pressure from the valves, causing them to automatically close and shut in the well.

    Emergency Shutdown (Trip)

    The ESD Panel can be activated from remote pneumatic ESD stations installed around the location.

    When an ESD station is activated:

    1. The trip signal is sent to the ESD Panel.
    2. Hydraulic pressure is released.
    3. Safety valves automatically close.
    4. The well is safely shut in.
    Purpose of the ESD Panel
    • Protect personnel from hazardous situations.
    • Protect equipment from damage.
    • Prevent uncontrolled well flow.
    • Reduce environmental risks.
    • Provide rapid emergency shutdown capability.
    Key Learning Points
    • The ESD Panel is a critical well safety system.
    • It uses hydraulic pressure to keep safety valves open.
    • Loss of hydraulic pressure causes automatic valve closure.
    • It controls SCSSSV, MV, WV, SSV, and ESDV.
    • It can be tripped manually or remotely through ESD stations.
    • Its primary function is to safely shut in the well during emergencies.

1.2 Control Line Pressure (SCSSSV)

  • What is Control Line Pressure?

    The SCSSSV (Surface Controlled Sub-Surface Safety Valve) is a downhole safety valve that remains open only when hydraulic pressure is applied through a control line.

    This hydraulic pressure is generated by an air-driven diaphragm pump with a working pressure of 10,000 psi.

    Hydraulic Pressure Supply

    The diaphragm pump uses compressed air or nitrogen to generate hydraulic pressure and send it through the control line to keep the SCSSSV in the open position.

    As long as hydraulic pressure is maintained, the safety valve remains open and well production can continue.

    Manual Override / Isolation Valve

    The system includes a manual override and isolation valve.

    This valve allows operators to manually isolate or control the hydraulic system if the air or nitrogen supply is lost.

    It provides additional operational flexibility during maintenance or emergency situations.

    Automatic ESD Trip Function

    The ESD system continuously monitors the pilot line pressure.

    If the pilot pressure falls below 40 psi, the ESD panel automatically trips.

    1. Hydraulic pressure is dumped.
    2. Control line pressure drops to zero.
    3. SCSSSV closes automatically.
    4. Other hydraulic safety valves close.
    5. The well is safely shut in.
    Emergency Push Button

    The ESD Control Panel is equipped with an Emergency Push Button located on the front panel.

    When the button is pressed:

    1. Hydraulic pressure is immediately released.
    2. The control line pressure is dumped.
    3. The SCSSSV closes.
    4. All connected hydraulic safety valves close.
    5. The well is shut in safely.

    This provides a quick manual method for emergency well shutdown.

    Key Learning Points
    • SCSSSV is held open by hydraulic control line pressure.
    • Hydraulic pressure is supplied by an air-driven diaphragm pump.
    • The pump has a working pressure of 10,000 psi.
    • A manual override/isolation valve is provided.
    • Pilot line pressure must remain above 40 psi.
    • If pilot pressure drops below 40 psi, the ESD panel trips automatically.
    • Loss of hydraulic pressure causes the SCSSSV to close.
    • The Emergency Push Button can manually shut in the well at any time.
    Quick Review Question

    Q: What happens if the pilot line pressure falls below 40 psi?

    A: The ESD panel trips, hydraulic pressure is dumped, the SCSSSV closes, other safety valves close, and the well is automatically shut in.

1.3 Safety Features of the ESD Panel

  • Purpose of Safety Features

    The ESD Control Panel is equipped with several safety devices to protect the hydraulic system, safety valves, and associated equipment from excessive pressure.

    These safety features prevent damage to the hydraulic components and ensure safe operation of the well control system.

    Hydraulic Relief Valve

    A Relief Valve is installed on the hydraulic pump output line.

    Its purpose is to limit the hydraulic pressure to a predetermined maximum value, typically 6,000 psi.

    If the hydraulic pressure rises above the set limit, the relief valve automatically opens and releases excess pressure back to the system, protecting equipment from overpressure damage.

    Example: If the pump attempts to generate 6,500 psi, the relief valve opens and maintains the system pressure near 6,000 psi.

    Air Relief Valve

    An Air Relief Valve is installed in the airline before the air-driven diaphragm pump.

    This valve limits the amount of air pressure supplied to the pump and prevents the pump from generating excessive hydraulic pressure.

    The air relief valve ensures that the pump output pressure does not exceed approximately 7,000 psi.

    Why Are These Safety Devices Important?
    • Prevent hydraulic system overpressure.
    • Protect SCSSSV and other safety valves.
    • Protect hydraulic hoses, fittings, and tubing.
    • Reduce the risk of equipment failure.
    • Improve personnel and operational safety.
    How the Protection System Works

    The air relief valve acts as the first level of protection by limiting air pressure supplied to the pump.

    The hydraulic relief valve acts as the second level of protection by limiting hydraulic pressure within the control system.

    Together, these devices prevent dangerous pressure build-up inside the ESD control panel.

    Key Learning Points
    • The hydraulic relief valve is installed on the pump discharge line.
    • Its typical pressure setting is 6,000 psi.
    • The air relief valve is installed before the air-driven pump.
    • It prevents pump output pressure from exceeding 7,000 psi.
    • Both valves protect the hydraulic control system from overpressure.
    • These safety features improve equipment reliability and operational safety.
    Quick Review Question

    Q: What is the purpose of the hydraulic relief valve?

    A: To limit hydraulic pressure (typically to 6,000 psi) and protect the ESD hydraulic system from overpressure damage.

1.4 ESD System Check

  • Purpose of ESD System Check

    The Emergency Shut Down (ESD) System must be tested and verified before use and throughout the well test operation to ensure it is fully functional and capable of responding during an emergency.

    The ESD system is one of the most critical safety systems on the well site, protecting personnel, equipment, and the environment.

    When Should the ESD System Be Checked?
    1. Before starting any well testing operation.
    2. Before offshore operations.
    3. Before onshore operations.
    4. At regular intervals throughout the well test.
    5. After maintenance or repairs to the system.
    What Should Be Verified?
    • Hydraulic system pressure is within operating limits.
    • Air or nitrogen supply is available.
    • Control line pressure is maintained.
    • Pilot line pressure is above the required limit.
    • Emergency push buttons are operational.
    • Safety valves respond correctly during testing.
    • No hydraulic or pneumatic leaks are present.
    Importance of Regular Testing

    Regular ESD testing ensures that the safety system will perform correctly if an emergency occurs during the well test.

    A non-functional ESD system could prevent proper well shutdown and increase the risk of injury, equipment damage, or environmental incidents.

    Safety Objective

    The main objective of the ESD System Check is to confirm that the emergency shutdown system is fully operational and capable of safely shutting in the well whenever required.

    Key Learning Points
    • ESD checks must be completed before using the system.
    • Checks are required for both offshore and onshore operations.
    • The system must be tested regularly throughout the well test.
    • The purpose is to verify emergency shutdown functionality.
    • A properly functioning ESD system improves safety and well control.
    Quick Review Question

    Q: Why must the ESD system be checked before and during a well test?

    A: To ensure the emergency shutdown system remains fully operational and capable of safely shutting in the well during an emergency.

2- Closing a Hydraulic Actuator Safety Valve

  • Purpose

    Hydraulic actuator safety valves are designed to automatically close whenever hydraulic pressure is removed. This provides a fail-safe mechanism to protect personnel, equipment, and the well during emergency situations.

    Manual Closure Procedure

    To close a hydraulic actuator safety valve, the operator pushes the handle knob of the three-way pneumatic relay valve located on the ESD control panel.

    This action causes the air or nitrogen pressure acting on the diaphragm of the three-way hydraulic valve to be released.

    What Happens Internally?
    1. Air/Nitrogen pressure is exhausted from the relay valve diaphragm.
    2. The hydraulic valve changes position.
    3. Pump output pressure is blocked.
    4. Hydraulic pressure to the safety valves is released.
    5. Hydraulic oil returns to the reservoir.
    6. The safety valves automatically close.
    Automatic Shutdown Through ESD Stations

    Any pilot station or remote ESD station connected to the pilot line can trigger the same shutdown sequence.

    When activated, the ESD station sends a trip signal that causes the hydraulic pressure to be dumped and the safety valves to close.

    Fail-Safe Design

    The system is designed according to a fail-safe principle.

    Any of the following conditions will automatically close the safety valves:

    • Activation of an ESD station.
    • Activation of a pilot device.
    • Loss of hydraulic pressure.
    • Loss of air supply pressure.
    • Loss of nitrogen supply pressure.
    Why Is This Important?

    The safety valves are designed to remain open only when hydraulic pressure is present.

    If control pressure is lost for any reason, the valves automatically move to the closed position, shutting in the well and preventing uncontrolled flow.

    Sequence of Events

    Push ESD Handle / Activate ESD Station

    ↓

    Air/Nitrogen Pressure Released

    ↓

    Hydraulic Pressure Dumped

    ↓

    Hydraulic Oil Returns to Reservoir

    ↓

    Safety Valves Close

    ↓

    Well Shut In

    Key Learning Points
    • Safety valves are hydraulically held open.
    • The three-way pneumatic relay valve controls hydraulic pressure.
    • Pushing the relay valve handle initiates shutdown.
    • Hydraulic pressure is released back to the oil reservoir.
    • Remote ESD stations perform the same function.
    • Loss of air, nitrogen, or hydraulic pressure automatically closes the safety valves.
    • The system is designed to be fail-safe.

3- Opening Hydraulic Actuator Safety Valves

  • Purpose

    Hydraulic actuator safety valves such as MV, WV, SSV, ESDV, and SCSSSV require hydraulic pressure to open and remain in service.

    The ESD Control Panel provides the hydraulic and pneumatic pressure necessary to operate these safety valves safely.

    Step 1 – Open Air Supply

    Open the air or nitrogen supply valve to the ESD manifold and verify that sufficient supply pressure is available on the pressure gauge.

    Step 2 – Isolate Hydraulic Flow

    Turn the hydraulic regulator fully anticlockwise to prevent hydraulic oil from passing through the regulator during system preparation.

    Step 3 – Build Air Pressure

    Turn the pump air regulator clockwise to gradually increase air pressure to the air-driven hydraulic pump.

    Monitor the pump supply pressure gauge while increasing pressure.

    Step 4 – Build Hydraulic Pressure

    Continue increasing the pump air supply pressure until the desired hydraulic output pressure is achieved.

    The hydraulic pressure can be monitored on the pump output pressure gauge.

    Step 5 – Open Hydraulic Regulator

    Turn the hydraulic regulator clockwise to allow hydraulic oil to flow to the selected safety valve.

    This may include:

    • Master Valve (MV)
    • Wing Valve (WV)
    • Surface Safety Valve (SSV)
    • Emergency Shutdown Valve (ESDV)
    Step 6 – Open the Safety Valves

    Continue increasing hydraulic pressure until approximately 3000 psi is reached.

    Observe the valve stem movement and ensure that it travels fully to the open position, indicating that the valve is completely open.

    Opening the SCSSSV

    In most installations, the Surface Controlled Sub-Surface Safety Valve (SCSSSV) is connected directly to the pump output pressure and does not pass through a hydraulic regulator.

    Therefore, opening the selected SCSSSV line allows the valve to receive full pump output pressure.

    In some installations, a hydraulic regulator may be fitted to protect the well liner and downhole equipment from excessive hydraulic pressure.

    Step 7 – Establish Pilot Pressure

    Turn the pilot line air regulator clockwise to build pilot system pressure.

    The pilot supply pressure should be adjusted between:

    50 – 80 psi

    This pressure can be monitored on the gauge marked "Pilot Supply Pressure".

    Step 8 – Reset the ESD System

    Pull the pneumatic relay valve handle marked:

    "Push to Close – Pull to Reset"

    Hold the handle in the OUT position until the pilot line pressure reaches at least 40 psi.

    This action supplies pneumatic pressure to the diaphragm of the hydraulic control valve, allowing hydraulic pressure to reach the safety valves and begin opening them.

    Step 9 – Verify Pilot Line Pressure

    Once the safety valves are fully open and all pilots are active, pilot pressure should build on the pilot line gauge.

    When the pilot line pressure exceeds 40 psi, the relay valve lock pin can be released.

    The relay handle should remain in the OUT position automatically.

    Important Note

    The safety system is NOT considered operational while the manual override is engaged.

    After pilot pressure is established, pull the relay valve handle to the OUT position and release the override lock pin.

    Only then is the ESD safety system fully active and in service.

    Key Operating Values
    • Safety Valve Opening Pressure ≈ 3000 psi
    • Pilot Supply Pressure = 50 – 80 psi
    • Minimum Pilot Holding Pressure = 40 psi
    • Pump Working Pressure = 10,000 psi
    Quick Review Question

    Q: What pilot line pressure must be achieved before releasing the relay valve lock pin?

    A: The pilot line pressure must be above 40 psi.

3.1 & 3.2 Hydraulic Actuator Valves (Close / Open Procedure)

  • Closing Hydraulic Actuator Valves

    Hydraulic actuator valves such as MV, WV, SSV, ESDV, and SCSSSV can be closed either by initiating a complete Emergency Shutdown (ESD) or by selectively isolating a specific valve without shutting down the entire safety system.

    Method 1 – Emergency Shutdown (ESD)
    1. Push the RED Emergency Stop Button on the ESD panel front.
    2. Twist the button to activate the shutdown function.
    3. The pilot pressure will be released.
    4. The hydraulic pressure will dump back to the oil reservoir.
    5. All hydraulic safety valves will automatically close.
    6. The well will be safely shut in.
    Method 2 – Close a Selected Valve Only

    If it is necessary to close a specific valve without tripping the entire ESD system, the manual service valve can be used.

    1. Identify the selected valve (MV, WV, SSV, ESDV, or SCSSSV).
    2. Locate the manual ball valve and verify the pointer is in the SERVICE position.
    3. Turn the manual ball valve to the opposite direction.
    4. Hydraulic oil pressure will bleed back to the panel oil reservoir.
    5. The actuator valve will begin closing.
    6. Observe the valve stem extending outward.
    7. Ensure full stem travel to confirm the valve is completely closed.
    Closing the SCSSSV

    The Surface Controlled Sub-Surface Safety Valve (SCSSSV) may be closed:

    • Manually using its service valve.
    • Automatically during an Emergency Shutdown (ESD).
    Opening Hydraulic Actuator Valves

    After an ESD trip or maintenance activity, the hydraulic actuator valves must be reopened and the ESD system reset.

    Step 1 – Release Emergency Shutdown
    1. Twist the RED Emergency Stop Button until it releases.
    2. Unlock the lock pin.
    3. Verify all remote ESD stations have been reset.
    Step 2 – Charge the Pilot System
    1. Press and hold the System Start / Reset Button.
    2. Allow pilot air pressure to build up in the system.
    3. The main air control valve will open.
    4. The hydraulic dump valve will close.

    The startup pressure is factory preset approximately 5 psi higher than the minimum pressure required to open the main air control valve.

    Step 3 – Build Hydraulic Pressure
    1. Turn the pump air regulator slowly clockwise.
    2. Increase air supply pressure gradually.
    3. Monitor the pump output pressure gauge.
    4. The pump will begin stroking automatically to maintain system pressure.
    Step 4 – Open Hydraulic Valves
    1. Increase hydraulic pressure until the desired value is achieved.
    2. Turn the hydraulic regulator clockwise.
    3. Allow hydraulic oil to flow to the selected valve.
    4. Continue increasing pressure until approximately 3000 psi.
    5. Observe the valve stem movement.
    6. Ensure full stem travel indicating the valve is fully open.
    Opening the SCSSSV

    In most installations, the SCSSSV is connected directly to the pump output pressure and does not require a hydraulic regulator.

    Opening the SCSSSV line allows the valve to receive full pump output pressure and open accordingly.

    Some systems may include a hydraulic regulator to protect the well liner and downhole equipment from excessive hydraulic pressure.

    Step 5 – Establish Pilot Pressure

    Turn the pilot line regulator clockwise and build pilot pressure.

    Pilot Supply Pressure should be adjusted between:

    50 – 80 psi

    Monitor the pressure on the gauge labeled "Pilot Supply Pressure".

    System Ready for Service

    Once pilot pressure is established and all selected hydraulic valves are opened, the ESD system becomes fully operational.

    • SCSSSV Open
    • MV Open
    • WV Open
    • SSV Open
    • ESDV Open
    • Remote ESD Stations Armed
    • Safety System Active
    Automatic Shutdown Features

    High Pressure (HP) and Low Pressure (LP) pilots may be installed on process pressure lines and connected to the ESD air loop.

    These pilots automatically activate the ESD system whenever abnormal pressure conditions are detected.

    Key Operating Values
    • Valve Opening Pressure ≈ 3000 psi
    • Pilot Supply Pressure = 50 – 80 psi
    • Automatic Shutdown via HP/LP Pilots
    • Pump Working Pressure = 10,000 psi

ESD Panel Course Summary

  • What is an ESD Panel?

    The Emergency Shut Down (ESD) Panel is a critical well control and safety system used to operate hydraulic safety valves on the wellhead and Christmas Tree. It uses hydraulic pressure, pneumatic controls, and pilot systems to open, monitor, and automatically close safety valves during emergency situations.

    Main Components
    • Hydraulic Oil Reservoir
    • Air Driven Hydraulic Pump
    • Instrument Air Regulator
    • Hydraulic Regulators
    • Three-Way Pneumatic Relay Valve
    • Pilot Line System
    • Remote ESD Stations
    • Pressure Gauges
    Safety Valves Controlled by the ESD Panel
    • SCSSSV – Surface Controlled Sub-Surface Safety Valve
    • MV – Master Valve
    • WV – Wing Valve
    • SSV – Surface Safety Valve
    • ESDV – Emergency Shut Down Valve
    How the System Works

    Hydraulic pressure is generated by an air-driven pump and supplied to the safety valves. The valves remain open only while hydraulic pressure and pilot pressure are available.

    If hydraulic pressure, air supply pressure, nitrogen supply pressure, or pilot pressure is lost, the system automatically closes the safety valves and shuts in the well.

    Control Line Pressure (SCSSSV)

    The SCSSSV control line receives hydraulic pressure from an air-driven diaphragm pump rated to 10,000 psi.

    If pilot pressure falls below 40 psi, the hydraulic pressure is automatically dumped and the SCSSSV closes.

    Safety Features
    • Hydraulic Relief Valve limits hydraulic pressure (typically 6000 psi).
    • Air Relief Valve prevents pump output pressure from exceeding 7000 psi.
    • Remote ESD Stations provide emergency shutdown capability.
    • Automatic shutdown occurs during abnormal pressure conditions.
    • Fail-safe design ensures valves close when control pressure is lost.
    ESD System Checks

    The ESD system must be tested before offshore or onshore operations and periodically during well testing to ensure emergency shutdown functionality remains available at all times.

    Opening Hydraulic Safety Valves
    1. Open air supply to the panel.
    2. Build hydraulic pressure using the air-driven pump.
    3. Adjust the hydraulic regulator.
    4. Open selected safety valves.
    5. Increase pressure to approximately 3000 psi.
    6. Build pilot pressure between 50–80 psi.
    7. Pull the relay valve handle to RESET.
    8. Maintain pilot pressure above 40 psi.
    9. Release the lock pin and place the system in service.
    Closing Hydraulic Safety Valves
    • Press the RED Emergency Stop Button to trip the ESD system.
    • Hydraulic pressure is dumped to the oil reservoir.
    • All safety valves automatically close.
    • The well is safely shut in.
    • Individual valves may also be manually isolated and closed.
    Emergency Shutdown Logic

    Any of the following conditions will automatically initiate a shutdown:

    • Activation of a local ESD station.
    • Activation of a remote ESD station.
    • Loss of pilot pressure below 40 psi.
    • Loss of hydraulic pressure.
    • Loss of air supply pressure.
    • Loss of nitrogen supply pressure.
    • High-pressure pilot trip.
    • Low-pressure pilot trip.
    Key Operating Values
    • SCSSSV Pump Working Pressure = 10,000 psi
    • Hydraulic Relief Valve Setting ≈ 6,000 psi
    • Air Relief Valve Limit ≈ 7,000 psi
    • Valve Opening Pressure ≈ 3,000 psi
    • Pilot Supply Pressure = 50–80 psi
    • Minimum Pilot Holding Pressure = 40 psi
    Key Takeaway

    The ESD Panel is a fail-safe hydraulic and pneumatic safety system designed to control, monitor, and automatically shut in a well by operating SCSSSV, MV, WV, SSV, and ESDV valves. Its primary purpose is to protect personnel, equipment, and the environment during well testing and production operations.
