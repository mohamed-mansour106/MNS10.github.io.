How to Operate Eruption Manifold

1- Eruption Manifold Description & Specifications

  • What is an Eruption Manifold?

    An Eruption Manifold is a high-pressure flow control manifold used in Surface Well Testing (SWT) and production operations to safely control, route, and divert well fluids under high-pressure conditions.

    It is specifically designed to handle severe service applications, including wells containing H₂S and CO₂.

    Sour Service Design

    The Eruption Manifold is manufactured for Sour Service applications.

    This means it is suitable for handling fluids containing:

    • Hydrogen Sulfide (H₂S)
    • Carbon Dioxide (CO₂)

    Special materials are used to resist corrosion, sulfide stress cracking, and other failures associated with sour environments.

    Size and Pressure Rating

    The manifold is rated as:

    • 2 Inch Line Size
    • 10,000 psi Cold Working Pressure (CWP)

    This high-pressure rating makes the manifold suitable for demanding well testing and production operations.

    Valve Configuration

    The Eruption Manifold is equipped with:

    • 8 Plug Valves, or
    • 8 Gate Valves

    Depending on the manifold design and operational requirements.

    These valves provide flexibility in directing and controlling high-pressure well flow.

    Inlet Connections

    The manifold contains multiple inlet connections to allow connection from different process lines.

    • 4 × 2" Figure 1502 Threaded Inlet Connections

    Multiple inlets provide operational flexibility during flow routing and well testing activities.

    Outlet Connection

    The manifold is equipped with:

    • 1 × 3" Figure 1502 Wing Outlet Connection

    The outlet directs flow safely to downstream equipment and process systems.

    Figure 1502 Hammer Union Connections

    The manifold uses Figure 1502 Hammer Union Connections, which are widely used in high-pressure oilfield applications.

    These connections provide:

    • High-pressure capability.
    • Reliable sealing performance.
    • Quick rig-up and rig-down operations.
    • Excellent durability in harsh field conditions.
    NACE MR0175 Compliance

    The Eruption Manifold complies with NACE MR0175 requirements.

    This international standard specifies material requirements for equipment operating in sour service environments containing H₂S.

    Compliance helps prevent:

    • Sulfide Stress Cracking (SSC).
    • Hydrogen Embrittlement.
    • Corrosion-related failures.
    Typical Applications
    • Surface Well Testing (SWT).
    • High-Pressure Well Flow Operations.
    • Well Clean-Up Activities.
    • Production Testing.
    • Emergency Flow Routing.
    • Sour Gas and Sour Oil Operations.

2- Purpose & Benefits of Eruption Manifold

  • Gathering Manifold for Dual String Wells

    The Eruption Manifold is designed to serve as a gathering and control manifold for Dual String Wells.

    A dual string well contains two separate production strings:

    • Long String
    • Short String

    The manifold allows operators to safely manage, monitor, and direct flow from both strings independently.

    Saving Operational Time

    One of the major advantages of an Eruption Manifold is the ability to switch flow from one string to another without disconnecting any pipework or flowlines.

    This significantly reduces:

    • Rig-up time.
    • Rig-down time.
    • Operational delays.
    • Personnel exposure to hazardous operations.
    Simultaneous Production and Treatment

    The Eruption Manifold allows operators to produce from one string while simultaneously monitoring or treating the other string.

    Examples include:

    • Flowing the Long String while monitoring the Short String.
    • Producing one string while pumping acid into the other.
    • Testing one string while keeping the second string isolated.

    This flexibility improves efficiency and minimizes production downtime.

    Independent Monitoring and Pumping Operations

    The manifold enables independent monitoring and pumping operations on each production string.

    Operators can:

    • Monitor pressure on both strings.
    • Inject treatment chemicals.
    • Pump acid.
    • Perform well kill operations.
    • Conduct pressure testing.

    Each string can be controlled separately from different flow sources.

    Well Kill Operations

    The Eruption Manifold allows kill fluid to be pumped into a selected string without affecting the second string.

    This capability is important during well control, intervention, and maintenance operations.

    Annulus Pressure Monitoring

    The manifold allows monitoring of both annulus spaces in a dual-string well:

    • A Annulus (Production Tubing to Production Casing)
    • B Annulus (Between Casing Strings)

    Continuous annulus monitoring helps detect:

    • Pressure build-up.
    • Tubing leaks.
    • Casing leaks.
    • Well integrity issues.
    Key Benefits
    • Provides control of dual-string wells.
    • Eliminates the need to disconnect flowlines when switching strings.
    • Allows production and treatment operations simultaneously.
    • Supports independent monitoring of each string.
    • Facilitates acid pumping and well kill operations.
    • Allows monitoring of A and B annulus pressures.
    • Improves operational efficiency and safety.

3- Applications & Operations of Eruption Manifold

  • Flowing the Long String (LS) – Valve No. 8

    When the Long String (LS) is selected for production or well testing, the flow path must be established safely through the Eruption Manifold.

    1. Ensure Choke Manifold gate valves are closed.
    2. Verify all Eruption Manifold valves are closed.
    3. Gradually open Valve No. 2.
    4. Gradually open Valve No. 8.
    5. Read the shut-in pressure using the DWT gauge (WHCIP).
    6. Open the Choke Manifold to start clean-up or well testing operations.

    WHCIP = Wellhead Closed-In Pressure.

    Shutting In the Long String (LS)

    To safely isolate the Long String after testing or production:

    1. Close the Choke Manifold and stop well flow.
    2. Close Valve No. 8.
    3. Close Valve No. 2.

    This completely isolates the Long String from the flow path.

    Flowing the Short String (SS) – Valve No. 5

    The Short String (SS) can be flowed independently through a separate route within the Eruption Manifold.

    1. Ensure Choke Manifold gate valves are closed.
    2. Confirm all Eruption Manifold valves are closed.
    3. Gradually open Valve No. 1.
    4. Gradually open Valve No. 5.
    5. Record WHCIP using the DWT gauge.
    6. Open the Choke Manifold to begin clean-up or testing operations.
    Shutting In the Short String (SS)

    To isolate the Short String:

    1. Close the Choke Manifold.
    2. Stop all well flow.
    3. Close Valve No. 5.
    4. Close Valve No. 1.

    The Short String is now fully shut in.

    Acidizing / Kill Pumping to Short String (Valve No. 5)

    This configuration allows acid treatment or kill fluid to be pumped into the Short String.

    1. Close all Eruption Manifold valves.
    2. Gradually open Valve No. 5.
    3. Gradually open Valve No. 3.
    4. Gradually open Valve No. 6.
    5. Record initial well pressure.
    6. Start pumping acid or kill fluid (Bullheading operation).

    Bullheading means pumping fluid directly into the well against formation pressure without allowing flow back to surface.

    Shut In Pumping Operations to Short String
    1. Stop pumping operations.
    2. Record well pressure.
    3. Gradually close Valve No. 6.
    4. Gradually close Valve No. 3.
    5. Gradually close Valve No. 5.

    This safely isolates the pumping line and the Short String.

    Acidizing / Kill Pumping to Long String (Valve No. 8)

    This configuration is used when treatment fluids must be pumped into the Long String.

    1. Close all Eruption Manifold valves.
    2. Gradually open Valve No. 8.
    3. Gradually open Valve No. 4.
    4. Gradually open Valve No. 6.
    5. Record initial well pressure.
    6. Start pumping acid or kill fluid.

    The Long String can now receive treatment fluid safely.

    Shut In Pumping Operations to Long String
    1. Stop pumping.
    2. Record well pressure.
    3. Gradually close Valve No. 6.
    4. Close the pumping line valve.
    5. Close the Long String valve (Valve No. 8).

    This isolates both the pump and the Long String after treatment.

    Key Learning Points
    • Valve No. 8 is typically associated with the Long String (LS).
    • Valve No. 5 is typically associated with the Short String (SS).
    • Always record WHCIP before flowing the well.
    • Always close the Choke Manifold before shutting in a string.
    • Acid and kill fluids can be pumped independently into either string.
    • Valves should always be opened and closed gradually to prevent pressure shock.
    • Proper valve sequencing is critical for safe well control operations.

Eruption Manifold - Summary Q&A

  • Q1: What is an Eruption Manifold?

    An Eruption Manifold is a high-pressure flow control manifold used to direct, monitor, isolate, and control flow from dual-string wells.

    Q2: What is the working pressure of the Eruption Manifold?

    The manifold is rated for 10,000 psi Cold Working Pressure (CWP).

    Q3: Why is the Eruption Manifold suitable for sour service?

    Because it complies with NACE MR-0175 requirements and is designed for H₂S and CO₂ service.

    Q4: What is the main purpose of an Eruption Manifold?

    To control flow from dual-string wells and allow production, monitoring, acidizing, and well kill operations.

    Q5: What are the two strings commonly connected to the manifold?

    Long String (LS) and Short String (SS).

    Q6: What is the benefit of switching between strings using the manifold?

    It saves time because operators can switch strings without disconnecting any flowlines or pipework.

    Q7: Which valve is connected to the Long String (LS)?

    Valve No. 8.

    Q8: Which valve is connected to the Short String (SS)?

    Valve No. 5.

    Q9: Which valves are opened to flow the Long String?

    Open Valve No. 2 and Valve No. 8.

    Q10: Which valves are opened to flow the Short String?

    Open Valve No. 1 and Valve No. 5.

    Q11: What must be recorded before opening the Choke Manifold?

    WHCIP (Wellhead Closed-In Pressure).

    Q12: What does WHCIP stand for?

    Wellhead Closed-In Pressure.

    Q13: Which valve is commonly used as the Pump/Kill connection?

    Valve No. 6.

    Q14: Which valves are opened to pump acid into the Short String?

    Open Valve No. 5, Valve No. 3, and Valve No. 6.

    Q15: Which valves are opened to pump acid into the Long String?

    Open Valve No. 8, Valve No. 4, and Valve No. 6.

    Q16: What is Bullheading?

    Bullheading is pumping fluid directly into the well against formation pressure without allowing returns to surface.

    Q17: What annulus pressures can be monitored using the Eruption Manifold?

    Both A Annulus (Production-Casing) and B Annulus (Casing-Casing).

    Q18: Why should valves be opened and closed gradually?

    To prevent pressure shock, water hammer effects, and equipment damage.

    Q19: What are the most important valves to remember?

    Valve 8 = Long String (LS)
    Valve 5 = Short String (SS)
    Valve 6 = Pump / Kill Line

    Q20: What is the key learning point of the Eruption Manifold?

    The Eruption Manifold allows safe control, monitoring, production, testing, acidizing, and well kill operations for dual-string wells while minimizing downtime and improving safety.
