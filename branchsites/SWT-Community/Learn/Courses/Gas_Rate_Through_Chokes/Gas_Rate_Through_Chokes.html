Gas Rate Through Chokes

Video lesson: Gas Rate Through Chokes

Choke Nipple Gas Rate Calculation

This calculator estimates the gas rate through a choke nipple from the wellhead pressure, the choke size, the gas gravity and the wellhead temperature.

Accurate results need four conditions: critical flow, dry gas, a positive choke bean (6 inch long) and ideal gas.

Qmscfd = (Coefficient × Pabs) / √(G × Tabs)
Pabs = WHP + 14.7 and Tabs = WHT + 460

Inputs and units

  • WHP in psig
  • Choke size in 64ths (a size from the coefficient table)
  • Gas gravity, air = 1 (default 0.7)
  • WHT in °F (default 60)
  • Result in MSCF/D

Input checks in the calculator

  • WHP must be greater than zero, and the choke size must be in the table.

Worked example

WHP = 1,000 psig, choke = 32/64, G = 0.7 and WHT = 100 °F.

Pabs = 1,000 + 14.7 = 1,014.7 and Tabs = 560
Q = 112.72 × 1,014.7 / √(0.7 × 560) = 5,776.9 MSCF/D
If the flow is not critical, or the gas is not dry, the result is not reliable.

Dry Gas Flow through a Positive Choke

This calculator scales a known calibration point. The page is based on a chart calibration at 3,750 psig, where a 28/64 inch choke passes 15.5 MMscf/day.

Qsc = (15.5 / 3,750) × Pup × (size / 28)2
Flow is proportional to the pressure and to the choke area (size squared)

Inputs and units

  • Upstream pressure in psig
  • Choke size: 24, 28, 32, 36, 40, 44 or 48 (in 64ths)
  • Gas gravity G
  • Result in MMscf/Day

Input checks in the calculator

  • Upstream pressure and choke size must be greater than zero.

Worked example

Upstream pressure = 3,000 psig and choke = 32/64.

(15.5 / 3,750) = 0.0041333
Area ratio = (32 / 28)2 = 1.3061
Q = 0.0041333 × 3,000 × 1.3061 = 16.20 MMscf/Day
At the calibration point (3,750 psig, 28/64) the result is 15.5 MMscf/Day.

Gas Rates by Choke Coefficient

This calculator builds the gas rate from the choke area, the upstream pressure and temperature, and the gas gravity. It gives the rate in mscf/d and in mmscf/d.

mscf/d = (C × A × P1 × Cd) / √(T × γg × Z)
A = π × (d/2)2, with d = size / 64 in inches

Inputs and units

  • Choke size in 64ths
  • Upstream temperature in °F (T = °F + 460)
  • Upstream pressure in psig (P1 = psig + 14.7)
  • Gas S.G. (γg)
  • Constants in the calculator: C = 605.4, Cd = 0.83, Z = 0.9

Worked example

Choke = 32/64, T = 100 °F, P = 1,000 psig and S.G. = 0.7.

A = π × (0.25)2 = 0.1963 sq in
mscf/d = 605.4 × 0.1963 × 1,014.7 × 0.83 / √(560 × 0.7 × 0.9)
mscf/d = 5,330.0, which is 5.330 mmscf/d
mmscf/d is the mscf/d value divided by 1,000.

Gas Flow Rate Estimation

This calculator estimates the gas rate through a fixed choke when critical flow exists, meaning the downstream pressure is low enough compared with the upstream pressure. It first builds an empirical choke coefficient.

C = (D/64)2.0871 × 26.616 × 18
Q = (C × Pup) / √(T × G)

Inputs and units

  • D: choke size in 1/64 inch
  • Pup: upstream pressure in psig
  • T: absolute temperature, °F + 459.67
  • G: gas gravity, air = 1.0
  • Q: flow rate in Mscfd

Worked example

Choke = 32/64, Pup = 1,000 psig, T = 100 °F and G = 0.7.

C = (0.5)2.0871 × 26.616 × 18 = 112.75
Q = 112.75 × 1,000 / √(559.67 × 0.7) = 5,697 Mscfd
The page shows the coefficient C next to the estimated rate, so you can see both.

Quick Gas Rate Estimation

This is the quick estimate of the dry gas rate through a choke, from the choke size, the wellhead pressure and temperature and the gas gravity.

coefficient = 0.11817 × choke2
qg = (coefficient × Pwh + 14.73) / √(γg × (Twh + 460)) × 10−3

Inputs and units

  • Choke size in 64ths
  • Wellhead pressure Pwh (the page labels it psia)
  • Wellhead temperature Twh in °F
  • Gas gravity (default 0.7)
  • Result in mscf/day

Input checks in the calculator

  • The calculator needs a choke size and a wellhead pressure greater than zero.

Worked example

Choke = 32/64, Pwh = 1,000, Twh = 100 °F and gravity = 0.7.

coefficient = 0.11817 × 322 = 121.006
qg = (121.006 × 1,000 + 14.73) / √(0.7 × 560) / 1,000 = 6.11 mscf/day
This is the quick method. Always read the units on the page and check the result against your test separator readings.
