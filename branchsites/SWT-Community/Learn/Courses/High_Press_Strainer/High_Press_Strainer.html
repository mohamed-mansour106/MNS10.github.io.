How to Operate 15K High Pressure Strainer Skid

Portable Strainer Skid Package – General Overview

A Portable Strainer Skid Package is a high-pressure filtration unit used during well testing and production operations to remove solid particles from the produced hydrocarbon stream before the fluids reach downstream equipment.

Wellhead
↓
Portable Strainer
↓
Separator / Heater / Flowline

Why is a Strainer Needed?

During well cleanup, flowback, and production testing, produced fluids often contain unwanted solid materials such as:

  • Sand
  • Formation particles
  • Scale
  • Corrosion debris
  • Metal fragments

If these solids are allowed to continue through the process system, they can damage downstream equipment such as chokes, valves, separators, meters, and pipelines.

Solids can cause erosion, blockage, inaccurate measurements, equipment failure, and costly downtime.

High Pressure Capability

The strainer package is designed for operation at pressures up to 15,000 psi.

This allows it to be installed directly downstream of high-pressure wellheads, where solids concentration is typically highest.

Maximum Working Pressure: 15,000 psig

Dual Strainer Design

The package contains two separate strainer vessels operating in a Duty / Standby configuration.

Process Flow
↓
Duty Strainer (Online)
↓
Cleaned Process Stream

Standby Strainer
(Ready for Service)

While one strainer is filtering the process stream, the second remains available as a backup. When the operating strainer becomes full of solids, flow can be diverted to the standby strainer, allowing cleaning without interrupting operations.

Manual Operation

The Portable Strainer Package is completely manual and does not require:

  • Electrical power
  • Instrument air
  • PLC control systems
  • External monitoring systems

All valve operations and switching between strainers are performed manually by field personnel.

This simple design makes the unit highly reliable and suitable for remote well testing locations.

The main purpose of the strainer is to remove solid particles from produced oil and gas streams before they enter downstream process equipment.

Proper filtration helps:

  • Increase equipment reliability.

Operational Policy

This procedure applies to all types of strainers used during Surface Well Testing operations, including:

  • Rig Operations
  • Rigless Operations
  • Onshore Installations
  • Offshore Installations

Operations must always be carried out according to company procedures and HSEQ (Health, Safety, Environment and Quality) requirements.

A Portable Strainer is a high-pressure filtration system used to remove sand and other solids from produced oil and gas streams. It protects downstream equipment, improves operational reliability, and can safely operate at pressures up to 15,000 psi using a duty/standby design.

Instrumentation & Control

The Portable Strainer Package is a fully manual system and does not require any automatic control devices, PLC systems, instrument air, or external monitoring systems.

  • No PLC Controller
  • No Automatic Shutdown System
  • No Remote Monitoring System
  • No External Utility Requirements

All valve operations and strainer changeovers are performed manually by field personnel.

The differential pressure (DP) represents the pressure loss across the strainer caused by flow resistance.

Normal Operation

When the strainer basket is clean, the pressure drop across the strainer is relatively low, indicating unrestricted flow through the filtration element.

Low Differential Pressure = Clean Strainer = Normal Flow Conditions

A rising PDI reading is a clear indication that the strainer basket is becoming plugged and may require cleaning or replacement.

Operating Procedures – Pre-Start Checks, Startup & Strainer Changeover

Before introducing any process fluid into the Portable Strainer Package, several inspections and safety checks must be completed to ensure the system is ready for operation and free from leaks or obstructions.

Pre-Start Checks

1. Rig Up Upstream and Downstream Facilities

Verify that all upstream and downstream process equipment has been properly connected to the strainer package according to the approved flow layout.

2. Walk the Flow Lines

Perform a visual inspection of all process lines and connections. Ensure hammer unions, flanges, fittings, and supports are properly secured and tightened.

Any loose connection can result in leakage, pressure loss, or a serious safety incident once the system is pressurized.
3. Verify Strainers are Clean

Ensure both strainer baskets have been cleaned and inspected. Confirm that the covers have been correctly reinstalled and securely tightened.

↓

↓

4. Purge the Manifold

Purge the manifold to remove trapped air, moisture, debris, or any unwanted gases before introducing process flow.

5. Close All Drain Valves

Verify that all drain valves are fully closed before startup. Open drains can lead to product loss, pressure release, and unsafe operating conditions.

Startup Procedure

The purpose of the startup procedure is to safely introduce the process stream through the selected duty strainer while maintaining pressure control and monitoring differential pressure.

Step 1 – Select the Duty Strainer

Choose either S-200A or S-200B as the operating (duty) strainer and open its inlet and outlet double block valves.

S-200A = Duty
or
S-200B = Duty
Step 2 – Install Pressure Gauges

Install pressure gauges upstream and downstream of both strainers. These gauges are used to monitor operating pressure and calculate differential pressure across the strainer.

DP = Pin − Pout
Step 3 – Isolate the Standby Strainer

Ensure all inlet and outlet valves on the standby strainer remain fully closed.

Only one strainer should normally be online during operation.
Step 4 – Open Downstream Isolation Valves

Gradually open the downstream isolation valves of the package. Opening valves slowly helps prevent sudden pressure shocks and flow disturbances.

Step 5 – Open Upstream Isolation Valves

Gradually open the upstream isolation valves to allow process fluid to enter the selected duty strainer.

Step 6 – Monitor Differential Pressure

Observe the Differential Pressure Indicator (PDI-200) during operation.

↓

↓
Normal Operation

Increasing differential pressure indicates solids accumulation inside the strainer basket.

System Purge

Before the Strainer Package can receive any hydrocarbon fluids, it must first be purged to remove air trapped inside the system. This is an important safety requirement because the presence of air mixed with hydrocarbons can create a potentially explosive atmosphere.

Air + Hydrocarbon Gas = Potentially Explosive Mixture

Purging is normally performed using Nitrogen gas or fresh water. The objective is to remove oxygen-containing air before introducing oil or gas into the equipment.

↓

↓

↓

Why Nitrogen is Preferred

Nitrogen is an inert gas that does not support combustion and is commonly used for safe purging operations in the oil and gas industry.

  • Non-flammable
  • Non-reactive
  • Displaces oxygen effectively
  • Reduces explosion risk

Although fresh water may be used as a purging medium, the package does not have a dedicated high-point vent. Therefore, Nitrogen is the recommended purging method.

Nitrogen Purging provides a safer and more reliable method of removing air from the system.

Nitrogen Purge Procedure

Step 1 – Isolate the Package

Ensure the Strainer Package is completely isolated from both upstream and downstream process lines using the package isolation valves.

Upstream Isolation
↓
Strainer Package
↓
Downstream Isolation

Step 2 – Connect Nitrogen Supply

Connect a Nitrogen hose or fill line to one of the package drain valves.

Nitrogen Cylinder
↓
Drain Valve
↓
Strainer Package

Step 3 – Open Internal Isolation Valves

Open all in-line isolation valves within the package to allow Nitrogen to flow throughout the entire system.

Step 4 – Pressurize the System

Slowly pressurize the package with Nitrogen to approximately 1 bar (14.5 psi).

Nitrogen Purge Pressure ≈ 1 bar

This pressure is sufficient to displace trapped air without stressing the equipment.

Step 5 – Vent the System

Open another drain valve and carefully vent the package pressure while maintaining a slight positive pressure inside the system.

Nitrogen In
↓
Air Out
↓
Positive Pressure Maintained

Step 6 – Repeat the Cycle

Repeat the pressurization and venting process several times. Each cycle removes additional air from the package.

Pressurize
↓
Vent
↓
Pressurize
↓
Vent
↓
Repeat Several Times

During the final cycle, do not vent the Nitrogen. Instead, retain a slight positive pressure by closing all drain valves.

Step 7 – Disconnect Nitrogen Supply

Once purging is complete, disconnect the Nitrogen hose from the package.

Step 8 – Reinstall Drain Flanges

Refit and secure all drain valve flanges before returning the package to service.

The package is now purged, oxygen-free, and ready to receive hydrocarbon fluids.

Water Purge / Flush

Fresh water can also be used as a purging medium if Nitrogen is unavailable.

Water used for purging must be completely free of hydrocarbons.

Water Purging Principle

Water Enters Package
↓
Air Displaced
↓
Air Vented Out
↓
Package Filled With Water

Because the package does not have a dedicated high-point vent, an alternative vent location is required during water flushing.

One connection point from the Differential Pressure Indicator (PDI) can be temporarily used as a high-point vent to allow trapped air to escape.

Trapped air pockets reduce purge effectiveness and may leave oxygen inside the system. Proper venting is therefore essential during water flushing operations.
Key Takeaway:
Before introducing hydrocarbons, the Strainer Package must be purged to eliminate air and oxygen. Nitrogen purging is the preferred method because it safely displaces air and prevents the formation of explosive gas mixtures. Water flushing may be used as an alternative when a suitable vent point is available.

Strainer Package Inspection Checklist

This checklist is performed during maintenance, inspection, or preparation of the Strainer Package before returning it to service. The objective is to ensure the equipment is clean, safe, mechanically sound, and ready for high-pressure operation.

14.1 Strainer Pots Inspection

1. Check Strainer Pots Have Been Flushed

Ensure the strainer vessels have been thoroughly flushed to remove any remaining hydrocarbons, sand, scale, debris, or contaminated fluids.

Flush Pot
↓
Remove Residual Fluids
↓
Safe for Inspection

2. External Visual Inspection

Inspect the external condition of the strainer vessels and verify there are no dents, cracks, corrosion, impact damage, or mechanical defects.

3. Check for Explosive Gases and H₂S

Before opening the vessel, conduct gas testing to verify the atmosphere inside the vessel is free from:

  • Hydrocarbon vapors
  • Explosive gas mixtures
  • Hydrogen Sulfide (H₂S)
Never enter or inspect an enclosed vessel until gas testing confirms safe conditions.

4. Verify Vessel is Empty

Ensure the strainer pot contains no sand, solids, liquids, sludge, or other contaminants.

5. Remove Strainer Screens

Remove the basket or screen elements for detailed cleaning and inspection.

6. Internal Inspection

Inspect the internal surfaces for:

  • Corrosion
  • Erosion
  • Flow damage
  • Metal loss
  • Cracks
High-velocity sand production can cause severe erosion damage inside strainers.

14.2 Pipework Inspection

1. Visual Inspection of Pipework

Inspect all pipework for corrosion, rust, scale buildup, dents, mechanical damage, or excessive wear.

2. Check Hammer Unions

Verify that all hammer unions rotate freely and can be assembled without excessive force.

Hammer Union
↓
Rotate Freely
↓
Proper Seal
↓
Safe Operation

3. Replace Seals and O-Rings

Replace all hammer union seals and O-rings with new certified components.

Batch numbers should be recorded for quality control and traceability purposes.

4. Inspect Inlet and Outlet Connections

Check all connection points for thread damage, flange defects, impact damage, or sealing surface deterioration.

5. Verify Pipework is Clear

Ensure all lines are completely free of:

  • Sand
  • Scale
  • Metal fragments
  • Debris

Particular attention should be given to flushing lines because they are more susceptible to blockage.

14.3 Gate Valve Inspection

1. Strip and Inspect Valves

All gate valves should be dismantled and inspected during major maintenance activities.

Remove Valve
↓
Disassemble
↓
Inspect Components
↓
Reassemble

2. Inspect Valve Components

Examine all valve parts for:

  • Wear
  • Erosion
  • Corrosion
  • Cracks
  • Mechanical damage

3. Inspect Valve Body Areas

Carefully inspect:

  • Seat pockets
  • Valve bores
  • Bonnets
  • Flanges

These locations experience the highest pressure and flow-related stress.

4. Replace Soft Components

Replace all soft sealing elements including:

  • O-rings
  • Seals
  • Packing elements

Record all batch numbers for maintenance records and certification purposes.

5. Functional Test

After reassembly, operate the valve through a complete open and close cycle.

↓
Smooth Movement
↓
Close Valve
↓
No Binding

The valve should move smoothly without sticking, excessive torque, or abnormal resistance.

6. Record Number of Turns

Record the number of turns required to fully open and fully close the valve.

Key Takeaway:
This checklist ensures that strainers, pipework, and gate valves are clean, free from damage, and mechanically fit for high-pressure service. Proper inspection reduces the risk of leaks, equipment failure, erosion damage, and safety incidents during well testing operations.

Portable High Pressure Strainer Package – Course Summary

The Portable High Pressure Strainer Package is designed to protect downstream well testing equipment by removing sand, solids, scale, and debris from produced oil and gas streams. It is commonly installed immediately downstream of the wellhead and can safely operate at pressures up to 15,000 psi.

Main Purpose:
Protect downstream equipment by filtering solids from produced hydrocarbons.

1. Package Overview

  • Portable skid-mounted design.
  • Rated for high-pressure service up to 15,000 psi.
  • Contains two strainers (Duty / Standby arrangement).
  • Fully manual operation.
  • No external utilities required.
  • No automatic control system.

The strainer removes unwanted solids from the process stream before they reach separators, chokes, flow meters, control valves, or production facilities.

Sand production can cause erosion, plugging, equipment damage, and inaccurate measurements.

8. Strainer Changeover

When differential pressure becomes excessive, the standby strainer is placed into service.

  1. Open standby strainer inlet and outlet valves.
  2. Monitor pressure equalization.
  3. Close duty strainer valves.
  4. Isolate and depressurize duty strainer.
  5. Remove cover and clean screen.
  6. Reassemble and prepare for next service.
Changeover allows uninterrupted production while maintenance is performed.

9. Purging Requirements

Before introducing hydrocarbons, air must be removed from the package to prevent formation of explosive gas mixtures.

Air Inside Package
↓
Purge System
↓
Remove Oxygen
↓
Safe for Hydrocarbons

10. Nitrogen Purging

Nitrogen is the preferred purging medium because it is inert and non-flammable.

  1. Isolate package.
  2. Connect nitrogen line.
  3. Open isolation valves.
  4. Pressurize to approximately 1 bar.
  5. Vent pressure while maintaining slight positive pressure.
  6. Repeat several cycles.
  7. Leave package under positive nitrogen pressure.
Recommended Nitrogen Purge Pressure ≈ 1 bar

11. Water Purging

Water flushing may be used when nitrogen is unavailable, provided the water is free from hydrocarbons and an adequate vent point is available.

Water purge is less preferred because trapped air may remain inside the package.

12. Inspection & Maintenance Checklist

Strainer Pots

  • Flush pots.
  • Inspect external condition.
  • Gas test for H₂S and hydrocarbons.
  • Remove solids and liquids.
  • Inspect screens.
  • Inspect internal corrosion and erosion.

Pipework

  • Inspect pipe condition.
  • Check hammer unions.
  • Replace seals and O-rings.
  • Inspect connections.
  • Remove sand and debris.

Gate Valves

  • Strip and inspect valves.
  • Inspect seats, bores, flanges, and bonnets.
  • Replace soft goods.
  • Function test open and close operation.
  • Record valve turns.

13. Key Safety Points

  • Always isolate equipment before maintenance.
  • Never open a pressurized strainer.
  • Gas test before opening vessels.
  • Purge air before introducing hydrocarbons.
  • Monitor differential pressure continuously.
  • Use proper PPE when handling process equipment.
  • Follow HSEQ requirements at all times.
