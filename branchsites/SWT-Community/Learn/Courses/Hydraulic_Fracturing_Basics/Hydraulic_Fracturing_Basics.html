Hydraulic Fracturing Basics

Video lesson: Hydraulic Fracturing Basics

The Idea of Fracturing

Some rocks let fluid pass too slowly.

  • Fluid is pumped at a high pressure until the rock splits.
  • The split is a fracture that goes away from the well.
  • The fracture is a new path with a much larger contact area.
  • Flow into the well increases.
  • It is used in tight rocks and to bypass damage.
A bigger contact area means more flow.

Fluid and Proppant

The fracture must stay open.

  • The fracturing fluid opens the fracture and carries the proppant.
  • Proppant is sand or a similar material that holds the fracture open.
  • When the pumping stops, the rock tries to close, and the proppant props it.
  • The proppant must resist the closure stress.
  • Additives control the thickness and the cleanup of the fluid.
No proppant, no lasting channel.

Stages of a Job

A job follows a plan.

  • A pad of fluid without proppant starts and widens the fracture.
  • Then fluid with proppant follows in steps.
  • A flush pushes the last proppant into the fracture.
  • Pressure, rate and proppant are monitored all the time.
  • After the job, the well is flowed back and cleaned.
Pad first, proppant next, flush last.

Equipment on Location

A job needs a lot of equipment.

  • Pumping units that give the high pressure and rate.
  • A blender that mixes fluid and proppant.
  • Tanks for fluid and water, and storage for proppant.
  • A control cabin where the job is watched and recorded.
  • Treating iron and valves rated for the pressure.
Everything must be rated for the job pressure.

Fracturing Safety

High pressure and chemicals need care.

  • Pressure test the lines before the job, as the procedure says.
  • Keep people clear of the treating iron during pumping.
  • Know the chemicals on location and their data sheets.
  • Control dust from the proppant.
  • Have a plan for fluid returns and waste.
  • Follow your company and site procedures.
Nobody near the iron while pumping.
