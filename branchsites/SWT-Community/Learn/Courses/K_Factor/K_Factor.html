How to Operate Heater

Indirect Fired Heater

An Indirect Fired Heater is a heating unit used in well testing operations to safely heat oil and gas streams without exposing hydrocarbons directly to a flame. It helps prevent hydrates, wax deposition, freezing problems, and improves separator efficiency.

1. Shell (Heating Bath)

The shell is a horizontal cylindrical vessel filled with a heating medium consisting of:

  • Fresh Water
  • Water/Glycol Mixture

The glycol concentration depends on ambient weather conditions and is used to prevent freezing. The shell operates at atmospheric pressure and stores the heating medium that transfers heat to the process fluid.

The shell acts as a large hot water bath surrounding the process coils.

2. High Pressure (HP) Coil Tubes

Inside the shell are several coil-shaped high-pressure tubes (typically 8 passes). These tubes carry the production fluids (oil and gas) through the heater.

The coils are manufactured from high-pressure carbon steel pipe and are designed for working pressures up to 10,000 psi.

  • Schedule XXS Pipe
  • Fig 1502 Hammer Union Connections
  • 8 Coil Passes for Maximum Heat Transfer
  • Inlet and Outlet Temperature Gauges

As the process fluid flows through the heated coils, its temperature increases before entering downstream equipment.

More coil passes provide greater heat transfer efficiency.

3. Burner and Fire Tube (U-Tube)

The heater contains a fuel burner that operates using:

  • Natural Gas
  • Diesel Fuel

The burner flame heats a U-shaped fire tube located inside the shell. Hot combustion gases travel through the tube, transferring heat to the surrounding water/glycol bath.

After transferring heat, the exhaust gases leave through a chimney stack equipped with a flame arrestor for safety.

Why Is It Called "Indirect Fired"?

The burner flame never comes into direct contact with the oil or gas. Heat is transferred indirectly through the fire tube and heating bath.

Indirect Heating Path:
Burner → Fire Tube → Water/Glycol Bath → Coil Tubes → Hydrocarbon Stream

This design significantly improves safety compared to direct-fired systems.

Benefits of Indirect Fired Heaters

  • Prevents hydrate formation in gas streams
  • Reduces wax and paraffin deposition
  • Improves oil and gas separation efficiency
  • Prevents freezing during cold weather operations
  • Provides safe heating without direct flame exposure
  • Improves overall flow assurance

Typical Applications

  • Well Testing Operations
  • Production Testing
  • Gas Wells
  • Condensate Wells
  • Multiphase Production Streams
  • Cold Weather Operations

Applications of Indirect Fired Heaters

Indirect Fired Heaters are widely used in oil and gas operations to improve production efficiency, solve flow assurance problems, and enhance separation performance. They are particularly important during well testing and production operations where temperature control is critical.

1. Hydrate Prevention

One of the most important applications of a heater is preventing the formation of gas hydrates.

Hydrates are ice-like crystals formed when:

  • Water vapor is present in the gas stream.
  • Pressure drops suddenly.
  • Gas temperature decreases rapidly.
High Pressure Gas
↓
Pressure Drop
↓

↓

↓

The heater raises the gas temperature before the pressure reduction point, preventing hydrates from forming and blocking the process equipment.

Hydrates can completely block chokes, flowlines, and separators if temperature is not controlled.

2. Wax Dissolution and Viscosity Improvement

During cold weather operations, crude oil temperature may fall below its wax appearance temperature. This causes wax crystals to form and deposit inside pipelines and equipment.

Heating the crude oil helps:

  • Dissolve existing wax deposits.
  • Prevent new wax formation.
  • Reduce oil viscosity.
  • Improve crude oil mobility and flow.
Higher Temperature → Lower Viscosity → Easier Oil Flow

3. Emulsion Reduction

During production, especially from wells with high water cut, stable oil-water emulsions may form.

An emulsion is a mixture where tiny water droplets remain suspended inside the oil, making separation difficult.

Heating reduces the viscosity of the fluids and weakens the emulsion, allowing oil and water to separate more easily.

4. Improve Three-Phase Separator Performance

Some crude oils produce excessive foam during production and well testing. Foam can negatively affect separator performance and reduce separation efficiency.

Heating helps:

  • Reduce foam stability.
  • Improve gas-liquid separation.
  • Improve oil-water separation.
  • Increase overall separator efficiency.
Three-phase separators are designed to separate Gas, Oil, and Water. Proper heating significantly improves this separation process.

Failure to maintain adequate process temperature can lead to hydrate plugging, wax deposition, poor separation efficiency, and production losses.

Installation of Indirect Fired Heater

Indirect Fired Heaters are supplied as skid-mounted, self-contained units. The complete heater assembly can be transported to the location and prepared for operation with minimal installation work. Typical site preparation includes erecting the chimney stack, connecting the fuel source, and filling the heating medium system with a fresh water and glycol mixture.

Initial Start-Up of Indirect Fired Heater

After the heater has been fully installed and rigged up on location, a series of startup checks and pressure tests must be completed before introducing process fluids. These procedures ensure safe operation, leak-free performance, and protection of the heater coils from excessive pressure.

Step 1 – Verify Chimney Installation

Ensure the heater chimney stack is installed vertically and firmly secured.

Step 2 – Fill Heater Shell

Fill the heater shell with a mixture of fresh water and glycol. The liquid level should be maintained approximately 3 inches above the upper coil.

Water transfers heat efficiently while glycol provides freeze protection during cold weather operations.

During filling, inspect the shell carefully for leaks.

Step 3 – Inspect High Pressure Manifold

Verify that all high-pressure manifold valves are closed and all flange bolts are properly tightened.

  • Check inlet valve.
  • Check outlet valve.
  • Check bypass valve.
  • Inspect flange bolts.
  • Verify no visible leaks.
Loose flange bolts can lead to dangerous leaks when pressure testing begins.

Gas Fired Indirect Heater – Start-Up Procedure

A Gas Fired Indirect Heater uses fuel gas to heat a water/glycol bath, which then transfers heat to the process fluid flowing through the heater coil. Proper startup is critical to ensure safe operation, maximum heating efficiency, and protection of personnel and equipment.

1. Complete Initial Startup Checks

Before lighting the heater, ensure all installation and pressure testing procedures have been completed successfully. The chimney must be secured, the shell filled with water and glycol, and all piping connections verified leak-free.

2. Adjust Fuel Gas Pressure

Fuel gas must be lined up to the heater and regulated correctly:

  • High Pressure Regulator: 30–40 psig
  • Low Pressure Regulator: 10–15 psig

These pressures supply both the burner and pilot flame with the proper amount of fuel gas.

3. Verify Fuel Gas Control System

The heater contains a thermostat and High Temperature Shutdown (HTSD) controller. These devices automatically control the fuel gas valve and shut down the burner if excessive temperatures occur.

4. Purge the Firebox

Before ignition, briefly allow fuel gas into the firebox and then close the valve. Wait at least five minutes to purge any accumulated gas. This prevents flash fires or explosions during ignition.

5. Ignite the Pilot Flame

The pilot flame is ignited first and observed through the magic-eye viewing glass. Slowly open the pilot gas valve and adjust the flame until a stable, steady pilot flame is obtained.

6. Start the Main Burner

With the pilot burning properly, slowly open the fuel gas shutoff valve. The main burner should ignite automatically from the pilot flame.

If the burner does not ignite, adjust the thermostat clockwise until the fuel gas valve opens and the burner lights.

7. Adjust Fuel-to-Air Ratio

Proper combustion requires the correct mixture of fuel gas and air:

  • Too much air: Unstable or blasting flame.
  • Too little air: Orange flame and black smoke.
  • Correct adjustment: Clean, stable flame with minimal smoke.

Air adjustment is performed using the rotating air damper located near the burner assembly.

8. Set Operating Temperature

Monitor the bath temperature gauge and adjust the thermostat accordingly.

  • Recommended Bath Temperature: 180°F
  • High Temperature Shutdown (HTSD): 200°F

These settings provide efficient heating while maintaining a safe margin below the water boiling point of 212°F.

9. Monitor Water/Glycol Level

The bath liquid level must always remain at least 3 inches above the upper process coil. Low water level can reduce heating efficiency and may damage the heater coil.

10. Connect the ESD System

The heater must be connected to the Emergency Shutdown (ESD) system. In case of fire, gas leak, H₂S release, or any emergency condition, the ESD system can automatically trip and shut down the heater.

11. Energize the Heater

Once all systems are verified:

  1. Open instrument air supply.
  2. Energize the ESD panel.
  3. Switch electrical power ON.
  4. Allow the burner to start automatically.

12. Keep Flow Paths Open During Warm-Up

During initial heating, it is recommended to keep the inlet valve, outlet valve, and bypass valve open. This prevents excessive pressure build-up caused by thermal expansion of trapped fluids inside the heater coil.

13. Heat the Water Bath

Adjust the burner to maximum firing rate initially to raise the bath temperature quickly and reduce startup time.

14. Monitor Chimney Exhaust

The exhaust should be clean and nearly smokeless.

  • Black Smoke: Indicates insufficient combustion air.
  • Solution: Increase air intake and adjust the fuel/air ratio.

15. Divert Process Flow Through the Heater

Once the water bath reaches approximately 180°F:

  • Keep inlet valve open.
  • Keep outlet valve open.
  • Close the bypass valve.

This forces the process fluid through the heater coil, allowing heat transfer to occur.

16. Heater Shutdown

To safely shut down the heater:

  1. Switch the heater OFF.
  2. Close the fuel gas supply valve.
  3. Verify burner flame extinction.
  4. Leave the system in a safe condition.
Important Safety Notes:
  • Always purge the firebox before ignition.
  • Maintain water level above the process coil.
  • Monitor burner flame quality continuously.
  • Ensure ESD functionality is tested before operation.
  • Keep bypass, inlet, and outlet valves properly positioned to prevent thermal pressure build-up.

Diesel Fired Indirect Heater – Start-Up Procedure

A Diesel Fired Indirect Heater operates using a diesel burner instead of fuel gas. The burner heats a water/glycol bath inside the heater shell, and the heat is then transferred to the process fluid flowing through the heater coil. Proper startup procedures ensure safe operation, maximum heating efficiency, and protection of personnel and equipment.

1. Complete Initial Startup Requirements

Before operating the heater, ensure all Initial Startup procedures have been completed, including chimney installation, shell filling, pressure testing, and leak inspection.

2. Inspect the Diesel Burner

Check the RELIO diesel burner and verify that all components are in good condition. Inspect fuel nozzles, ignition system, blower, and electrical connections.

A damaged burner can result in poor combustion, excessive smoke, or ignition failure.

3. Connect the Diesel Fuel Supply

Open the diesel supply valve and allow fuel to flow through the diesel filter to the burner. Ensure all fuel connections are leak-free.

4. Verify the Diesel Return Line

Check that the diesel return line is properly connected to the diesel tank and free from blockages.

Excess fuel not consumed by the burner is returned through this line.

5. Fill the Diesel Tank

Fill the diesel tank with sufficient fuel for the operation. Keep an external diesel refill hose available and fitted with a control valve for safe refueling during operation.

6. Connect Electrical Power

Connect the heater electrical panel to the appropriate power source:

  • 110V or 220V
  • 50Hz or 60Hz

Install and secure the grounding (earthing) rod to prevent static electricity buildup.

Proper grounding is mandatory to prevent electrical hazards and ignition risks.

7. Check Blower Rotation Direction

Verify that the blower impeller rotates in the correct direction according to the arrow marked on the burner housing.

If rotation is incorrect, reconnect electrical phases (E, N, L1, L2) until the proper rotation direction is achieved.

8. Connect Emergency Shutdown (ESD)

Connect the heater pneumatic shutdown port to the ESD control panel.

Perform a function test to verify that the heater trips properly during an emergency shutdown.

The heater must shut down immediately when an ESD signal is activated.

9. Energize the System

Once all checks are complete:

  1. Open instrument air supply.
  2. Energize the ESD panel.
  3. Switch electrical power ON.
  4. Wait for the diesel burner to start automatically.

10. Keep Heater Flow Paths Open

During warm-up, keep the following valves open:

  • Inlet Valve
  • Outlet Valve
  • Bypass Valve

This prevents pressure buildup caused by thermal expansion of trapped fluids inside the heater coil.

Trapped fluid exposed to heat can create extremely high pressures and damage the coil.

11. Heat the Water Bath

Adjust the burner to maximum firing rate to heat the water/glycol bath quickly.

12. Monitor Chimney Exhaust

Observe the exhaust leaving the chimney.

  • Black Smoke: Insufficient air supply.

If black smoke is observed:

  • Increase combustion air intake.
  • Adjust fuel-to-air ratio.
  • Lubricate moving air intake components if required.

13. Set Operating Temperature

Monitor the bath temperature gauge continuously.

  • Recommended Bath Temperature: 180°F
  • High Temperature Shutdown (HTSD): 200°F

The HTSD controller automatically shuts down the burner if temperature exceeds the safe limit.

Water boils at approximately 212°F. The HTSD setting provides a safety margin below the boiling point.

To safely shut down the heater:

  • Maintain adequate diesel supply throughout operation.

  • Ensure ESD functionality is tested before use.

  • Never operate the heater without proper grounding.

Indirect Fired Heaters – Course Summary

This course explains the design, operation, installation, and safe start-up of indirect fired heaters used in oil and gas well testing operations to control temperature, improve separation efficiency, and prevent hydrate, wax, and emulsions issues.

1. Equipment Overview

  • Shell: Atmospheric horizontal vessel filled with water/glycol mixture used as a heat medium.
  • Coil (HP Tubes): High-pressure carbon steel coil (multi-pass) carrying process oil & gas.
  • Burner System: Gas or diesel fired system generating heat inside firebox.
  • U-Tube / Firebox: Transfers combustion heat to heating medium safely without mixing with process fluid.

2. Main Function

Indirect fired heaters increase fluid temperature in a controlled way to improve flow assurance and separation.

  • Prevent hydrate formation in gas streams.
  • Reduce wax deposition and improve crude mobility.
  • Break emulsions and improve oil-water separation.
  • Enhance separator efficiency in foamy crude conditions.

3. Installation Summary

  • Place skid on leveled foundation.
  • Install and secure chimney stack.
  • Verify burner assembly tightness.
  • Connect coil inlet/outlet and fuel system.
  • Perform hydrotest (coil) and pneumatic test (instrument lines).

4. Pre-Start Safety Checks

  • Ensure shell is filled with water/glycol above coil level (~3 inches minimum).
  • Verify all valves, flanges, and manifolds are properly tightened.
  • Confirm bypass line is available for pressure protection.
  • Check ESD system and pneumatic shutdown connection.

5. Gas Fired Heater Operation

  • Set fuel gas pressure regulators (high and low pressure stages).
  • Purge firebox before ignition.
  • Ignite pilot flame and confirm stable combustion.
  • Start main burner and adjust air/fuel ratio.
  • Control temperature using thermostat (typically ~180°F operating).
  • Monitor water bath level continuously for efficiency.
  • Ensure smokeless combustion (proper air adjustment).

6. Diesel Fired Heater Operation

  • Check diesel system, burner condition, and return line.
  • Ensure correct electrical supply and grounding.
  • Verify blower rotation direction.
  • Start burner via control panel and ESD system.
  • Adjust fuel/air ratio for clean combustion.
  • Maintain water bath temperature around 180°F.

7. Safety Highlights

  • Never allow coil to overpressure — always keep bypass logic in mind.
  • Ensure ESD system is functional before operation.
  • Prevent dry operation of heater shell.
  • Maintain proper ventilation and chimney flow.
  • Use controlled shutdown procedures for both gas and diesel systems.

8. Key Engineering Concepts

  • Heat transfer through indirect water bath system.
  • Pressure protection via multi-pass coil design.
  • Fuel combustion control (air/fuel ratio optimization).
  • Thermal efficiency monitoring via inlet/outlet temperature.

Final Summary

Indirect fired heaters are critical surface well testing equipment designed to safely heat hydrocarbon fluids using controlled combustion without direct contact between flame and process fluid. Proper installation, pressure testing, and burner control ensure safe operation and high efficiency during well testing operations.

Indirect Fired Heaters – Q&A (Well Testing)

This section covers common questions and answers for understanding indirect fired heaters used in oil & gas well testing operations.

Q1: What is an indirect fired heater?

It is a heating system where combustion heat is transferred to a water/glycol bath, which then heats a high-pressure coil carrying the process fluid without direct flame contact.

Q2: Why do we use indirect fired heaters in well testing?

To safely heat oil and gas streams for improving flow, preventing hydrates, reducing wax, breaking emulsions, and improving separator performance.

Q3: What is inside the heater shell?

A mixture of fresh water and glycol that acts as a heat transfer medium surrounding the coil system.

Q4: What is the function of the coil?

The coil carries high-pressure production fluids and absorbs heat from the surrounding hot water bath.

Q5: Why do we use glycol in the shell?

Glycol prevents freezing in cold environments and improves heat transfer stability.

Q6: What is the purpose of the burner?

The burner generates controlled heat using gas or diesel fuel to heat the water/glycol bath.

Q7: What is the safe operating temperature?

Typically around 180°F, with a high temperature shutdown set below the boiling point of water (212°F).

Q8: What is the role of the bypass line?

It allows fluid to bypass the heater during startup, shutdown, or pressure stabilization to avoid overheating or overpressure.

Q9: What is the purpose of pressure testing?

To ensure the coil and manifold can safely handle design pressure (up to 5K–10K psi depending on specification) without leaks or failure.

Q10: What is the difference between gas-fired and diesel-fired heaters?

Gas-fired heaters use natural gas as fuel, while diesel-fired heaters use liquid diesel. Both perform the same heating function but differ in burner system and fuel handling.

Q11: What happens if water level drops in the shell?

It reduces heat transfer efficiency and may cause overheating and damage to the coil system.

Q12: Why is air/fuel ratio important?

It ensures clean combustion, prevents smoke formation, improves efficiency, and protects burner components.

Q13: What is the function of the ESD system?

Emergency Shutdown (ESD) system automatically shuts down the heater in unsafe conditions such as high temperature, fire risk, or abnormal pressure.

Q14: Why do we purge before ignition?

To remove any accumulated gas from the firebox and prevent explosion during ignition.

Q15: What is the main safety rule during operation?

Never operate the heater without proper water level, functional ESD system, and controlled pressure in the coil.
