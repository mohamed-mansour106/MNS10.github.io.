Liquid Meter Calibration and Meter Factor

Video lesson: Liquid Meter Calibration and Meter Factor

Meter Factor Explained

The meter factor (MF) is the ratio between the actual measured volume and the volume the meter indicated.

MF = Prover Volume / Meter Indicated Volume
MF = Actual Volume / Meter Volume

Inputs and units

  • Prover (actual) volume in bbl, above zero
  • Meter indicated volume in bbl, above zero
  • The calculator tells you if MF is above 1, below 1 or equal to 1

Worked example

Prover volume 100 bbl and meter indicated volume 99.5 bbl (example inputs chosen for this course).

MF = 100 / 99.5 = 1.00503
MF is above 1, so the actual volume is higher than the indicated volume.
MF above 1 means the meter reads low. MF below 1 means the actual volume is below what the meter indicated.

Composite Meter Factor

The composite meter factor (CMF) combines the meter factor with the oil shrinkage.

CMF = MF × SF
SF = 1 − Shrinkage (as a fraction, the input is in %)

Inputs and units

  • Meter factor MF, above zero
  • Oil shrinkage in %, between 0 and 100
  • Results: shrinkage factor SF and CMF

Worked example

MF = 1.005 and oil shrinkage = 4%.

SF = 1 − 4/100 = 0.96000
CMF = 1.005 × 0.96 = 0.96480
The shrinkage here is entered as a percentage loss, not as a shrinkage factor like 0.96.

Advanced Meter Factor Calibration

This calculator compares the volume that went into a calibration tank with the volume that the meter indicated, each corrected for temperature.

Ktank = 1 − [(0.066 × API + 2.75) × (Ttank − 60) × 10−4]
Kmeter = 1 − [(0.066 × TF + 2.75) × (Tmeter − 60) × 10−4]
Tank volume = (Final Tank − Initial Tank) × Tank Factor × Ktank
Meter volume = (Final Meter − Initial Meter) × Kmeter
Correction factor Cf = Tank volume / Meter volume

Inputs and units

  • API at 60°F and tank temperature in °F
  • Initial and final tank readings in cm, tank factor in bbl/cm
  • Meter temperature in °F, initial and final meter readings in bbl
  • Pre-test Mf

Worked example

API 40, tank temperature 90°F, tank 10 cm to 60 cm, tank factor 2.0 bbl/cm, meter temperature 85°F, meter 1000 to 1098 bbl (example readings chosen for this course; the meter K uses the tank factor value in the place the formula shows as TF).

Ktank = 0.98383
Tank volume = 50 × 2.0 × 0.98383 = 98.383 bbl
Kmeter = 0.99279
Meter volume = 98 × 0.99279 = 97.294 bbl
Cf = 98.383 / 97.294 = 1.01119
Read the tank and meter at the same moment, and record both temperatures. Cf compares what the tank received with what the meter indicated.

Halliburton Turbine Meter Calibration

This calculator gives the values to enter on an MCII analyzer from the meter calibration in pulses per gallon.

Kbbl = Kgal × 42 (rounded down to a whole number)
TOP display (bbls) = Kbbl / 100, one decimal
BOTTOM display (factor) = 86,400 / Kbbl = (1440 × 60) / Kbbl

Inputs and units

  • Meter calibration in pulses/gal (above zero)
  • Results: pulses per bbl to enter in SCAN, TOP display and BOTTOM display factor

Worked example

Meter calibration 100 pulses/gal.

Kbbl = 100 × 42 = 4200 pulses/bbl
TOP display = 4200 / 100 = 42.0
BOTTOM display = 86,400 / 4200 = 20.571
There are 42 gallons in a barrel, which is why the page multiplies by 42.

Use the Halliburton Turbine Meter Calibration Calculator to check your own numbers.

Liquid Meter Operating Ranges

Pick a meter model and enter your current flow. The calculator checks if the flow is inside the meter operating range.

QBPD = QGPM × 34.2857 (1440 min / 42 gal)
Check: Qmin ≤ Qcurrent ≤ Qmax

Inputs and units

  • Meter model: Floco F2500 (1 in, 2 in, 3 in), Floco F382 2 in, Floco F383 3 in, Rotron 2 in, 3 in, 4 in, Halliburton 1.5 in, 2 in, 3 in, 4 in
  • Current flow in BPD or GPM
  • Status: under-ranged below the minimum, over-ranged above the maximum, optimal in between

A few ranges from the page

MeterMin GPMMax GPMMin BPDMax BPD
Floco F2500 2 in6602062,057
Halliburton 2 in404001,30013,000
Halliburton 4 in1001,2003,40041,000

Worked example

Halliburton 2 in meter, current flow 100 GPM.

100 GPM × 34.2857 = 3,429 BPD
The range is 1,300 to 13,000 BPD, so the status is Optimal range.
Below the minimum the meter is under-ranged and loses linear accuracy. Above the maximum it is over-ranged.

Flow Meter Performance

This calculator turns a flow in GPM into the meter output frequency, and checks it against the maximum frequency of the selected meter size.

Freq (Hz) = GPM × Factor / 60
BBLS/day = GPM × 34.2857
m³/day = GPM × 5.45099

Inputs and units

  • Meter size (3/8 in up to 8 in, each with its own factor and maximum frequency)
  • Current flow rate in GPM
  • Status: Safe Operating Range, High Load Warning above 80% of the maximum frequency, and Exceeds Max above it

Two entries from the page

MeterFactorMax frequency (Hz)
2 in55365
4 in30600

Worked example

4 in meter at 1,000 GPM, and a 2 in meter at 100 GPM.

4 in: 1,000 × 30 / 60 = 500 Hz, which is above 80% of 600 (480 Hz) but below 600, so High Load Warning
2 in: 100 × 55 / 60 = 91.67 Hz, which is below 80% of 365 (292 Hz), so Safe Operating Range
Keep the output frequency under the meter maximum. The calculator warns you when it passes 80% of that limit.
