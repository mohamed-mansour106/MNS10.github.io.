How to Operate Multistage pump

📌 Multi-Stage Pumps Procedure (Explanation)

1. General

Multi-stage pumps (or other pump types) are used in surface well testing and production operations to re-inject produced fluids such as oil and dissolved gas back into either the production line or the sea line.

The main objective is to comply with environmental regulations and operating philosophy of Zero Flaring and Zero Dumping, meaning no burning of gas and no disposal of fluids to the environment. This helps reduce pollution affecting air, sea, and land.

2. Purpose

The purpose of this procedure is to define a safe and clear method for re-injecting produced hydrocarbons (oil and dissolved gas) during well testing operations.

It ensures protection of:

  • 👷 Personnel safety
  • ⚙️ Equipment and assets
  • 🌍 Environment

All activities must follow the company HSEQ policy (Health, Safety, Environment, Quality).

3. Policy

This procedure applies to all types of multi-stage pumps used in surface well testing operations, whether offshore or onshore.

It is aligned with standard operating procedures (SOPs) to ensure consistent, safe, and controlled handling of produced fluids during reinjection operations.

📌 Multi-Stage Pump Handling, Transport & Installation

4. Pump Handling

This section explains how to safely handle a multi-stage pump during lifting and installation.

The main safety rule is to always respect the center of gravity, which is closer to the motor end of the skid (not the middle). If this is ignored, the pump can tilt or drop during lifting.

  • ✔ Use proper lifting lugs or designed cross members only.
  • ⚠️ Always check the center of gravity before lifting even slightly.
  • ❌ Never use chains or straps directly on pump body, supports, or brackets.
  • ❌ Do NOT lift using motor eyebolts or wrap straps over the motor.
  • ⚠️ Do NOT disturb pump-motor alignment (very critical for performance).

4.2 Pump Transportation

This part covers how to safely transport the pump from one location to another.

  • 🚛 Follow proper loading and offloading procedures at all times.
  • 📸 Use tag lines for stability and take photos if any damage is observed.
  • 📩 Report damage immediately to logistics coordinator for insurance follow-up.
  • 🧳 Ensure transport method provides short-term protection during shipping.
  • 🧪 If long-term storage is required, fill pump with proper solvent and seal inlet/outlet.

4.3 Pump Spot & Location (Installation Positioning)

This section explains how to properly position the pump at site for safe and efficient operation.

  • 📏 Maintain proper NPSH (Net Positive Suction Head) to avoid cavitation. This is done by placing the pump close to the fluid source and ensuring sufficient liquid head.
  • ⚠️ Poor NPSH leads to cavitation, vibration, and possible pump damage.
  • 🏗️ Ensure enough floor space and overhead clearance for crane lifting and maintenance.
  • 🧰 The pump skid can be supported along its full length or on support pillars.
  • 📐 Support spacing should not exceed approximately 4 feet between supports.

📌 Piping Instructions (شرح بسيط لمواسير الـ Multi-Stage Pump)

🔧 1) تركيب المواسير (General Piping Rules)

لازم تركيب المواسير يتم حسب Hydraulic Institute Standards علشان تضمن تشغيل آمن بدون اهتزازات أو مشاكل تدفق.

  • ✔ المواسير لازم تكون مدعومة كويس (steel أو wood supports).
  • ❌ ممنوع تعتمد على الـ pump flange في حمل المواسير.
  • ⚠️ أي force على الفلانشات ممكن يسبب leakage أو misalignment.

📉 2) Suction Line (خط السحب)

خط السحب هو أهم جزء لأنه يؤثر مباشرة على أداء المضخة و NPSH.

  • ✔ لازم يكون قصير قدر الإمكان لتقليل friction losses.
  • ⚠️ كل ما الخط أطول = فقد ضغط أعلى = احتمال cavitation أعلى.
  • 🔧 يفضل استخدام flexible hose أو expansion joint.

الهدف من الـ flexible connection هو امتصاص:

  • 🔹 التمدد الحراري (thermal expansion)
  • 🔹 الاهتزازات (vibration)
  • 🔹 أي حركة في المواسير

لو لم يتم استخدامه → ممكن يحدث: misalignment + leakage + pump failure

📈 3) Discharge Line (خط الطرد)

  • ✔ لازم يتحمل ضغط أعلى من pump shut-off pressure (no flow condition).
  • ✔ لازم يكون مناسب لـ operating duty point pressure.

🔒 4) Valves & Protection Devices

🟢 Suction & Discharge Isolation Valves

يتم تركيب valves لعزل المضخة أثناء التشغيل أو الصيانة.

🔁 Relief Valve

يتم توصيله يرجع إلى suction tank. وظيفته حماية النظام من overpressure ويجب أن يتحمل على الأقل minimum flow of pump.

🎚️ Adjustable Choke / VSD

يستخدم للتحكم في التدفق والضغط أثناء التشغيل.

مهم جدًا عند بداية التشغيل لأن المضخة ممكن تشتغل على ضغط منخفض، لذلك نستخدم auto-choke لتجنب تشغيلها في حالة unsafe (upper thrust condition).

🔄 Check Valve

يتم تركيبه على discharge line لمنع رجوع السائل (backflow) عند توقف المضخة.

📌 الخلاصة

أهم 3 أفكار في piping:

  • ✔ لا تضغط على الفلانشات
  • ✔ suction line قصير ومرن
  • ✔ حماية المضخة من backflow و overpressure

🔹 Pump Operations Procedure – Detailed Explanation

This procedure explains how to safely start, operate, and monitor the transfer pump while maintaining proper flow rate, pressure, cooling, and equipment protection.

1. Determine Required Flow Rate

Before starting the pump, determine the required production or transfer rate.

  • 50 Hz: 2,000 – 6,200 BPD
  • 60 Hz: 3,000 – 7,400 BPD

Pump capacity depends on motor frequency. Higher frequency increases pump speed and flow rate.

2. Start the Generator

Start the generator according to standard operating procedures and set the required frequency and voltage.

3. Check Thrust Chamber Oil Level

Verify that the thrust chamber oil level is between the minimum and maximum marks.

The thrust chamber oil lubricates and cools the thrust bearings that absorb axial loads generated by the pump.

4. Check Seal Pot Oil Level

Ensure the seal pot oil level is between the minimum and maximum limits.

The seal pot protects the mechanical seal and prevents process fluid contamination.

5. Charge Seal Pot with Nitrogen

Pressurize the seal pot with nitrogen gas at approximately 25 psi higher than the pump intake pressure.

This positive pressure prevents process fluid from entering the seal system.

6. Check for Leaks

Inspect all piping connections, valves, flanges, hoses, and fittings for leaks before startup.

7. Open Cooling Water Valves

Open the cooling water supply to the seal pot at approximately 10% flow.

Cooling water removes heat generated around the seal assembly during operation.

8. Open Pump Suction Valves

Ensure all suction valves are fully open to provide unrestricted fluid flow to the pump.

Restricted suction flow may cause cavitation and pump damage.

9. Maintain Adequate NPSH

Keep the surge tank liquid level at least 500 mm above the pump centerline.

This provides sufficient Net Positive Suction Head (NPSH) and prevents vapor formation inside the pump.

10. Set Adjustable Choke

Initially set the adjustable choke at approximately 20% open.

This allows controlled startup and gradual increase in flow rate.

11. Open Discharge Isolation Valves

Ensure all discharge isolation valves are fully open before starting the pump.

12. Bleed Air from the System

Open bleed valves when necessary to remove trapped air from suction and discharge lines.

Air pockets can reduce pump performance and create unstable operation.

13. Energize the Control Panel

Switch ON the power supply to the control panel and verify normal indications.

14. Start the Pump

Start the pump and observe startup conditions carefully.

15. Adjust Flow Rate and Pressure

Monitor discharge pressure and adjust the choke to achieve the required operating flow rate and pressure.

16. Maintain Differential Pressure

Compare pump discharge pressure with the sea line or production line pressure.

Maintain a differential pressure between:

  • 50 – 100 psig

This pressure difference ensures proper flow through the system without overloading the pump.

17. Choke Adjustment During Operation

If differential pressure increases above the required range:

  • Gradually open the adjustable choke.
  • Reduce discharge pressure.
  • Maintain the pump pressure only 50–100 psi above line pressure.

If differential pressure becomes too low:

  • Reduce choke opening gradually.
  • Do not close the choke below 24/64".

Operating below this minimum opening may cause excessive backpressure and equipment damage.

18. Temperature Monitoring

After approximately 15 minutes of operation, measure temperatures using a temperature gun.

Record temperatures of:

  • Pump body
  • Motor housing
  • Thrust chamber
  • Seal housing

Abnormally high temperatures may indicate lubrication problems, cooling issues, or excessive mechanical load.

19. Record Operating Pressures

Record:

  • Pump intake pressure
  • Pump discharge pressure

Initial readings should be taken after startup and then recorded every hour throughout operation.

20. Assign Dedicated Operator

One person should be assigned to continuously monitor both the pump and generator during operation.

The operator is responsible for:

  • Monitoring pressures
  • Monitoring temperatures
  • Checking oil levels
  • Checking cooling water flow
  • Checking for leaks
  • Monitoring generator performance

🔹 Key Safety Points

  • Never start the pump with suction valves closed.
  • Always maintain sufficient NPSH.
  • Keep seal pot pressure higher than intake pressure.
  • Monitor temperatures regularly.
  • Maintain differential pressure between 50–100 psig.
  • Never reduce choke opening below 24/64".
  • Always keep a dedicated operator supervising the equipment.

Diesel Fired Indirect Heater – Start-Up Procedure

A Diesel Fired Indirect Heater operates using a diesel burner instead of fuel gas. The burner heats a water/glycol bath inside the heater shell, and the heat is then transferred to the process fluid flowing through the heater coil. Proper startup procedures ensure safe operation, maximum heating efficiency, and protection of personnel and equipment.

1. Complete Initial Startup Requirements

Before operating the heater, ensure all Initial Startup procedures have been completed, including chimney installation, shell filling, pressure testing, and leak inspection.

2. Inspect the Diesel Burner

Check the RELIO diesel burner and verify that all components are in good condition. Inspect fuel nozzles, ignition system, blower, and electrical connections.

A damaged burner can result in poor combustion, excessive smoke, or ignition failure.

3. Connect the Diesel Fuel Supply

Open the diesel supply valve and allow fuel to flow through the diesel filter to the burner. Ensure all fuel connections are leak-free.

4. Verify the Diesel Return Line

Check that the diesel return line is properly connected to the diesel tank and free from blockages.

Excess fuel not consumed by the burner is returned through this line.

5. Fill the Diesel Tank

Fill the diesel tank with sufficient fuel for the operation. Keep an external diesel refill hose available and fitted with a control valve for safe refueling during operation.

6. Connect Electrical Power

Connect the heater electrical panel to the appropriate power source:

  • 110V or 220V
  • 50Hz or 60Hz

Install and secure the grounding (earthing) rod to prevent static electricity buildup.

Proper grounding is mandatory to prevent electrical hazards and ignition risks.

7. Check Blower Rotation Direction

Verify that the blower impeller rotates in the correct direction according to the arrow marked on the burner housing.

If rotation is incorrect, reconnect electrical phases (E, N, L1, L2) until the proper rotation direction is achieved.

8. Connect Emergency Shutdown (ESD)

Connect the heater pneumatic shutdown port to the ESD control panel.

Perform a function test to verify that the heater trips properly during an emergency shutdown.

The heater must shut down immediately when an ESD signal is activated.

9. Energize the System

Once all checks are complete:

  1. Open instrument air supply.
  2. Energize the ESD panel.
  3. Switch electrical power ON.
  4. Wait for the diesel burner to start automatically.

10. Keep Heater Flow Paths Open

During warm-up, keep the following valves open:

  • Inlet Valve
  • Outlet Valve
  • Bypass Valve

This prevents pressure buildup caused by thermal expansion of trapped fluids inside the heater coil.

Trapped fluid exposed to heat can create extremely high pressures and damage the coil.

11. Heat the Water Bath

Adjust the burner to maximum firing rate to heat the water/glycol bath quickly.

12. Monitor Chimney Exhaust

Observe the exhaust leaving the chimney.

  • Clean Exhaust: Proper combustion.
  • Black Smoke: Insufficient air supply.

If black smoke is observed:

  • Increase combustion air intake.
  • Adjust fuel-to-air ratio.
  • Lubricate moving air intake components if required.

13. Set Operating Temperature

Monitor the bath temperature gauge continuously.

  • Recommended Bath Temperature: 180°F
  • High Temperature Shutdown (HTSD): 200°F

The HTSD controller automatically shuts down the burner if temperature exceeds the safe limit.

Water boils at approximately 212°F. The HTSD setting provides a safety margin below the boiling point.

14. Divert Process Flow Through the Heater

Once the bath reaches operating temperature:

  • Keep inlet valve open.
  • Keep outlet valve open.
  • Close bypass valve.

Closing the bypass valve forces the process fluid through the heated coil, allowing heat transfer.

15. Heater Shutdown Procedure

To safely shut down the heater:

  1. Switch the heater OFF.
  2. Close the diesel fuel supply valve.
  3. Verify burner shutdown.
  4. Leave the heater in a safe condition.
Key Safety Reminders:
  • Always verify blower rotation before startup.
  • Maintain adequate diesel supply throughout operation.
  • Monitor chimney exhaust for signs of poor combustion.
  • Ensure ESD functionality is tested before use.
  • Keep the water level above the upper process coil.
  • Never operate the heater without proper grounding.

Indirect Fired Heaters – Course Summary

This course explains the design, operation, installation, and safe start-up of indirect fired heaters used in oil and gas well testing operations to control temperature, improve separation efficiency, and prevent hydrate, wax, and emulsions issues.

1. Equipment Overview

  • Shell: Atmospheric horizontal vessel filled with water/glycol mixture used as a heat medium.
  • Coil (HP Tubes): High-pressure carbon steel coil (multi-pass) carrying process oil & gas.
  • Burner System: Gas or diesel fired system generating heat inside firebox.
  • U-Tube / Firebox: Transfers combustion heat to heating medium safely without mixing with process fluid.

2. Main Function

Indirect fired heaters increase fluid temperature in a controlled way to improve flow assurance and separation.

  • Prevent hydrate formation in gas streams.
  • Reduce wax deposition and improve crude mobility.
  • Break emulsions and improve oil-water separation.
  • Enhance separator efficiency in foamy crude conditions.

3. Installation Summary

  • Place skid on leveled foundation.
  • Install and secure chimney stack.
  • Verify burner assembly tightness.
  • Connect coil inlet/outlet and fuel system.
  • Perform hydrotest (coil) and pneumatic test (instrument lines).

4. Pre-Start Safety Checks

  • Ensure shell is filled with water/glycol above coil level (~3 inches minimum).
  • Verify all valves, flanges, and manifolds are properly tightened.
  • Confirm bypass line is available for pressure protection.
  • Check ESD system and pneumatic shutdown connection.

5. Gas Fired Heater Operation

  • Set fuel gas pressure regulators (high and low pressure stages).
  • Purge firebox before ignition.
  • Ignite pilot flame and confirm stable combustion.
  • Start main burner and adjust air/fuel ratio.
  • Control temperature using thermostat (typically ~180°F operating).
  • Monitor water bath level continuously for efficiency.
  • Ensure smokeless combustion (proper air adjustment).

6. Diesel Fired Heater Operation

  • Check diesel system, burner condition, and return line.
  • Ensure correct electrical supply and grounding.
  • Verify blower rotation direction.
  • Start burner via control panel and ESD system.
  • Adjust fuel/air ratio for clean combustion.
  • Maintain water bath temperature around 180°F.

7. Safety Highlights

  • Never allow coil to overpressure — always keep bypass logic in mind.
  • Ensure ESD system is functional before operation.
  • Prevent dry operation of heater shell.
  • Maintain proper ventilation and chimney flow.
  • Use controlled shutdown procedures for both gas and diesel systems.

8. Key Engineering Concepts

  • Heat transfer through indirect water bath system.
  • Pressure protection via multi-pass coil design.
  • Fuel combustion control (air/fuel ratio optimization).
  • Thermal efficiency monitoring via inlet/outlet temperature.

Final Summary

Indirect fired heaters are critical surface well testing equipment designed to safely heat hydrocarbon fluids using controlled combustion without direct contact between flame and process fluid. Proper installation, pressure testing, and burner control ensure safe operation and high efficiency during well testing operations.

Indirect Fired Heaters – Q&A (Well Testing)

This section covers common questions and answers for understanding indirect fired heaters used in oil & gas well testing operations.

Q1: What is an indirect fired heater?

It is a heating system where combustion heat is transferred to a water/glycol bath, which then heats a high-pressure coil carrying the process fluid without direct flame contact.

Q2: Why do we use indirect fired heaters in well testing?

To safely heat oil and gas streams for improving flow, preventing hydrates, reducing wax, breaking emulsions, and improving separator performance.

Q3: What is inside the heater shell?

A mixture of fresh water and glycol that acts as a heat transfer medium surrounding the coil system.

Q4: What is the function of the coil?

The coil carries high-pressure production fluids and absorbs heat from the surrounding hot water bath.

Q5: Why do we use glycol in the shell?

Glycol prevents freezing in cold environments and improves heat transfer stability.

Q6: What is the purpose of the burner?

The burner generates controlled heat using gas or diesel fuel to heat the water/glycol bath.

Q7: What is the safe operating temperature?

Typically around 180°F, with a high temperature shutdown set below the boiling point of water (212°F).

Q8: What is the role of the bypass line?

It allows fluid to bypass the heater during startup, shutdown, or pressure stabilization to avoid overheating or overpressure.

Q9: What is the purpose of pressure testing?

To ensure the coil and manifold can safely handle design pressure (up to 5K–10K psi depending on specification) without leaks or failure.

Q10: What is the difference between gas-fired and diesel-fired heaters?

Gas-fired heaters use natural gas as fuel, while diesel-fired heaters use liquid diesel. Both perform the same heating function but differ in burner system and fuel handling.

Q11: What happens if water level drops in the shell?

It reduces heat transfer efficiency and may cause overheating and damage to the coil system.

Q12: Why is air/fuel ratio important?

It ensures clean combustion, prevents smoke formation, improves efficiency, and protects burner components.

Q13: What is the function of the ESD system?

Emergency Shutdown (ESD) system automatically shuts down the heater in unsafe conditions such as high temperature, fire risk, or abnormal pressure.

Q14: Why do we purge before ignition?

To remove any accumulated gas from the firebox and prevent explosion during ignition.

Q15: What is the main safety rule during operation?

Never operate the heater without proper water level, functional ESD system, and controlled pressure in the coil.
