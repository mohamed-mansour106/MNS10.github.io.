Orifice Meter and Gas Measurement

Video lesson: Orifice Meter and Gas Measurement

Gas Flow Rate Calculator (Orifice Meter)

This calculator gives the gas rate from an orifice meter chart or recorder reading. It multiplies the meter increment, the combined correction factor, the separator pressure and the square root of the differential pressure.

C = Fb × Fg × Ftf × Fpv × Fr × Y2 × Ftb × Fpb
Qgas = Int.T × C × Pf × √HW

Inputs and units

  • Int.T: meter increment
  • Pf: separator pressure in psia
  • HW: differential pressure (ΔP), it cannot be negative
  • Fb: basic orifice flow factor
  • Fg: specific gravity factor
  • Ftf: flowing temperature factor
  • Fpv: super-compressibility factor
  • Fr: Reynolds number factor
  • Y2: expansion factor
  • Ftb: temperature base factor (default = 1)
  • Fpb: pressure base factor (default = 1)

Worked example

Int.T = 1, Pf = 500 psia, HW = 100, Fb = 1.2 and every other factor = 1 (example inputs chosen for this course, not real meter data).

C = 1.2
Qgas = 1 × 1.2 × 500 × √100 = 6,000
All eleven fields must hold valid numbers. Use your own meter tables for each factor, and enter 1 for a factor that does not apply.

Use the Gas Flow Rate Calculator (Orifice Meter) to check your own numbers.

Beta Ratio Calculator

The beta ratio is the orifice bore diameter divided by the internal pipe diameter. This calculator also gives the area ratio and the velocity of approach factor.

β = d / D
Area ratio = β2
Ev = 1 / √(1 − β4)

Inputs and units

  • d: orifice diameter, in inches or mm
  • D: pipe internal diameter, in inches or mm (units can be mixed)
  • Both must be above zero and d must be smaller than D

Typical range

The page notes that standard flanged orifice meter installations typically target a beta between 0.15 and 0.75 (AGA 3 / API MPMS 14.3). The calculator shows a note if your beta is outside that range.

Worked example

Orifice 2 in in a 4 in pipe.

β = 2 / 4 = 0.5000
β2 = 0.2500
Ev = 1 / √(1 − 0.0625) = 1.0328
Both diameters are converted to inches before dividing, so you can enter d in mm and D in inches.

Use the Beta Ratio Calculator (Orifice Meter) to check your own numbers.

Pressure Drop: Straightening Vanes

This calculator gives the pressure loss across a straightening vane for gas, liquid and steam. Pick the nominal pipe size and it fills in the Cv value.

Gas: ΔP = [2 × SG × (T + 460) / (0.75 × P)] × [Q / (1360 × Cv)]2
Liquid: ΔP = (Q / Cv)2 × SG
Steam: ΔP = (Q / Cv)2 × (0.302 / P)

Inputs and units

  • Nominal pipe size: 2 in (Cv 128), 3 in (304), 4 in (558), 6 in (1325), 8 in (2450), 10 in (3940), 12 in (5850)
  • Line pressure P in psi and line temperature T in °F
  • Gas: flow in SCFH and gas S.G.
  • Liquid: flow in GPM and liquid S.G.
  • Steam: flow in PPH
  • Results: pressure drop in psi

Worked example

A 4 in vane (Cv = 558) at 100 psi and 80°F. Gas 50,000 SCFH with S.G. 0.65, liquid 100 GPM with S.G. 0.85, steam 1,000 PPH (example flows chosen for this course).

Gas ΔP = 0.0406 psi
Liquid ΔP = 0.03 psi
Steam ΔP = 0.01 psi
The code notes that the liquid formula is simplified and that the steam factor 0.302 comes from a spreadsheet ratio. Treat the results as a quick estimate.
