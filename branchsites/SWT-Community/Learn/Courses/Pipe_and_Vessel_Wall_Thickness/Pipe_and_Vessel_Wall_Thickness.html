Pipe and Vessel Wall Thickness

Video lesson: Pipe and Vessel Wall Thickness

Pipe Wall Thickness Calculator

This calculator gives the minimum wall thickness of a pipe from the design pressure, the outside diameter, the allowable stress and the allowances.

tm = P × D / [2 (S × E + P × Y)] + C.A.

Inputs and units

  • Outside diameter D in inches
  • Pressure P in psi
  • Allowable stress S
  • Temperature coefficient Y
  • Weld quality E
  • Corrosion allowance C.A. in inches
  • Results: minimum wall in inches and in mm (× 25.4)

Worked example

D = 4.5 in, P = 1,000 psi, S = 20,000 psi, Y = 0.4, E = 1 and C.A. = 0.05 in (example inputs chosen for this course).

tm = 1,000 × 4.5 / [2 × (20,000 × 1 + 1,000 × 0.4)] + 0.05 = 0.1603 in
In mm: 4.07
The printed formula above is the standard one. The page code groups the denominator a little differently, so the number on the page can differ slightly in the third or fourth decimal (0.1614 in for these inputs).

Pipe ID and Wall Thickness

This is a reference table for nominal pipe size (NPS). It gives the outside diameter, and the wall thickness and inside diameter for Schedule 40 (Standard) and Schedule 80 (Extra Strong).

ID = OD − 2 × twall

Inputs and units

  • Nominal pipe size: 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10 or 12 in
  • Results: OD, wall thickness and ID for Sch 40 and Sch 80

Worked example

A 4 in pipe (OD 4.500 in).

Sch 40: ID = 4.500 − 2 × 0.237 = 4.026 in

A heavier schedule has a thicker wall and a smaller inside diameter for the same outside diameter.

Pressure Vessel Wall Thickness (ASME)

This tool gives the minimum allowable wall thickness of a pressure vessel shell and of a 2:1 elliptical head, per ASME. It is valid for vessels from SA 285 Grade C or ASTM 516 Grade 70 plate.

Shell: t = P × R / (S × E − 0.6 × P) + C.A.
2:1 elliptical head: t = P × D / (2 × S × E − 0.2 × P) + C.A.
Constants: S = 17,500 psi, E = 1, C.A. = 0.125 in

Inputs and units

  • P: pressure in psi
  • R: shell radius in inches (shell), D: diameter in inches (head)
  • Change only the P and R/D columns; the constants are fixed
  • Results: t in inches and in mm

Worked example

P = 250 psi and a 60 in diameter vessel (R = 30 in, D = 60 in).

Shell: t = 250 × 30 / (17,500 − 0.6 × 250) + 0.125 = 0.5573 in (14.15 mm)
Head: t = 250 × 60 / (35,000 − 0.2 × 250) + 0.125 = 0.5542 in (14.08 mm)
The fixed constants are S = 17,500 psi, E = 1 and a corrosion allowance of 0.125 in. For other materials or joint efficiencies, this tool does not apply.
