Pressure Testing and Ratings

Video lesson: Pressure Testing and Ratings

Hydrotest Pressure Calculator

This calculator gives the hydrostatic test pressure from the working pressure with the standard field rule of 1.5 times the working pressure.

Ptest = Pworking × 1.5
1 bar ≈ 14.5038 psi, 1 bar = 100 kPa

Inputs and units

  • Working pressure in psig, bar or kPa (zero or greater)
  • Test factor multiplier (1.5 is the standard field factor, it must be above zero)
  • Results: test pressure in psig, bar and kPa

Worked example

Working pressure 1,000 psig with the standard factor.

Ptest = 1,000 × 1.5 = 1,500 psig
In bar: 103.4
Always verify the equipment MAWP, the lowest-rated downstream component, temperature derating and site HSE permits before pressurizing any line or skid.

Flanged Equipment Working Pressure

This is a quick field estimate of the approximate maximum allowable working pressure (MAWP) of flanged equipment such as a separator, from the ANSI flange class.

Low pressure (class 150 or lower): WP = Class × 1.9
Medium pressure (class above 150): WP = Class × 2.4

Inputs and units

  • Flange class: 150, 300, 600 or 900, or a custom class number above zero
  • Results: working pressure in psig, bar and kPa

Worked example

Classes 150, 300 and 600.

Class 150: 150 × 1.9 = 285 psig
Class 300: 300 × 2.4 = 720 psig
Class 600: 600 × 2.4 = 1,440 psig
The page calls these multipliers rapid field rules of thumb. Real ratings depend on the material group, gasket type, temperature and the vessel nameplate (ASME B16.5 / B16.34).

Flange Bolt Torque

This calculator gives the torque needed on a flange stud from the stud diameter, the threads per inch, the bolt stress and the friction coefficient.

As = (π/4) × [D − 0.9743 × P]², with P = 1 / N
F = Stress × As
T = F E (P + π f E S) / [2 (π E − P f S)] + F f (H + D + K) / 4
E = D − 0.649519 P, S = 1.1547, H = 1.5 D + 0.125, K = 0.125
Torque in ft-lbf = T (in-lbf) / 12

Inputs and units

  • Stud diameter D in inches
  • Threads per inch N
  • Bolt stress in psi
  • Friction coefficient f: 0.07 (lubricated) or 0.13 (dry / zinc)
  • Results: tension force F in lbf and required torque in ft-lbf

Worked example

A 1 in stud with 8 threads per inch at 52,500 psi, dry / zinc (f = 0.13).

As = 0.6057 in²
F = 52,500 × 0.6057 = 31,802 lbf
T = 474 ft-lbf
With a lubricated stud (f = 0.07): 279 ft-lbf
Lubricated studs need much less torque for the same bolt stress, so always use the friction coefficient that matches the stud condition.

Full ANSI and API Flange Database

This lookup gives the connection hardware for a flange: number of studs, stud diameter, stud length for RTJ, ring gasket and spanner size.

No formula. The page looks the values up from its table by rating and nominal pipe size.

Inputs and units

  • Service rating: ANSI 150# (275 psi), ANSI 300# (720 psi), ANSI 600# (1440 psi), API 5000 (5K) or API 10000 (10K)
  • Nominal pipe size (NPS)
  • Results: number of studs, stud diameter, stud length (RTJ), ring gasket and spanner size in mm
