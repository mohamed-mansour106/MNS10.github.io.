Pressure Transmitter Basics

Video lesson: Pressure Transmitter Basics

What a Pressure Transmitter Does

A pressure transmitter senses pressure and sends it as an electrical signal to a control or display system.

  • A sensor in the transmitter reacts to the pressure applied.
  • The electronics turn the reading into a standard signal, often 4 to 20 mA.
  • The signal goes to a controller, display, recorder or data system.
  • Transmitters are used for line pressure, separator pressure, tank level (by pressure) and flow (by differential pressure).
  • A gauge shows the pressure at the spot. A transmitter sends it away.
The sensor feels the pressure, the electronics send the number.

Types of Pressure Measured

The same pressure can be read against different references.

  • Gauge pressure is measured against the surrounding atmosphere. Open to air it reads zero.
  • Absolute pressure is measured against a perfect vacuum.
  • Differential pressure is the difference between two points, for example across an orifice plate or a filter.
  • Absolute pressure is about gauge pressure plus atmospheric pressure.
  • Always check which type the instrument reads and which unit is shown: psi, bar or kPa.
  • Vacuum is pressure below atmospheric.
Before you read a number, ask: gauge, absolute or differential?

The 4 to 20 mA Signal, Zero and Span

Most transmitters use a current signal that is easy to send over a long cable.

  • The lowest value of the range gives 4 mA and the highest value gives 20 mA.
  • The zero is the pressure that gives 4 mA, and the span is the range from 4 to 20 mA.
  • The signal is half way (12 mA) at the middle of the range.
  • A signal of 0 mA or well below 4 mA usually points to a broken wire or a failure, not to zero pressure.
  • Example: a range of 0 to 100 bar. At 25 bar the signal is 4 + 0.25 x 16 = 8 mA.
  • Ranges are set and checked by qualified instrument personnel.
Because 4 mA means zero pressure, a dead signal is easy to tell from a real zero.

Good Practice and Simple Checks

A transmitter is only useful if it is installed and checked well.

  • Use a transmitter with a range that fits the real pressure, and not much larger, so the reading is accurate.
  • Keep the impulse line or connection clean, not blocked and not leaking.
  • Watch for zero drift: a reading that is off when there is no pressure.
  • Compare with a local gauge when it is safe to do so.
  • Calibrate on schedule against a reference, using the approved procedure.
  • Never exceed the overpressure limit of the instrument.
  • Isolate and depressurize only by following your company and site procedures.
A transmitter that reads well at zero and at a known pressure can be trusted.
