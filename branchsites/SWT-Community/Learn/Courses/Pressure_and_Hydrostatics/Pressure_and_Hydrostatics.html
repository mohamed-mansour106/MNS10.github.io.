Pressure and Hydrostatics

Video lesson: Pressure and Hydrostatics

Pressure Law Calculator (P = F / A)

Pressure is the force acting on an area. This calculator gives the pressure in psi, bar and kPa.

P = F / A
1 bar ≈ 14.5038 psi
1 Pa = 1 N/m², 1 kPa = 1000 Pa
Circular area: A = (π / 4) × d²

Inputs and units

  • Force F in lbf, N or kN
  • Area A in in², m² or cm² (above zero)
  • Results: pressure in psi, bar and kPa

Worked example

A force of 500 lbf on an area of 2 in² (example inputs chosen for this course).

P = 500 / 2 = 250.0 psi
In bar: 17.24
In kPa: 1,723.7
Force and area are converted to consistent units before dividing. For a piston, seal or pipe bore of diameter d, use A = π/4 × d².

Hydrostatic Pressure Calculator

This calculator gives the hydrostatic pressure at the bottom of a fluid column from the fluid density (S.G.) and the true vertical depth.

Phyd = TVD × (SG × 0.4333)
0.4333 = pressure gradient of water in psi/ft

Inputs and units

  • Fluid density as S.G. (relative to water)
  • True vertical depth TVD in ft
  • Results: hydrostatic pressure in psi and pressure gradient in psi/ft

Worked example

S.G. 0.85 and TVD 10,000 ft.

Gradient = 0.85 × 0.4333 = 0.36830 psi/ft
Phyd = 10,000 × 0.36830 = 3,683.05 psi
The page has an option to sync the S.G. with its density converter.

Gas Gradient and Static BHP

This calculator gives the static bottom hole pressure (Pws) of a gas well and the gas gradient. It has an exponential method and a simplified rule of thumb.

Gas gradient G = 0.01875 × γg × Pwh / (Zavg × Tavg)
Exponential: Pws = Pwh × e(0.01875 × γg × D / (Zavg × Tavg))
Simplified: Pws = Pwh + 0.25 × (Pwh/100) × (D/100)

Inputs and units

  • Gas gravity (S.G.)
  • Wellhead pressure in psia
  • Depth D in ft
  • Average temperature in Rankine (°R = °F + 460)
  • Average Z-factor

Worked example

Gas S.G. 0.7, wellhead pressure 1,000 psia, depth 8,000 ft, average temperature 600°R and Z = 0.9.

Gas gradient = 0.0243 psi/ft
Exponent = 0.19444
Pws exponential = 1,214.6 psia
Pws simplified = 1,200.0 psia
The exponential method needs S.G., temperature and Z. The simplified method is a field approximation for when gas properties are unknown.

Downhole Pressure Calculators

This page has three bottom hole pressure calculators (gas, fluid in ppg, fluid in pcf) and a density converter.

Gas BHP = Pwh × e(0.01875 × SG × Depth / (Z × T))
Fluid BHP (ppg) = Mud weight × 0.052 × Depth
Fluid BHP (pcf) = Mud weight × Depth / 144
ppg to pcf: × 7.48052. Gradient (psi/ft) = ppg × 0.052 = pcf / 144

Inputs and units

  • Gas: depth, Z average, wellhead pressure in psia, gas S.G. (0.7 default), temperature in Rankine
  • Fluid ppg: mud weight in lbs/gal and depth in ft
  • Fluid pcf: mud weight in pcf and depth in ft

Worked example

Mud weight 10 ppg at 10,000 ft.

BHP = 10 × 0.052 × 10,000 = 5,200
10 ppg = 10 × 7.48052 = 74.81 pcf
From pcf: 74.81 × 10,000 / 144 = 5,195
The two fluid methods differ slightly because the page uses 7.48052 pcf per ppg and a fixed 0.052.

Drawdown Pressure Calculator

Drawdown is the difference between reservoir pressure and bottomhole flowing pressure. The calculator can also show the drops to the wellhead.

Drawdown = Pr − Pwf
Total drop = Pr − Pwh
String (tubing) drop = Pwf − Pwh

Inputs and units

  • Reservoir pressure Pr in psi, bar or kPa
  • Bottomhole flowing pressure Pwf, same unit
  • Wellhead pressure Pwh (optional)
  • All pressures must be zero or greater

Worked example

Pr = 4,000 psi, Pwf = 3,200 psi and Pwh = 800 psi.

Drawdown = 4,000 − 3,200 = 800 psi
Total drop = 4,000 − 800 = 3,200 psi
String drop = 3,200 − 800 = 2,400 psi
Keep all pressures on the same basis, all absolute or all gauge. If Pwf is above Pr, the drawdown is negative (injection or pressure build-up).
