Separator and Vessel Sizing

Video lesson: Separator and Vessel Sizing

Horizontal Vessel Sizing

This calculator finds the vessel length needed for a target liquid retention time, and checks if the gas velocity is inside the allowable limit.

Liquid: L = Qbpd × tmin / (1440 × Aliquid), with volume converted using 5.61458 ft³/bbl
Aliquid = (π r²) × operating level %
Gas (Souders-Brown): Vmax = K × √[(ρL − ρg) / ρg], with K = 0.35
ρg = 2.7 × SG × (P + 14.7) / 520, ρL = 52 lb/ft³
Actual gas velocity = gas flow in ft³/s / (half of the vessel cross-section)

Inputs and units

  • Target retention time in min
  • Design flowrate in bpd
  • Vessel diameter in inches
  • Operating level in %
  • Gas flow in MMSCFD, operating pressure in psig and gas S.G.
  • Results: seam-to-seam length in feet (cylindrical volume, heads excluded), gas velocity in ft/s and a SAFE or OVERLOAD status

Worked example

3 min retention, 5,000 bpd, a 48 in vessel at 50% level; gas 5 MMSCFD at 500 psig with S.G. 0.65 (example inputs chosen for this course).

Required volume = 5,000 / 1440 × 3 = 10.42 bbl = 58.5 ft³
Aliquid = 12.566 × 50% = 6.283 ft²
L = 58.5 / 6.283 = 9.31 ft
ρg = 1.737 lb/ft³, Vmax = 1.88 ft/s
Actual gas velocity = 0.26 ft/s, so the status is SAFE
The K-factor 0.35 is the value the page gives for a horizontal separator with a mist extractor. The page keeps the gas in the upper half of the vessel for the velocity check. Low gas velocity avoids carry-over of oil droplets, and long enough liquid residence lets gas bubbles break out.

Retention Time Calculator

This calculator estimates how long the liquid stays in a separator from its capacity, the fluid level and the flow rate.

tmin = (Vbbls × Level%) / Qbpd × 1440
V × Level is the actual liquid volume in the vessel

Inputs and units

  • Separator capacity in bbls
  • Fluid level in %
  • Flowrate in bpd (above zero)
  • Result: retention time as mm:ss, with a status badge

Separation efficiency guide on the page

StatusTime (min)Effect
Optimalabove 4.0Excellent gas-oil separation
Good2.0 to 4.0Standard operating range
Low1.0 to 2.0Risk of liquid carry-over in gas line
Criticalbelow 1.0Poor separation / possible emulsion issues

Worked example

A 100 bbl separator at 50% level with 5,000 bpd.

Liquid volume = 100 × 0.50 = 50 bbl
t = 50 / 5,000 × 1440 = 14.4 min, which is 14:24
Status: Optimal (above 4 min)
More flow or a lower level means less retention time, which risks carry-over.

Desander Velocity Estimation

This calculator estimates the gas flow velocity from the wellhead pressure and gas rate, and the matching desander velocity.

Vflow = 32000 × qg / (16 × Pwhp)
Desander velocity = 3 × Vflow
P (kPa) = P (psia) × 6.89476

Inputs and units

  • WHP in psia (above zero)
  • Gas rate in MMscf/day
  • Results: flow velocity in ft/s, desander velocity in ft/s and WHP in kPa

Worked example

WHP 1,000 psia and a gas rate of 5 MMscf/day.

Vflow = 32,000 × 5 / (16 × 1,000) = 10.00 ft/s
Desander velocity = 3 × 10.00 = 30.00 ft/s
WHP = 1,000 × 6.89476 = 6,895 kPa
Velocity falls as the pressure goes up, and rises with the gas rate.
