Shrinkage Factor and Stock Tank Oil

Video lesson: Shrinkage Factor and Stock Tank Oil

Estimate of Shrinkage Factor

Oil measured in the separator contains dissolved gas. When it goes to the stock tank at 60°F, part of the volume leaves as gas, so the oil shrinks. This calculator estimates the shrinkage factor from separator conditions with an empirical model.

γg,adj = γg,obs + [0.00069 − 0.0003725 × γg,obs] × (Tobs − 60)
F1 = (1.797 / γg,adj − 1.838) × Psep
F2 = APIobs × (0.02 − 3.57×10−6 × F1) + 0.25
Fvol = F1 × √(F2 / γg,adj) + 1.25 × Tsep
Bo = 0.9759 + 12×10−5 × Fvol1.2
Shrinkage = 1 / Bo

Inputs and units

  • Separator pressure in psig
  • Separator temperature in °F
  • Observed gas S.G.
  • Observed oil API
  • Observed temperature in °F
  • Result: (1 − Shr) at 60°F, shown as a factor with 4 decimals

Worked example

Separator at 100 psig and 90°F, gas S.G. 0.80, oil 38 API, observed temperature 80°F (example inputs chosen for this course).

γg,adj = 0.80784
F1 = 38.65
F2 = 1.005
Fvol = 155.60
Bo = 1.0271
Shrinkage = 1 / 1.0271 = 0.9736
The model first adjusts the observed gas gravity for temperature, then calculates the liquid volume correction factor Fvol.

Shrinkage Factor Estimation (Katz Chart)

This calculator reads the shrinkage factor at 60°F from a Katz chart table (source noted on the page: Halliburton). It interpolates between the table values for your separator pressure and oil gravity. It also gives a net production summary.

Shrinkage = bilinear interpolation of the Katz table at (Psep, °API)
Qnet = Qgross × (1 − BS&W) × Shrk
Water rate = Qgross × BS&W

Inputs and units

  • Separator pressure in psig (table covers 20 to 1350 psig)
  • Oil gravity in °API (table covers 10 to 60 °API)
  • Measured liquid in BBL/D
  • BS&W in %
  • Results: shrinkage factor, net oil rate in STB/D and water rate in BBL/D

Katz table values (a few points from the page)

Sep. pressure (psig)30 °API40 °API50 °API
1000.9840.9760.968
5000.9340.9120.890
10000.8980.8660.830

Worked example

Separator at 100 psig, oil 40 API, measured liquid 1,000 BBL/D and BS&W 10%.

Shrinkage from the table = 0.976
Water rate = 1,000 × 0.10 = 100 BBL/D
Net oil = 1,000 × (1 − 0.10) × 0.976 = 878.4 STB/D
Shrinkage gets lower (more shrinkage) as separator pressure and oil API go up. The calculator is only valid inside the table range.

Stock Tank Oil from Net Oil and Shrinkage

This calculator applies the shrinkage factor at 60°F to the net oil rate. The result is the Stock Tank Oil (STO) rate.

STO = Net Oil × Shrinkage@60°F
Loss = Net Oil − STO
Volume factor = 1 / Shrinkage@60°F

Inputs and units

  • Net oil rate in bbl/d (or separator bbl/d) or m³/d
  • Shrinkage@60°F as a decimal factor (for example 0.92) or as a percentage (for example 92)
  • Both values must be zero or greater, and the shrinkage must be above zero

Worked example

Net oil 850 bbl/d and shrinkage 0.92.

STO = 850 × 0.92 = 782.0
Loss = 850 − 782.0 = 68.0
Volume factor = 1 / 0.92 = 1.0870
If your lab or PVT sheet gives the shrinkage as a percentage such as 92%, switch the format selector to Percentage so the calculator divides by 100.

Use the Stock Tank Oil (STO) from Net Oil & Shrinkage@60°F Calculator to check your own numbers.

Oil Rate with BS&W and Shrinkage Correction

This calculator gives a corrected oil production rate from meter readings. It has two methods: BS&W correction and oil shrinkage correction.

Method 1: QOil = KF × MF × INC × Int.T × (1 − BS&W/100)
Method 2: QLiquid = Int.T × MK × Oil Shrinkage × MeterIntOil × KF

Inputs and units

  • KF: volume correction factor
  • MF: meter factor
  • INC: increment multiplier
  • Int.T: meter total increment
  • BS&W in % (must be between 0 and 100)
  • MK: meter constant or multiplier
  • Oil Shrinkage: shrinkage correction factor
  • Meter Int Oil: oil meter increment

Worked example

Method 1 with KF = 0.99, MF = 1.00, INC = 1, Int.T = 500 and BS&W = 10% (example numbers chosen for this course).

QOil = 0.99 × 1.00 × 1 × 500 × (1 − 0.10) = 445.5
Method 1 removes the water with BS&W. Method 2 multiplies in the shrinkage factor instead, so use the method your test procedure asks for.
