Steam Exchanger

Video lesson: Steam Exchanger

What a Steam Exchanger Does

A steam exchanger heats the well fluid with steam. The fluid and the steam never mix. Heat passes through the metal wall between them.

  • Why heat the fluid? To avoid hydrates and freezing after the choke, to lower the viscosity of heavy oil, and to help the separator split oil and water.
  • Steam comes from a boiler or a steam generator on location.
  • The well fluid is on the high-pressure side. The steam is on the low-pressure side.
  • A steam exchanger is different from an indirect fired heater: there is no water bath. Steam heats the tubes directly.
  • The most common design is shell and tube.
Keep in mind the simple picture: hot steam on one side of the wall, cold fluid on the other, heat goes through the metal.

Main Parts

A shell and tube steam exchanger has a few main parts.

  • Tube bundle: strong tubes that carry the well fluid at high pressure.
  • Shell: the outer vessel that holds the steam around the tubes.
  • Steam inlet and condensate outlet: steam goes in, and as it gives up heat it turns back into water, called condensate, which leaves through a steam trap.
  • Steam trap: lets condensate out but keeps the steam in.
  • Valves and gauges: temperature and pressure gauges on both sides, a steam control valve, and isolation and drain valves.
  • Safety devices: a relief valve on the shell, and a thermal relief for the tubes where the procedure requires it.
A steam trap that is stuck open wastes steam. One that is stuck closed fills the shell with water and the heating drops.

Starting, Running and Stopping

Follow the company procedure. These are the common steps and ideas.

Start-up

  • Check the unit, lines and valves. All drains and vents are in the right position.
  • Start the well fluid flow through the tubes first, before steam.
  • Open the steam slowly, to avoid thermal shock and water hammer. Drain condensate first.
  • Watch the outlet temperature and the pressure on the shell.

Running

  • Keep the outlet temperature steady with the steam control valve.
  • Check the steam trap and look for leaks.

Shutdown

  • Close the steam first, then stop the well fluid flow when the procedure allows.
  • Drain the shell and the tubes if freezing is possible.
Fluid first, steam second on start-up. Steam off first on shutdown.

Safety and Common Problems

Steam and high-pressure fluid together need care. Always follow your company procedure and site rules.

  • Burns: steam lines and the shell are very hot. Keep away and use guards and insulation.
  • Water hammer: trapped condensate pushed by steam can hit with great force. Open steam slowly.
  • Tube leak: if well fluid leaks into the steam side, you may see hydrocarbons in the condensate. Stop and report.
  • Over-pressure: never block the relief valve. Never close the outlet of the steam shell.
  • Hydrates at low temperature: check the outlet temperature is high enough.
  • Scale and fouling on the tubes lowers heat transfer over time.
Hydrocarbons in the condensate mean a tube leak. This is a stop-and-report situation.
