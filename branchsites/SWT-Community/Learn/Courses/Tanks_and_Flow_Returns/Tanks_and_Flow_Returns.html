Tank Volume and Loading Estimator: Rectangular Tank

This calculator gives the volume of a rectangular tank, the volume at a fluid level, the weight of the fluid and the deck loading.

Vbbl = L × W × H / 9702 (9702 in³ per barrel)
Vgal = Vbbl × 42
Wtons = Vgal × PPG / 2240
Load (lb/ft²) = Wtotal × 2240 / [(L × W) / 144]

Inputs and units

  • Height, length and width of the tank in inches
  • Height of the fluid level in inches
  • Weight of the fluid in ppg
  • Tare weight of the tank in tons
  • Results: total volume and volume at level in bbls and gallons, max fluid weight, weight of tank plus fluid in tons and max deck loading

Worked example

A tank 72 in high, 240 in long and 96 in wide, a fluid level of 36 in, 8.33 ppg fluid and a 5 ton tare (example dimensions chosen for this course).

Total volume = 72 × 240 × 96 / 9702 = 170.98 bbl (7,181.3 gal)
At 36 in: 36 × 240 × 96 / 9702 = 85.49 bbl
Fluid weight = 8.33 × 7,181.3 / 2240 = 26.71 tons (the page uses the total gallons here)
Tank plus fluid = 31.71 tons, deck load = 444 lb/ft²
The weight is a long ton of 2,240 lb. The page labels the fluid weight as "Max fluid weight", so it is the weight with the tank full.

Horizontal Cylindrical Tank

The second part of the same page gives the volume of a horizontal cylindrical tank, in total and at a partial fill.

Vtotal = π × r² × L / 5.61458, with r in feet
Fluid area A = r² × cos−1[(r − h) / r] − (r − h) × √(2 r h − h²)
Fill fraction = A / (π r²), fluid volume = fraction × Vtotal

Inputs and units

  • Diameter of the vessel in inches
  • Length of the vessel in feet
  • Height of the fluid level in inches
  • Results: total volume and fluid volume in bbls and gallons

Worked example

A 96 in diameter vessel, 20 ft long, filled to 48 in (half the diameter).

Vtotal = π × 4² × 20 / 5.61458 = 179.05 bbl
Fill fraction at h = r = 0.500
Fluid volume = 89.5 bbl (the page rounds it to 1 decimal)
Use the same units for the fluid height and the diameter in the fill fraction. At half the diameter the fill fraction is 50%.

Estimated Flow Returns (Tank Readings)

This calculator turns two tank readings and the time between them into a flow rate in bpd and bph.

ΔV = (Hcm − Vinit) × Ktank
Rate (bpd) = ΔV / Δtmin × 1440
Rate (bph) = Rate (bpd) / 24

Inputs and units

  • Initial reading
  • Final height in cm
  • Start time and end time (if the end is earlier than the start, it counts as the next day)
  • Tank calibration constant, 1 cm = K bbls (0.4348 if left empty)

Worked example

Initial reading 10, final height 50 cm, K = 0.4348 bbl/cm, from 10:00 to 11:00, calculated with the printed formula (example readings chosen for this course).

ΔV = (50 − 10) × 0.4348 = 17.39 bbl
Rate = 17.39 / 60 × 1440 = 417.4 bpd
Rate in bph = 17.39
The page code applies the initial reading in a slightly different way than the printed formula, so check the number on the page against your own tank sheet.
