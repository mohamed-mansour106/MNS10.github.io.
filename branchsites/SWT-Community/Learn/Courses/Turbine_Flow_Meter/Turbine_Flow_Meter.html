Turbine Flow Meter

Video lesson: Turbine Flow Meter

How a Turbine Meter Works

A turbine meter measures liquid flow with a small rotor that spins in the stream.

  • The liquid flows through the meter body and turns the rotor with blades.
  • The faster the liquid flows, the faster the rotor spins.
  • A pickup coil outside the body senses each blade passing and gives an electric pulse.
  • A counter or flow computer counts the pulses. Pulses per unit volume is called the K-factor.
  • The volume is the number of pulses divided by the K-factor. The rate is the pulses per second divided by the K-factor.
Count the pulses, divide by the K-factor, and you have the volume.

What the Meter Needs to Read Well

A turbine meter is accurate when the conditions are right.

  • Clean liquid: solids and debris damage the blades and the bearings. Use a strainer upstream.
  • Single phase: gas bubbles or slugs make the reading wrong and can over-speed the rotor.
  • Straight pipe before and after the meter, as the manufacturer says, and a flow conditioner if needed.
  • A flow rate inside the meter range. Too low and the rotor sticks; too high and it over-speeds.
  • Viscosity changes the reading at low flow. Calibrate with a fluid like the one measured.
Gas in a liquid line is the turbine meter enemy. Keep the line full and free of gas.

Protecting the Meter

A turbine has moving parts, so it can be damaged more easily than a meter without them.

  • Open valves slowly to avoid a sudden surge that over-speeds the rotor.
  • Do not blow gas or air through the meter, for example when purging a line.
  • Never exceed the maximum flow rate or pressure on the meter label.
  • Use a bypass when you must flush the line with a high rate.
  • Listen and look: a rough sound, an unstable pulse reading or a reading that drifts can mean worn bearings.
  • Keep the pickup coil and cable clean, dry and connected well.
Most turbine meters are damaged by one careless purge or one sudden surge.

Calibration, Meter Factor and Records

No meter reads perfectly. The meter factor corrects it.

  • A meter is calibrated against a reference, for example a prover or a gauge tank, at the working flow rate.
  • The meter factor is the reference volume divided by the meter volume. It is applied to the reading.
  • Calibrate again after repairs, after a change of liquid, and at the planned interval.
  • A meter factor that changes a lot from test to test means a meter problem.
  • Record pulses, K-factor, meter factor, flow rate, temperature and pressure, with the time and the meter number.
A meter factor is only as good as the calibration conditions. Calibrate at the rate and the fluid you will measure.
