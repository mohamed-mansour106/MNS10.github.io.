Valve Types: Gate, Needle, Ball and Plug

Video lesson: Valve Types: Gate, Needle, Ball and Plug

What a Valve Does and How to Choose One

A valve is a device that starts, stops, directs or limits the flow of a fluid in a pipe. On a surface well test site the same pipe can carry oil, gas, water and sand at high pressure, so the right valve in the right place matters for both safety and for good data.

The main jobs of a valve

  • Shut-off: fully open or fully closed, nothing in between (on/off).
  • Throttling: partly open to control the flow rate or pressure.
  • One-way flow: let fluid go in one direction only (check valves).
  • Protection: open by themselves at a set pressure (relief valves).

What decides the choice

  • Pressure rating, such as 5,000, 10,000 or 15,000 psi, and the temperature range.
  • Fluid and service, for example sour (H2S) service needs suitable materials.
  • Size and end connections.
  • Is it for on/off use or for flow control?
  • How the seal is made, metal-to-metal or soft seat.
A valve is rated for what it was designed to do. Using a shut-off valve for throttling is the most common way to damage one.

Gate Valves

A gate valve has a flat or wedge-shaped gate that slides across the flow path when you turn the handwheel. When the gate is lifted fully out of the way, the pipe is open with a straight path and very little pressure loss.

Where it is used

  • On/off shut-off service, for example on flowheads, manifolds and the lines of a test package.
  • High pressure lines, with common ratings of 5,000, 10,000, 15,000 and even 20,000 psi.

Seals

  • High pressure gate valves normally seal metal to metal between the gate and the seat.
  • Some designs have grease fittings, so sealant can be injected under pressure to help the seal. Grease points are used only as the maker describes.

Good to know

  • It takes many turns to move from open to closed, so it closes slowly.
  • A partly open gate sits in the flow. The flow can damage the gate and the seat. Do not use it to throttle.
  • The stem may rise out of the body as you open it (rising stem), which shows the position.
Gate valve: fully open or fully closed. Never leave it half open.

Needle Valves

A needle valve has a long, tapered stem that goes into a small seat. Because the stem moves a tiny distance for each turn, you can set a small flow very accurately.

Where it is used

  • Small lines for sampling and bleed-off points.
  • Isolating pressure gauges and other instruments.
  • Fine control of a small flow, for example chemical injection.

Good to know

  • The bore is small, so it is not for big flow rates.
  • It can be used for shut-off and for flow control, which makes it different from a gate valve.
  • Open it slowly. A fast opening sends a pressure shock to the gauge or instrument behind it.
  • Use clean fluid where you can, since dirt and sand can cut the tip and the seat.
When a needle valve feeds an instrument, open it slowly and close it again when the work is done.

Ball and Plug Valves

Ball and plug valves are quarter-turn valves. A quarter turn of the handle moves the valve from fully open to fully closed, which is fast.

Ball valve

  • Inside is a ball with a hole through it. When the hole lines up with the pipe, flow passes. A quarter turn puts the solid side of the ball across the pipe.
  • Often used at medium pressure, and for sampling and bleed-off points.
  • Seals are normally soft seats such as PTFE (Teflon), which limits the temperature and pressure the valve can take, and which can be damaged by sand.

Plug valve

  • Inside is a cylinder or a slightly tapered plug with a passage through it. A quarter turn also opens or closes it.
  • The seat is often a soft material, and some plug valves use grease or sealant to help seal and to ease the turning.

Good to know

  • The handle normally lies along the pipe when open and across the pipe when closed. Check the marking on your valve.
  • They are for shut-off. Throttling with a ball or plug valve wears the seat and can leak.
The handle position is the quick check: in line with the pipe is open, across the pipe is closed (check the maker marking too).
