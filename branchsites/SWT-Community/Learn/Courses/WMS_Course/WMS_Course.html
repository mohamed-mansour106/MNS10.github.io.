Work Management System

ADNOC Work Management System (WMS) Framework

The ADNOC Work Management System (WMS) Framework is a comprehensive safety tool designed to ensure that all operational work across ADNOC facilities is properly controlled, coordinated, and communicated. It provides a standardized approach to formal risk assessment, isolation, permitting, and job execution.

1. Key Objectives & Scope

The WMS Framework establishes unified governance across all ADNOC operating units:

  • Single System Procedure: One standardized set of rules for all ADNOC Group Companies.
  • Harmonized Terminology: Common definitions and roles across the entire workforce.
  • Workforce Mobility: Allows contractors and personnel to move between sites without system limitations.
  • Universal Scope: Applies strictly to all employees, contractors, operating sites, and interface areas.
The WMS Framework applies to all activities and services across ADNOC Group Companies without exception.

2. The 5 Core Elements of WMS

The Work Management System integrates five essential safety components into a unified workflow:

  • Permit to Work (PTW): Standardized authorization for high-risk work execution.
  • Job Safety Analysis (JSA): Formal step-by-step risk assessment and control identification.
  • Energy Isolation (EI): Safe isolation of hazardous mechanical, electrical, or chemical energy sources.
  • Temporary Defeat (TD): Strict authorization required when bypassing safety-critical systems or alarms.
  • Simultaneous Operations (SIMOPS): Risk management and coordination of concurrent work activities.

3. The 8-Step WMS Process Lifecycle

Work execution under the WMS framework follows a strict sequential 8-step lifecycle:

  1. Work Request & Planning: Initial job submission and operational review.
  2. Identify & Approve Controls: Defining mandatory safety precautions and JSA.
  3. Worksite Controls Implementation: Establishing isolations, barriers, and environmental controls.
  4. Authorize Work Permit: Formal approval by designated Area Authorities.
  5. Communicate Controls & Commence Work: Pre-job toolbox talk and work kickoff.
  6. Dynamic Risk Assessment: On-site continuous safety assessment during work execution.
  7. Work Handover / Suspension / Extension: Managing shifts, delays, and ongoing authorizations.
  8. Permit Closure & Handback: Restoring site to normal operational status and closing documentation.
Continuous Assurance: All work steps rely on active monitoring, clear sign-offs, and compliance checks at every tier of authority.

4. Governance, Roles & Responsibilities

Clear lines of responsibility are defined to ensure operational integrity and compliance:

  • ADNOC Group HSE, SVP: Reviews and approves WMS Standards, revisions, and Tier 3 audit overviews.
  • Group Company CEO: Authorizes and appoints Asset Owners across site facilities.
  • Group Company VP-HSE: Provides technical advice, conducts Tier 2 audits, and endorses deviation requests.
  • Asset Owner / VPO: Appoints authorized signatories (AAA, AA) in writing and ensures WMS compliance.
  • Area Authority (AA): Manages daily permit authorization, active limits, and daily PTW coordination meetings.
  • Job Performer (JP): Retains original permit on site and directly supervises safe work execution.

Permit To Work (PTW) System Framework

The Permit To Work (PTW) system is a formal safety framework designed to ensure that maintenance, construction, and operational tasks are executed safely to minimize risks to personnel, assets, and the environment.

1. Core Concept & Objectives

A PTW is a set of official documents used to maintain safe working conditions for a specific task. It provides clear operational boundaries prior to commencing work.

The primary objectives of the PTW framework include:

  • Clearly describing the work scope and boundaries.
  • Ensuring proper authorization is in place before execution.
  • Identifying hazards and verifying implemented controls.
  • Acting as a written agreement between involved parties.
  • Maintaining a permanent saved record of executed work.

A printed hard copy of the active permit must be displayed at the worksite at all times during task execution.

2. Types of PTW Forms

Permits are categorized based on risk and task nature. A Work Categorization Chart (WCC) is used to determine the correct permit type.

  • Hot Work Permit: Required for tasks containing a positive source of ignition or potential spark (e.g., naked flames, hot surfaces, grinding).
  • Critical Work Permit: Required for high-risk activities listed in the Work Categorization Chart or identified as Critical/High Risk.
  • General Work Permit: Used for non-critical activities that do not contain any hot work.
Exempt Work Criteria: Routine + Low Risk + Single Discipline + Completed in One Shift + No Operational Impact → No Permit Needed

Key responsibilities within the WMS include:

  • Asset Approval Authority (AAA): Approves Critical and Hot Work permits, ensuring risks are reduced to ALARP.
  • Area Authority (AA): Manages area safety, authorizes permits, and oversees re-validations, suspensions, and closures.
  • Permit Issuer (PI): Inspects worksite, verifies control measures, confirms zero energy state, and conducts gas tests.
  • Performing Authority (PA): Requests the permit, provides clear scope, and ensures complete JSA coverage.
  • Job Performer (JP): Directly supervises work execution, conducts Toolbox Talks (TBT), and maintains site safety controls.
Mandatory Separation of Roles: The Area Authority (AA) and Job Performer (JP) shall NOT be the same person. Similarly, the Permit Issuer (PI) and Job Performer (JP) shall NOT be the same person.

PTW Supporting Certificates & Governance Rules

Certificates are supporting, complementary documents attached to Permits to Work (PTWs) to authorize specific preparatory or high-risk activities. Certificates do not authorize work on their own and must be paired with an active permit.

1. The 10 Types of PTW Certificates

The WMS Framework defines ten specialized certificates to manage specific high-hazard activities:

  • Confined Space Entry: Controls entry precautions, atmospheric monitoring, gas testing, and rescue team readiness.
  • Energy Isolation: Regulates process, mechanical, electrical, and instrument isolations, including Sanction to Test (STT).
  • Temporary Defeat: Manages the temporary override or bypass of safety systems, alarms, or interlocks.
  • Excavation: Verifies underground services (electrical, telecom, piping) and structural shoring before ground disturbance.
  • Ionizing Radiation: Sets controlled boundary zones, exposure limits, and safety measures for radiography work.
  • Road Closure: Manages traffic re-routing, barrier placement, and emergency access notifications.
  • Diving: Oversees underwater operations, diving crew safety, and marine interface communications.
  • Vehicle Entry: Regulates unauthorized or motorized equipment entry into process or restricted areas.
  • Atmospheric Test Record Form: Logs mandatory gas test parameters (Oxygen, LEL, H2S, CO) before and during work.
  • Easement: Handles third-party or cross-boundary work authorizations and operational notifications.
Certificates are complementary to PTWs and do not, in themselves, authorize work to be carried out without an active PTW.

Energy Isolation (EI) & Lockout / Tagout (LOTO) Framework

The Energy Isolation (EI) Standard establishes a mandatory, structured process across all operational facilities to ensure a secure break in energy supplies. Isolation controls guarantee that equipment cannot be inadvertently re-energized or pressurized while work is underway, reducing operational risk to ALARP.

1. Key Roles & Responsibilities

Strict segregation of duties prevents procedural overlaps and ensures clear accountability across isolation activities.

  • Area Authority (AA): Approves all isolation points after technical consultation and retains primary responsibility for confirming full implementation prior to intrusive work.
  • Isolating Authority (IA): Holds technical oversight for correct isolation design and boundary integrity. Must possess detailed facility knowledge.
  • Isolating Operator (ISO): Executes physical field isolations, valve line-ups, and lock installations. Note: The ISO cannot act as the Job Performer (JP) for the same activity.

2. Isolation Certificates & Test Permits

Certificate / Form Application Scope Key Protocol / Duration
Mechanical / Process / Instrument Physical fluid, process, or mechanical energy boundaries. Documents positive isolation, method, zero energy test, and independent verification.
Electrical Isolation High/Low voltage electrical distribution equipment. Requires switching execution, racking out, and continuous lock control.
Sanction to Test (STT) Temporary removal of isolation points for equipment testing. Valid for 1 shift only. Suspends active PTWs during test execution.
In eWMS, e-signatures authorize isolation modules. When executing under Crash Pack paper contingencies, wet-ink signatures are mandatory on all physical certificate sections before field work begins.

3. Baseline Isolation Standards

Isolation complexity is governed by fluid toxicity, design pressure, and line sizing to determine positive isolation requirements.

  • Category 1 (Toxic / Extreme Flammables): Requires Single Block & Bleed up to ANSI Class 150; Double Block & Bleed required for ANSI Class 300 and above.
  • Category 2 (Hydrocarbons / Fuel Gas / N2): Single Block & Bleed up to ANSI Class 150/600; Double Block & Bleed mandatory for ANSI Class 900+.
  • Positive Isolation: Involves physical disconnects such as line blinds, spectacle plates, spades, or spool removal.

4. Lockout / Tagout (LOTO) Key Management Flow

Key security uses a multi-padlock cascade system via a centralized Group Lock Box.

ISO installs isolation locks & tags at field points
↓
Isolation keys placed inside Key Cabinet / Group Lock Box
↓
Asset Owner Representative locks cabinet with Master Lock
↓
Job Performer (JP) attaches personal lock to Group Lock Box
↓
Zero Energy Verification & Work Commencement
The JP retains personal control over their padlock on the Group Lock Box throughout the job. Physical isolation keys cannot be extracted from the cabinet until all working parties remove their personal locks.

5. De-Isolation & Extended Period Isolation (EPI)

Upon job completion or extended plant shutdown, strict handback protocols apply:

  • Standard De-isolation: Permits are closed, field checks confirm equipment readiness, AA authorizes lock removal, and the ISO reinstates the process equipment.
  • Extended Period Isolation (EPI): Applied when equipment remains isolated beyond active permit validity without ongoing work. Long-term isolations are logged in the EPI Register and require formal AA approval to reinstate to "Live" status.
Never attempt to perform work under an isolation controlled solely by a Sanction to Test (STT) form. STT is restricted to live test conditions and requires explicit AA authorization.

Energy Isolation & LOTO Framework Summary

1. Baseline Standards & Risk Assessments

Temporary Defeat (TD) of HSE Critical Equipment & Systems (HSECES)

The Temporary Defeat Standard establishes a mandatory, highly regulated methodology for temporarily bypassing, isolating, bridging, or overriding safety-critical equipment. It ensures that whenever safety safeguards are bypassed, risks remain reduced to ALARP through alternative control measures.

1. Scope & System Definition

A Temporary Defeat applies specifically to HSECES—installations, systems, and equipment whose failure could cause or fail to mitigate a Major Accident.

  • Integrity Protection Systems: Pressure Safety Valves (PSVs) and overpressure trip loops.
  • Release Control Systems: Emergency Blowdown Valves (BDVs) and ESD loops.
  • Secondary Containment: Bund walls, dykes, and drainage pits.
  • Fire & Gas Suppression: Deluge systems, foam systems, and gas detection loops.
Defeats are strictly prohibited if alternate protection is unavailable or if the plant is in an upset condition. DCS/PLC shutdown process overrides, fire/gas overrides, and car seal removal require explicit Lockout/Tagout enforcement.

2. Key Roles & Responsibilities

Role Core Responsibilities
Approval Authority Approves the TDC based on the unmitigated risk severity and proposed duration (Operations Manager / VP / SVP).
Defeat Authority (DA) Develops the defeat plan using P&IDs, identifies exact bypass points, leads the Temporary Defeat Risk Assessment, and endorses the TDC.
Defeat Performer (DP) Physically executes or removes the defeat, logs status in the Defeat Register and shift handover logs, and ensures alternate mitigations are functional.

3. Risk-Based Approval Matrix

Approval levels escalate based on system criticality (SIL / Severity) and the intended defeat duration.

Magnitude / Severity < 12 Hours 12 to 72 Hours > 72 Hours
Plant or SIL > 2 Manager Operations / Equivalent SVP / VP Operations / Equivalent SVP / VP Operations / Equivalent
Process Unit or SIL 2 Manager Operations / Equivalent Manager Operations / Equivalent SVP / VP Operations / Equivalent
Process Section or SIL 1 Team Leader / Section Leader Team Leader / Section Leader VP Operations / Equivalent
Individual Equipment Area Authority (AA) Team Leader / Section Leader VP Operations / Equivalent
Any defeat exceeding 72 hours without permanent resolution elevates the facility status to a Downgraded Situation (DGS), triggering heightened operational monitoring.

4. Temporary Defeat Execution & Restoration Workflow

The lifecycle of a Temporary Defeat follows a structured 9-step governance process within WMS:

1. Work Request & Planning (Certificate Applicant initiates TDC)
↓
2. Identify & Approve Controls (DA evaluates alternate controls using Hierarchy of Controls)
↓
3. Authorize Defeat (Approval signed per Matrix duration & severity)
↓
4. Authorize PTW (AA attaches TDC to active PTW)
↓
5. Communicate Controls & Commence Work (DP logs in Defeat Register; JP verifies worksite)

↓

↓

When returning HSECES back to service, functional testing is mandatory based on safeguard type (mechanical, electrical, or instrument). Test results must be logged in the facility Defeat Register prior to final TDC cancellation.

Simultaneous Operations (SIMOPS) Framework

The SIMOPS Standard provides a structured governance framework to identify, assess, and manage interaction hazards when two or more operations, maintenance, or construction activities occur concurrently in the same area.

1. SIMOPS Classification & Governance Tools

Category Characteristics Assessment Tool
Routine SIMOPS Pre-identified recurring concurrent operations (e.g., hot work vs. draining hydrocarbons). Governed by the pre-approved Matrix of Permitted Operations (MOPO) and Restrictions Tables.
Non-Routine SIMOPS Unplanned or complex concurrent tasks not pre-mapped in MOPO (e.g., heavy lifts over live process lines). Requires a dedicated specialist workshop, Task-Based Risk Assessment (TBRA/JSA), and specific mitigations.
The MOPO Matrix uses color codes: Green (Allowed), Yellow (Restricted with Controls defined in Restrictions Tables), and Red (Prohibited).

2. Key Roles & Emergency Matrices

  • SIMOPS Coordinator (SC): Competent supervisor appointed by the Asset Owner to manage daily SIMOPS schedules, chair coordination meetings, and verify emergency preparedness. (The Area Authority acts as SC during normal operations).
  • Adverse Weather Matrix: Enforces activity limits based on wind speeds, sandstorms, night work, heavy rain/lightning, or extreme temperatures (>50°C).
  • HSECES / Critical Equipment Impairment Matrix: Defines operational controls when safety-critical systems (gas/fire detection, ESD, secondary containment) or DCS/utilities are impaired.

3. Lifecycle Execution Phases

1. Identification Phase (Identify interaction hazards & restricted combinations)
↓
2. Preparation Phase (Appoint SC, draft SIMOPS Management Plan & Emergency Plan)
↓
3. Setup Phase (Approve permits, test emergency comms, hold site safety meetings)
↓
4. Operational Execution (Daily SIMOPS meetings, shift handovers, Dynamic Risk Assessments)
↓
5. Post-Completion (Conduct After Action Review to capture lessons learned & update MOC)

Simultaneous Operations (SIMOPS) Framework Summary

The SIMOPS Module provides a structured framework to identify potential hazards and threats associated with concurrent operations, ensuring combined risks are managed to As Low As Reasonably Practicable (ALARP).

1. SIMOPS Categories & Core Conditions

SIMOPS Aspect Description & Categories
Operational Categories SIMOPS are divided into two main types: Routine SIMOPS and Non-routine SIMOPS. Combinations can fall into allowed, prohibited, or restricted categories.
Defining Conditions Includes activities located in the same area, performed at the same time, or that directly/indirectly affect the safe performance of another activity.
The SIMOPS Standard applies fully to contractors and integrates closely with other WMS standards rather than operating independently.

2. Governance, Tools & MOPO Matrix

  • MOPO Matrix & Restrictions: Manual of Permitted Operations (MOPO) matrices and restriction tables must be approved by the Asset Owner, kept readily available in control facilities, and updated upon modifications.
  • Decision Tree & Review: The SIMOPS Matrix Decision Tree assists in identifying SIMOPS during the PTW process. An After Action Review (AAR) is initiated by the Asset Owner Representative to capture lessons learned.
  • SIMOPS Coordinator (SC) Roles: For routine operations, the Area Authority (AA) acts as the SC. For non-routine operations, the SC endorses the permit and facilitates daily planning meetings, handovers, and safety communication.

3. Lifecycle & Execution Workflow

1. Identification & Scheduling (Decision Tree maps concurrent operations during PTW planning)
↓
2. MOPO & Restriction Verification (Checked against approved restriction tables and matrix rules)
↓
3. Coordination & Endorsement (SC or AA manages conflicts, communication lines, and permit endorsement)
↓
4. Review & Lessons Learned (After Action Review evaluates effectiveness post-activity)

🟧 3. PERMIT TO WORK — PTW

What is PTW?

A PTW is a set of documents used to maintain safe working conditions for a particular task.

  • ✅ Describes the work
  • ✅ Ensures correct authorization
  • ✅ Identifies hazards
  • ✅ Ensures controls are implemented
  • ✅ Acts as a written agreement
  • ✅ Becomes a record of work performed

📋 Types of PTW

🔴 Critical Work Permit

For defined critical/high-risk activities.

🔥 Hot Work Permit

For work involving hot work / ignition hazards.

🟢 General Work Permit

Work that is not critical work and does not contain hot work.

⏰ PTW Validity

7 CALENDAR DAYS Maximum PTW validity
EVERY SHIFT Required revalidation

👷 4. PTW ROLES & RESPONSIBILITIES

👑 AAA — Asset Approval Authority

Remember: AAA = Critical + Hot Work Approval

  • Approves Critical and Hot Work Permits.
  • Confirms controls reduce risk to ALARP.
  • AAA must be at least ONE level above AA.

🟨 AA — Area Authority

Remember: AA = Controls the Area

  • Authorizes permits.
  • Confirms controls are effective.
  • Manages SIMOPS.
  • Reviews and approves isolation requirements.
  • Manages revalidation.
  • Can suspend permits.
  • Closes permits.
  • Authorizes STT and de-isolation.

🟦 PI — Permit Issuer

Remember: PI = Clearance to Start Work

  • Checks the worksite.
  • Confirms required controls are in place.
  • Provides clearance before work starts.

🟩 PA — Performing Authority

Remember: PA = Requests / Manages the Job

  • Requests the permit.
  • Describes the work.
  • Ensures JSA requirements are addressed.
  • Supervises / coordinates Job Performers.
  • Requests permit closure.

🟪 JP — Job Performer

Remember: JP = Performs / Supervises the Actual Work

  • Accepts the permit.

ADNOC Work Management System (WMS) Master Summary

The WMS Master Module integrates core safety standards—including Permit to Work (PTW), Job Safety Analysis (JSA), Energy Isolation, Temporary Defeat of HSECES, and Simultaneous Operations (SIMOPS)—to govern high-risk operational and project activities.

1. Framework Governance & Audit Requirements

Governance Element Standard Requirement
Custodianship Senior Vice President, ADNOC Group HSE, acts as the framework custodian. WMS standards apply fully to contractors.
Retention Period All signed documents, permits, and risk records must be retained for a minimum of 12 months.
Audit Tier Structure Comprises Tier 1 (Daily PTW Monitoring), Tier 2 (Independent Assessment), and Tier 3 (ADNOC Group Company assessment).
Permits have a maximum validity of 7 consecutive calendar days, with a shift work time extension rule allowing up to 4 extra hours.

2. Key Operational Rules Across Modules

  • PTW & Worksite Display: The original PTW copy must be held by the Job Performer (JP) and visibly displayed at the worksite, preceded by a mandatory Tool Box Talk (TBT).
  • JSA & Risk Reviews: Formal Risk Assessments (FRAs) are reviewed every 1 year, while JSA Level 2 templates are reviewed every 2 years. Asset Owner Representative approval is required before JSA library uploads.
  • Energy Isolation Verification: Zero energy state is demonstrated via testing, checks, venting/draining, or equipment operation. The Area Authority (AA) determines independent verification requirements.
  • Temporary Defeat Thresholds: Defeats active for more than 72 hours trigger downgraded situation requirements.
  • SIMOPS Coordination: For routine operations, the Area Authority (AA) acts as the SIMOPS Coordinator (SC), utilizing approved MOPO and restriction matrices.

3. End-to-End WMS Lifecycle Workflow

1. Planning & IWAP (Work Categorization Chart determines JSA level, isolations, and SIMOPS)
↓
2. Authorization & Verification (AA verifies JSA controls, isolation integrity, and defeat certificates)
↓
3. Permit Issuance & Execution (PTW displayed on-site; TBT conducted; work executed safely)
↓
4. Completion & Review (Work completed, permits closed out, and lessons learned logged via AAR)
