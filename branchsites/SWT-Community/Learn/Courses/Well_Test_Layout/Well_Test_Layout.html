Well Test Layout

Video lesson: Well Test Layout

The Flow Path from Well to Flare

A surface well test is a chain. The fluid moves from the well through each item in order, and each item has one job.

  1. Wellhead and flowhead: the well connection and the first valves.
  2. Emergency shutdown valve (ESD): closes the flow fast in an emergency.
  3. Sand catcher (if needed): stops sand.
  4. Choke manifold: sets the flow rate and drops the pressure.
  5. Heat exchanger (if needed): heats the fluid to avoid hydrates or to help separation.
  6. Separator: splits oil, gas and water and measures them.
  7. Tanks and pumps: store and move the oil.
  8. Burners and flare: burn the oil and gas safely.
Learn the order. If you can name it from the well to the flare, you can follow any layout drawing.

Why the Layout Looks the Way It Does

The position of each item follows a few simple ideas about safety and practical work.

  • Keep ignition sources away from gas: the burners and flare are placed at a safe distance and downwind of the main equipment.
  • Wind direction: the layout takes the usual wind direction into account, so gas and heat move away from people.
  • Keep the high-pressure side short: the pipe from the well to the choke is kept short and well supported.
  • Room to work and escape: clear paths and at least two ways out of every area.
  • Control and offices upstream: people areas are placed away from the equipment, on the safe side.
The distances for a real job come from the company standard and the site risk assessment, not from this course.

Pressure Zones: High, Low and Atmospheric

A layout drawing often shows zones. Knowing the zone tells you how careful to be.

  • High-pressure side: from the well to the choke manifold. The pipe, fittings and valves have the highest rating.
  • Low-pressure side: after the choke. The pressure is lower, but it is still pressure.
  • Atmospheric side: tanks and vents. They are open to the air or close to it, and they must not be over-pressured.
  • Relief and safety devices sit between zones so that a lower rated part is never exposed to the higher pressure.
Look for the choke manifold on the drawing. It is the dividing point between the high and the low pressure side.

Reading a Layout Drawing

A layout or P&ID style drawing uses symbols for each item. A few habits make it easy to read.

  • Start at the well and follow the flow arrows to the end.
  • Find the valves and note which ones must be open for the line-up.
  • Find the safety devices: ESD, relief valves, pressure switches.
  • Find the sample and measurement points.
  • Compare the drawing with what you see on location. If they do not match, stop and ask.
A line-up check on location, with the drawing in hand, is one of the best safety habits. Follow your company procedure and site rules.
