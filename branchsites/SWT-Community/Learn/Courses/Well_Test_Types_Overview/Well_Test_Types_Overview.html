Well Test Types Overview

Video lesson: Well Test Types Overview

Why There Are Different Tests

Different goals need different tests.

  • Some tests show how much a well can produce.
  • Some tests show how the reservoir behaves.
  • Some tests collect fluid samples.
  • The best test depends on the objective in the program.
  • Time, cost and well condition also limit the choice.
Choose the test from the objective.

Flow After Flow Test

Flow at several rates, one after the other.

  • The well flows at one rate until the pressure settles.
  • Then the rate is changed and the well flows again.
  • No shut in is used between the rates.
  • It shows how rate and pressure relate.
  • It works well when the pressure settles quickly.
Several steady rates, one after the other.

Isochronal Test

Equal flow times with shut ins between.

  • The well flows at several rates, each for the same length of time.
  • The well is shut in between flows so pressure can recover.
  • It is useful when the pressure takes long to settle.
  • A longer final flow is often added at the end.
  • It needs more time and planning than a simple flow test.
Same time, different rates, shut ins between.

Drawdown and Buildup

Watch pressure fall, then rise.

  • A drawdown test opens the well and watches the pressure fall.
  • A buildup test shuts the well in and watches the pressure rise.
  • Buildup is common because the rate is zero and steady.
  • Both give information about the reservoir and near well damage.
  • Both need good pressure gauges and a clear history of rates.
Pressure falling or rising tells the story.

Choosing the Test

A few questions guide the choice.

  • What is the objective of the client program?
  • How long does the pressure take to settle?
  • Is there time and budget for a longer test?
  • An interference test uses a second well to watch the effect.
  • Follow the program and company procedures.
Objective first, then time and cost.
