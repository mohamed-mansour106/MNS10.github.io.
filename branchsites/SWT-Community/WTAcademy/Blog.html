Drawdown testing

A drawdown test opens a shut-in well at a constant rate and records bottomhole pressure as it falls. Plot pressure against log time: the straight line during radial flow gives permeability from the slope and skin from the intercept. Late-time data can show boundaries and, in a long test, reservoir limits. Keep the rate as steady as possible, because rate changes distort the interpretation.

Buildup testing

A buildup test shuts the well in after a flow period and records pressure recovery. It is the most common test because the rate is zero and easier to control. Analyse it with a Horner or superposition plot. Report the flow time before shut-in, since it affects the extrapolation.

Horner method

Plot shut-in pressure against log((tp + dt) / dt), where tp is the flow time before shut-in and dt is the shut-in time. The straight-line slope gives permeability-thickness. Extrapolating to a Horner time ratio of 1 gives p*, which equals the initial pressure only in an infinite-acting reservoir.

MDH method

The Miller-Dyes-Hutchinson method plots shut-in pressure against log dt. It is valid when the flow time before shut-in is long compared with the shut-in time, so it suits wells that were produced for a long time. It is simpler than Horner but less accurate for short flow periods.

Pressure derivative

The pressure derivative is the slope of pressure versus the natural log of time, usually plotted on log-log axes with the pressure change. It shows flow regimes clearly: wellbore storage as a unit slope, radial flow as a flat line, and boundaries as late-time rises or drops. Diagnose the flow regimes first, then choose the model.

Wellbore storage and skin

Wellbore storage hides the reservoir response at early time because fluid in the wellbore expands or compresses. Skin is an extra pressure drop near the well from damage or stimulation: positive skin means damage, negative means stimulation. Use the derivative to see when storage ends before reading skin from the semilog line.

Average reservoir pressure

p* from Horner extrapolation equals the initial pressure only in an infinite reservoir. In a bounded reservoir, use correction methods such as MBH charts or Dietz shape factors to convert p* into the average reservoir pressure.
